# Philippine farmgate prices fall 11.7%: procurement impact

## Introduction

Philippine agricultural producer prices **fell 11.7% year on year in April–June 2026**, according to the Philippine Statistics Authority's August 28 release. The decline narrowed from a revised 13.2% in the first quarter, but prices remained far below a year earlier.

In plain language, the Producer Price Index (PPI) measures prices received at the producer's first point of sale. It is not a supermarket or restaurant price. Food processors, traders, retailers and hospitality operators need to separate commodity movements from the costs added between farm and shelf.

Collection, grading, processing, cold storage, transport, packaging, waste and retail margins sit between farmgate and shelf. Imported inputs add exchange rates and tariffs. The PPI is a useful signal, but not a mechanical justification for cutting every quotation by 11.7%.

The Q2 decline was smaller than Q1's, but far larger than in the comparable quarters of 2024 and 2025. The index also fell 3.6% from the previous quarter, so the result is not only a year-earlier base effect.

Within crops, cereals rose 24.5% and root crops 31.7%. Leafy vegetables fell 55.7%, condiments 39.4% and commercial crops 22.6%. Livestock fell 16.5%, poultry 7.8%. Commercial fisheries rose 10.7%, while marine municipal fisheries fell 12.8%.

A negative national average does not mean a cereal buyer faced lower prices. Procurement budgets need the company's actual input, origin and grade.

## What the index can and cannot explain

The PSA release shows what moved, but does not prove every commodity-specific cause. Output, imports, weather, disease, seasonality, quality mix, collection channels and government measures can all matter.

- **Confirmed:** the aggregate, crops, livestock and poultry, and fisheries all declined year on year.
- **Confirmed:** cereals, root crops and commercial fisheries moved in the opposite direction.
- **Our analysis:** margin effects depend on the raw-material share of total delivered cost.
- **Not established by PPI alone:** a supplier's regional output, quality and contract drivers.

## Procurement actions for Japanese companies

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8"><h3 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Who should pay attention</h3><p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed">Food processors, restaurants, convenience retailers, hotels, feed makers, cold-chain operators and agricultural-input suppliers should track this data. Lower producer prices can create short-term renegotiation room, but prolonged farm-income pressure can also reduce future planting, quality investment and supplier continuity.</p></div>

1. Put the national PPI, local market quote and company contract price side by side.
2. Ask whether price changes reflect volume, quality mix or seasonality.
3. Separate processing, cold storage, logistics, packaging and FX from raw-material cost.
4. Include supplier continuity, minimum purchases and quality support in contracts.
5. Set quarterly review clauses by commodity instead of applying one headline average.
