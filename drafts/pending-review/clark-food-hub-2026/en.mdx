# Clark National Food Hub construction accelerates: an opening for cold chain investors

## Introduction

Ask most people what Clark is known for and they'll probably say aircraft MRO, semiconductors, or logistics. In August 2026, though, the Philippine Department of Agriculture (DA) announced it is fast-tracking construction of the "Clark National Food Hub" — a national-scale facility meant to combine wholesale agri-trading, cold storage, processing, and export handling in one place, with the explicit goal of connecting farmers directly to markets.

The Philippines has a chronic cold chain shortage, and a large share of harvested produce is lost somewhere between the farm and the market. This article lays out what's actually been announced, why Clark was chosen as the site, and what it could mean for Japanese companies in cold storage, logistics, and food processing.

## What the Clark National Food Hub actually is

The Clark National Food Hub is a national project led jointly by the Department of Agriculture (DA), state-run Food Terminal Inc. (FTI), and Clark International Airport Corporation (CIAC). DA and FTI signed an agreement covering funding, development, and operations, while FTI and CIAC signed a separate agreement covering land within the Clark Civil Aviation Complex, part of the broader Clark Aviation Capital.

The initial phase covers 40 hectares, with room to expand to 64 hectares. The national government has committed PHP 4 billion, and officials are targeting partial operations by the end of 2027.

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8"><h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">"Fast-tracking construction" is not the same as "opening"</h4><p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed">As of August 2026, what's confirmed is a policy push to accelerate construction, along with a target date. The end-2027 partial operations date is a goal, not a locked-in commitment. This particular food hub concept has already gone through revisions in scale and budget over the past year, so the timeline should be treated as something to keep tracking rather than a fixed fact.</p></div>

## Planned functions

According to local reporting, the food hub is planned to include:

| Function | Description |
|---|---|
| Cold storage | Low-temperature storage for perishables and seafood |
| Processing area | Primary processing, sorting, and packaging of produce |
| Food safety lab | Pre-shipment quality and safety inspection |
| Export handling | Processing area geared toward overseas shipments |
| Wholesale/retail space | Direct trading space linking farmers and buyers |

FTI's team reportedly visited food distribution facilities in Thailand, France, Spain, the Netherlands, and Japan while planning the project, aiming to apply international best practices. Officials expect around 2,500 jobs once the facility is fully operational.

## Why Clark was chosen

The site was picked for a combination of geographic advantages:

    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      Clark International Airport handles cargo, and the site sits close to FedEx and UPS operations within the aviation complex — a natural fit for perishable exports and imports.

  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">2. A road network linking North, Central, and South Luzon</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      Clark sits at a junction of the major roads connecting northern, central, and southern Luzon, giving it a wide catchment and distribution area covering Metro Manila, New Clark City in Tarlac, and the Cordillera Administrative Region.
    </p>

## The Philippines' cold chain and food logistics problem

This project sits on top of a real structural gap. As of January 2024, the DA had stated that up to 30% of harvested crops were being lost due to poor logistics before reaching market. Separately, research from the Cold Chain Innovation Hub estimates the Philippines has roughly 860,000 cold-storage pallet positions against an estimated need of about 1.4 million — a gap of roughly 540,000 pallet positions. Existing cold storage capacity is also concentrated around consumption centers like Metro Manila, leaving production areas underserved.

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8"><h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">What companies evaluating the market should keep in mind</h4><p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed">The core issue in Philippine food logistics is that cold chain capacity is missing where the food is actually grown, not just where it's consumed. The Clark National Food Hub directly addresses that gap as a national project — and that also widens the market for private companies offering similar infrastructure nearby.</p></div>

## Where the business opportunity is for Japanese companies

Several categories look like a reasonable fit:

1. Cold storage / refrigerated warehouse builders and operators
2. Trucking and 3PL companies specializing in cold chain logistics
3. Primary processing and packaging equipment suppliers for produce and seafood
4. Food safety and quality inspection equipment and service providers
5. IT companies offering traceability and inventory management systems for agricultural products

That said, the Clark National Food Hub itself is still at the "accelerated construction" announcement stage — details on bidding and tenant recruitment had not been finalized as of August 2026. Companies evaluating this space should look beyond direct participation in the hub itself and also consider building independent cold chain facilities near the hub or elsewhere in the Clark Freeport Zone, while checking the latest bidding information with the DA, CIAC, and PEZA/CDC.
