# Philippines EO 122 pivots critical minerals to processing

## Introduction

Can the Philippines move from exporting nickel and other ores to refining them and producing batteries, electronics and clean-energy components at home?

In August 2026, the government issued Executive Order No. 122 (EO 122), a presidential direction establishing a national framework for critical minerals. These are inputs essential to batteries, semiconductors, renewable energy and communications whose supply disruption carries large economic consequences.

The direction is clear, but project economics are not automatic. The government statements reviewed do not announce an ore-export ban. The final mineral list, production targets, deadlines, incentive mechanics and standard approval timelines will need to be defined through the comprehensive strategy and implementing rules.

## What changes under EO 122

Mining law, environmental review, investment incentives and individual processing projects already existed. EO 122's significance is to organise them around a national critical-minerals value chain extending from extraction to higher-value products.

The Department of Trade and Industry-Board of Investments (DTI-BOI) leads industrial and investment strategy. The Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) oversees resources, mineral rights and environmental administration. The Department of Finance describes refining, advanced materials, batteries, electronic components and renewable-energy technologies as target activities.

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8"><h3 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">National priority does not remove permits</h3><p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed">Mining rights, Environmental Compliance Certificates, water and power, land, local-government requirements and—where applicable—indigenous-peoples processes remain separate diligence tracks. Tax incentives also require registration and eligibility.</p></div>

## Read value and physical supply separately

The Philippine Statistics Authority valued Class A reserves of gold, copper, nickel and chromite at PHP588.12 billion in 2025, up 17.6% from PHP500.20 billion in 2024. Class A reserves are commercially recoverable resources supported by a defined development project.

A higher monetary value does not necessarily mean more ore. Gold drove much of the 2025 increase, while physical Class A reserves of gold, copper and nickel declined. A processor therefore needs mine-level ore grade, committed volumes, haulage distance and pricing—not only national resource headlines.

## How the Philippine approach differs from Indonesia

Indonesia has prohibited all nickel-ore exports since 2020 and requires domestic processing. A WTO panel found in 2022 that the export ban and domestic-processing requirement were inconsistent with GATT Article XI; Indonesia appealed.

The Philippine statements reviewed focus on investment promotion, administrative coordination, responsible mining and processing capacity rather than a ban.

Philippine and Indonesian industry groups also launched cooperation in 2026 linking Philippine ore supply with Indonesian processing strength. The two countries may be partners in an ASEAN value chain as well as competitors for capital.

## Eight diligence items for Japanese companies

1. Define the product stage: ore, concentrate, sulphate, precursor, battery material or component.
2. Secure ore grade, volume, pricing formula, mine life and alternative supply.
3. Model electricity price, reliability and renewable-energy content.
4. Include water, discharge, tailings, slag, chemicals and closure liabilities.
5. Map responsibilities across DENR-MGB, EMB, BOI, local governments and, if applicable, NCIP.
6. Confirm qualification under the 2026 SIPP and CREATE MORE rules.
7. Obtain long-term offtake from battery, electronics or clean-energy customers.
8. Make human-rights, indigenous-peoples, carbon and origin data auditable.
