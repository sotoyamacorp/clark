# Philippines posts ₱106.3B July deficit as spending rises 19.8%

## Introduction

The Philippines recorded a **₱106.3 billion fiscal deficit in July 2026**, up from ₱18.9 billion a year earlier, according to the Bureau of the Treasury.

The composition matters. Revenue grew 2.1% to ₱482.3 billion while expenditure increased 19.8% to ₱588.6 billion. Social protection, military modernization and direct payments for foreign-assisted railway projects contributed to the spending rise. The January–July deficit, however, was 53.8% of the full-year plan, below a simple calendar-year pace.

The percentage increase looks extreme because the comparison base was unusually small. Actual pesos, the accumulated balance and the revenue-spending composition provide a more useful view.

## What drove the 19.8% spending rise

The Treasury cited social-protection programs including Unlad Pamilyang Pilipino, armed-forces modernization and direct payments for foreign-assisted railway projects. Payment timing for programs and investment projects—not only recurring operating costs—lifted the monthly figure.

Tax revenue grew 7.0% in July, while non-tax revenue fell 39.9%, mainly reflecting the timing of Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas dividends received in the prior year. This is not evidence that tax collection weakened by the same amount.

## Progress against the annual plan

The 2026 government plan is ₱4.807 trillion of revenue, ₱6.466 trillion of disbursements and a ₱1.659 trillion deficit. January–July represented 59.7%, 58.2% and 53.8%, respectively.

The July result alone does not imply a full-year overrun. Still, public-works disbursement often concentrates later in the year, and disaster response or new policies can add spending.

## Interest is taking a larger share

Interest payments rose 19.2% to ₱620.9 billion in January–July. By our calculation, interest absorbed **21.6% of revenue**, up from 19.1% a year earlier.

That reduces the amount available for infrastructure, education and healthcare from each peso of revenue. The government plans to narrow the deficit to 3.5% of GDP by 2030, balancing growth investment with consolidation.

## What it means for businesses

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8"><h3 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Who should pay attention</h3><p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed">Rail, construction, defense and social-protection suppliers may benefit from faster execution. Banks, PPP investors and companies raising long-term fixed-rate capital should monitor how government borrowing and interest costs affect yields and private financing conditions.</p></div>

Track three transmission channels separately:

1. **Demand:** program disbursements, tenders and payment timing.
2. **Tax and customs:** collection growth and implementation practices.
3. **Financing:** government issuance, interest rates and private credit.

A large monthly deficit is not by itself a go-or-no-go investment signal. Budget execution, project pipelines and interest rates belong on the same dashboard.
