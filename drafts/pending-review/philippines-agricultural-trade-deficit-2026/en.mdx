# Philippine Agricultural Trade Deficit Hits $1.06bn: Import Risk and Opportunity

## Introduction

Philippine agricultural trade totaled $2.47 billion in June 2026. Imports of $1.77bn accounted for 71.4% of the total and far exceeded exports of $706.92m. The agricultural trade deficit widened 30.3% year on year to $1.06bn.

That deficit is a food-security challenge. It also maps demand for importing, cold-chain logistics, processing, testing and inventory management.

Agricultural imports represented 12.9% of total Philippine imports, while agricultural exports were 8.1% of national exports. The deficit widened because imports rose as exports fell.

## Cereals Lead the Import Basket

Cereals were the largest import group at $441.93m or 25.0% of agricultural imports. Of $708.67m imported from ASEAN, Vietnam supplied $221.72m or 31.3%. Cereals were also the largest ASEAN product group at $214.28m.

From the EU, dairy, eggs and honey totaled $35.54m, while meat and edible offal totaled $27.82m. The supply chain extends beyond rice to dairy, meat, fats and processed foods.

## Record Local Output and Continued Imports Can Coexist

The Department of Agriculture (DA) reported Q2 2026 palay production up 5.7% year on year to a record 4.63m tons, the highest comparable second-quarter figure since 1987. Even so, DA plans to keep rice imports open in 2026.

The reason is a possible 750,000-ton output loss from strong El Niño conditions expected late in 2026. Rice arrivals had reached roughly 3.3m-3.5m tons by early August. Policy is balancing a strong past harvest against inventory and weather risk through the 2027 dry-season crop.

## Opportunity Extends Beyond Selling Imports

An import-reliant market creates adjacent demand for:

1. Temperature-controlled transport from ports and airports
2. Cold storage and inventory rotation
3. Residue, microbiological and quality testing
4. Demand forecasting, lot tracking and shelf-life management
5. Local processing and packaging

Consolidation sites such as the planned Clark National Food Hub could bring these functions together. Regulation remains a moving variable: phytosanitary rules, animal-disease controls and product safeguards change by commodity. In August 2026, for example, DA temporarily banned selected FMD-susceptible livestock and products from seven economies.

## Five Checks Before Market Entry

1. Product-specific permits, quarantine, tariff and safeguards
2. Supplier diversification beyond one country
3. Landed cost including peso, freight and fuel
4. Safety stock for port delays and temperature excursions
5. Lot and counterparty traceability for recalls
