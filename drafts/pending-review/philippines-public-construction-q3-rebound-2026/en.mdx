# Philippine public construction fell 32.4%: what Q3 brings

## Introduction

The Philippine government wants public construction to recover from the third quarter of 2026. It would be a mistake, however, to assume that a larger budget means contracts will immediately flow.

According to the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), general-government construction fell 32.4% year on year in Q2 2026. Department of Budget and Management (DBM) cash data separately show first-half infrastructure disbursements of PHP518.1 billion, down 28.1%.

In practical terms, **projects are encountering friction across procurement, mobilization, acceptance and payment—not simply a lack of budget**. DBM intends to accelerate implementation without relaxing audit and validation.

## What the numbers show

The PSA national accounts and DBM cash-disbursement data measure different things. The first captures construction as economic activity; the second records government payments. They cannot be added or compared as identical series, but they point in the same direction.

## Down sharply year on year, but close to plan

First-half infrastructure disbursements fell from PHP720.3 billion in 2025 to PHP518.1 billion in 2026.

The result was only PHP13.5 billion, or 2.5%, below the PHP531.6 billion first-half program. A high 2025 base and payment timing matter, so the year-on-year decline does not mean that an equivalent amount of 2026 budget disappeared.

Still, infrastructure and other capital outlays alone fell 40.8% in the first half and 34.1% in June. Tighter validation of road-work payments weighed on releases, while rail, defense and school projects provided support.

## Why implementation slowed

DBM documents indicate four connected causes:

1. DPWH strengthened validation of claims, physical accomplishment and supporting documents.
2. Procurement, implementation and payment delays carried over from the second half of 2025.
3. Accounts payable, right-of-way and mobilization items remained unresolved.
4. Disbursement timing shifted for foreign-assisted rail and other non-road projects.

Actual infrastructure disbursements in 2025 were PHP1.3241 trillion against a PHP1.5128 trillion program, a PHP188.7 billion shortfall. The bottleneck therefore predates Q2 2026.

## The Q3 plan: faster execution, unchanged safeguards

DBM says the necessary allotments are available for DPWH and expects implementation to accelerate in August and September. It will continue checking project need, implementation readiness, prior utilization, outcomes, regional-development approval, procurement schedules and milestones.

## The 2027 budget is larger—but mind the definitions

The proposed 2027 infrastructure budget is PHP1.467 trillion, 4.4% of GDP and PHP178 billion or 13.8% above the 2026 level. The proposed DPWH budget is PHP643.95 billion, up 21.29% from the PHP530.90 billion 2026 General Appropriations Act level.

DBM's 2026 Budget of Expenditures and Sources of Financing separately reports an “Infrastructure Program (Disbursements)” series: PHP1.5452 trillion actual in 2024, PHP1.5128 trillion programmed in 2025 and PHP1.5583 trillion projected in 2026. Its coverage and timing differ from the PHP1.467 trillion 2027 budget allocation, so the two series should not be placed in a simple growth table.

## Implications for Japanese companies

For construction, equipment, materials, surveying, design, project management and audit-technology companies, Q3 can create renewed opportunities. Yet a recovery phase makes acceptance and billing quality more—not less—important.

Do not review tender notices alone. Track the budget year, allotment, Notice of Cash Allocation, right-of-way, design completeness, contracting package, payment terms, audit trail and price-adjustment provisions separately.
