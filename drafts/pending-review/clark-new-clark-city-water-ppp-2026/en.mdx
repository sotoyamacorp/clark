# New Clark City water PPP: what the 25-year plan means

## Introduction

For a factory or data center, road access and tax incentives are not enough. The investment case can turn on whether the required volume of water will reach the site, on time and under bankable service conditions.

On August 26, 2026, Maynilad Water Services announced that its board had approved an investment in a proposed New Clark City water and wastewater project. The contemplated 25-year public-private partnership involves the Bases Conversion and Development Authority (BCDA), which develops New Clark City, and Korea Water Resources Corporation (K-Water).

In plain terms, the proposal seeks to build a long-term system covering abstraction, treatment, distribution, wastewater collection and treatment. The critical caveat is that **a board investment approval is not the same as a project award or signed concession**. The current disclosure does not confirm the project cost, tariff, capacity, construction date or final allocation of responsibilities.

An unsolicited proposal is a project concept submitted by the private sector before the government launches a fully specified solicitation. Even if a proponent later receives original proponent status, government approvals, competitive procedures and contract negotiations usually remain. Maynilad said it would disclose further material developments when they occur.

## Do not merge the old plan with the new proposal

An earlier BCDA procurement document described a 30-year arrangement, potentially extendable, with an estimated PHP10 billion project cost. It projected 200,000 m3/day of eventual water demand and 150,000 m3/day of wastewater treatment capacity.

The new Maynilad filing describes 25 years. Because the terms differ, the old cost and capacity numbers should be treated as planning benchmarks—not as the commercial terms of the current proposal.

## Demand must be built in stages

BCDA's 2019 accomplishments listed an additional well with 2,000 m3/day capacity and 3,000 m3 of storage among early facilities for the SEA Games area. The older citywide plan, by contrast, projected eventual demand of 200,000 m3/day.

## Why New Clark City is different from Metro Manila

Maynilad's core network serves a dense, mature concession area in western Metro Manila. New Clark City is a greenfield city where demand arrives district by district.

## Eight checks for investors

1. Separate original proponent status, competition, contract signing and financial close.
2. Get a service date for the exact plot, not only a citywide master plan.
3. Specify peak-hour volume and pressure, not just average daily demand.
4. Match water quality and pretreatment to food, semiconductor, cooling or potable use.
5. Confirm wastewater limits for volume, temperature, pH, COD/BOD and pretreatment.
6. Separate fixed tariff, volumetric tariff, minimum take, connection works and escalation.
7. Map NWRB water rights, environmental permits and the utility-plant interface.
8. Design backup storage, wells, tanker supply, recycled water and shutdown procedures.
