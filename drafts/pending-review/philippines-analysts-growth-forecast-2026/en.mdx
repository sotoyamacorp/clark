# Private Economists Slash 2026 Growth Forecasts to 3-3.9%, Below Government Target

## Introduction

Following [the weak Q2 GDP print we covered two days ago (2.3%)](/en/articles/philippines-q2-gdp-slowdown-2026/), private-sector economists have now weighed in. Major research houses including Nomura, ANZ, and Capital Economics have all cut their full-year 2026 growth forecasts for the Philippines, and every one of them now sits below the government's target range of 3.5-4.5%. This article covers what each institution is forecasting, and how the government has responded.

## Nomura Cut Sharply From 4.6% to 3.8%; ANZ Held at 3.9%

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Both clear the 3.5% government floor, but fall short of the 4.4% needed</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    Nomura Global Markets Research sharply cut its 2026 Philippine GDP growth forecast to 3.8% from 4.6%. ANZ Research held its forecast steady at 3.9%. Both estimates clear the floor of the government's full-year target range (3.5-4.5%), but fall short of [the 4.4% average H2 growth rate DEPDev Secretary Balisacan said is needed to hit the low end of that range](/en/articles/philippines-q2-gdp-slowdown-2026/). With Q2's actual 2.3% print coming in below market expectations, concerns about persistently weak investment and household consumption have broadened.
  </p>
</div>

---

## The Most Cautious Call, From Capital Economics, Is Around 3% — a Post-Pandemic Low Pace

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">The same firm expects a rebound to 4.5% in 2027</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    Among major institutions, Capital Economics has the most cautious outlook. Gareth Leather, the firm's senior Asia economist, is forecasting 2026 growth of around 3% — which, if realized, would be the slowest pace since the pandemic. Notably, though, the firm also projects a rebound to 4.5% in 2027, framing 2026 as more of a temporary dip than a structural slowdown. The two headwinds cited consistently across major institutions are elevated energy prices tied to Middle East tensions, and the flood-control infrastructure corruption scandal.
  </p>
</div>

## Four Days of Headlines: Target-Setting → World Bank Caution → the Q2 Miss → Private Downgrades

<div class="border-l-4 border-navy-600 bg-navy-50 p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Government, international institutions, and private economists have now all weighed in</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    Laid out chronologically, the last few days of coverage make the gap in perspective among stakeholders unusually clear. [August 6: the government expressed confidence in hitting its target via 3.7% H2 growth, while the World Bank took a more cautious stance](/en/articles/philippines-h2-growth-target-2026/). [August 7: Q2 GDP came in below market expectations at 2.3%](/en/articles/philippines-q2-gdp-slowdown-2026/). And now, August 10: Nomura, ANZ, Capital Economics, and other private economists have broadly cut their forecasts. Government, international institutions (the World Bank), and private economists — all three now largely agree that hitting the government's target won't come easily.
  </p>
</div>

---

## DEPDev Rejects "Stagflation" Talk, Calls This the Early Stage of a Recovery

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Points to resuming government spending and improving private-sector sentiment</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    In response to the wave of downgrades, DEPDev (Department of Economy, Planning and Development) Secretary Balisacan explicitly pushed back on concerns that the Philippines risks stagflation (the coexistence of weak growth and high inflation). He said the economy is "already entering the early stages of recovery," pointing to resuming large-scale government agency spending and signs of improving private-sector business confidence. That said, most private economists attach a caveat: whether the H2 recovery scenario plays out hinges on the pace of infrastructure spending execution — leaving a real gap between the government's optimism and the private sector's more cautious stance.
  </p>
</div>

## Why This Matters for Companies

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Economists broadly agree the pace of infrastructure spending is the key swing factor for H2</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    Several banks and research houses converge on the same three factors determining whether an H2 recovery materializes: the pace of infrastructure spending execution, how quickly inflation eases, and whether investor sentiment improves. There's a real gap between the government's target (3.5-4.5%) and private forecasts (3-3.9%), so companies weighing expansion or growth in the Philippines should reference both the official outlook and private-sector forecasts, and factor in a more conservative scenario. Businesses exposed to public infrastructure or construction-related demand in particular would benefit from tracking the actual pace of H2 budget execution on an ongoing basis.
  </p>
</div>
