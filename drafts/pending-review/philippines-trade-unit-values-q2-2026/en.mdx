# Philippine export unit values rise 0.5%, imports 0.3%

## Introduction

Philippine **export unit values rose 0.5% year on year in April–June 2026, while import unit values increased 0.3%**, according to an August 28 release from the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA). Export growth slowed from 1.09% in the first quarter and import growth from 1.5%.

In plain language, the Unit Value Index (UVI) tracks average value per unit—trade value divided by quantity. It is not a pure price index for an identical product. A change in the mix of high- and low-value goods can move the index, so businesses need volume, FX and product data as well.

In 2026, PSA moved the base year from 2018 to 2023 and adopted the 2022 Philippine Standard Commodity Classification. The change is intended to reflect the newer structure of trade. PSA also released a 2018–2022 backcast so analysts can use a consistent 2023-based series.

Machinery and electrical equipment includes major Philippine trade products such as electronics and components. It is a broad classification, however. A 1.5% section result cannot be copied into a company budget without drilling down to the relevant HS code and product mix.

## Why faster trade value is not a contradiction

PSA's monthly data show total merchandise trade reached USD22.27 billion in July 2026, up 16.3% year on year. Q2 unit-value growth was only 0.5% for exports and 0.3% for imports.

The periods do not fully overlap, but the concepts are compatible. Trade value can rise because volume increases, the product mix shifts, high-value goods take a larger share, or exchange rates move. Align the reference periods before placing monthly trade value next to quarterly UVI.

## Practical checks for Japanese firms

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8"><h3 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Who should pay attention</h3><p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed">Electronics, machinery, metals, auto-parts, trading and logistics companies should track these figures. When export and import unit values both rise slowly, selling prices must be read alongside imported-input cost, PHP/USD/JPY exposure, freight and inventory valuation.</p></div>

1. Map the company's HS code to the PSA product section.
2. Compare UVI, volume and trade value for the same period.
3. Split FX exposure by contract currency.
4. Separate FOB/CIF, freight, insurance and duties.
5. Check whether model or quality mix moved the average unit value.
6. Use the 2023-base backcast rather than mixing old and new bases.
