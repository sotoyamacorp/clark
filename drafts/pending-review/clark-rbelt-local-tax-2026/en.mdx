# Clark Local Tax Clarified: A Practical Guide to the 2% RBELT Cap

## Introduction

Clark tax explainers often mix two messages: a 5% tax in lieu of national and local taxes, and a separate local tax. Both can be true because the answer depends on the incentive regime and registration history. Joint Memorandum Circular No. 01-2026, effective March 30, 2026, gives investors a clearer decision framework.

## What RBELT Means

The Registered Business Enterprise Local Tax is a local tax introduced under the CREATE MORE Act. During an Income Tax Holiday or Enhanced Deductions Regime, the relevant LGU may impose a rate of no more than 2% on gross income from the registered project or activity.

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8"><h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">RBELT replaces covered local taxes once imposed</h4><p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed">JMC 01-2026 treats RBELT as being in lieu of local taxes, fees, and charges, including local business and real property taxes. Once an LGU adopts RBELT by ordinance, it cannot stack separate covered local levies on the same registered activity.</p></div>

## The Answer Changes Under ITH, EDR, and SCIT

| Incentive regime | Basic national-tax treatment | RBELT |
|---|---|---|
| ITH | Income-tax exemption for the registered activity | Up to 2%, subject to LGU ordinance |
| EDR | 20% income-tax rate plus enhanced deductions | Up to 2%, subject to LGU ordinance |
| SCIT | 5% of gross income in lieu of covered national and local taxes | Not imposed |

SCIT is generally an option for qualifying export enterprises. Domestic-market enterprises and firms choosing EDR may face a different local-tax profile. A Clark registration alone does not answer the question; the approved project and elected regime do.

## Three Checks for a Clark Investment

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8"><h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Set the tax classification before finalizing the site</h4><p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed">CDC handles investment registration in Clark, while relevant LGUs remain part of local-tax administration. Define the registered activity, incentive regime, and property-use structure, then ask CDC and tax advisers to assess the same fact pattern.</p></div>

1. Confirm the activities covered by the Certificate of Registration and Tax Incentives
2. Determine when ITH, EDR, or SCIT applies to each project
3. Check the relevant LGU's RBELT ordinance, rate, filing, and payment procedure

JMC 01-2026 also addresses beneficial use: where an investment promotion agency owns land but an RBE uses it, the RBE may be treated as the taxable person. Leasing does not automatically remove real-property-tax questions.
