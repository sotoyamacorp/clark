# Philippines expands a PHP300m egg-processing hub

## Introduction

When egg production exceeds fresh demand, farmers face falling prices and waste. The Philippine government's answer in Batangas is to convert surplus eggs into pasteurised liquid and long-life powder.

President Ferdinand Marcos Jr visited the PHP300-million expansion of the Batangas Egg Producers Multipurpose Cooperative (BEPCO) on August 27, 2026.

Its main pasteurisation line handles 1,400 kilograms, equivalent to 28,000 50-gram eggs, per hour. A PHP50-million spray dryer is intended to turn liquid egg into powder with a shelf life of up to two years.

In plain terms, the investment converts a perishable commodity into a standardised ingredient for hotels, airlines, bakeries, feeding programmes and disaster stocks.

BEPCO already supplies pasteurised liquid egg to hotels, airlines, bakeries and other food customers. Powder remained under development. Installed equipment and stable commercial production to an approved specification are different milestones.

## Why surpluses emerge

San Jose municipality produces seven to ten million eggs a day, depending on the season. Layers continue producing when consumer demand softens, so supply cannot be stopped immediately.

PSA recorded Batangas chicken-egg output at 217,581 tonnes in 2025, 25.7% of the national total. Batangas, Cebu, Bukidnon, Tarlac and Pampanga together contributed half of national output.

## Production rose for three consecutive fourth quarters

Philippine Q4 chicken-egg output rose from 188,639 tonnes in 2023 to 207,202 tonnes in 2024 and 225,730 tonnes in 2025. The latest annual increase was 8.9%.

DA says the process includes microbiological testing, including Salmonella checks, and labelling controls. Buyers and technical partners should verify FDA status, HACCP or equivalent systems, allergens, residues, temperature records and recall procedures.

## Reading the 28,000-eggs-per-hour capacity

Against local output of seven to ten million eggs a day, the line's hourly capacity is 28,000 eggs. An illustrative eight-hour shift equals 224,000 eggs, or about 2.2-3.2% of local daily production.

This is not a forecast. Actual throughput depends on operating days, cleaning, product changeovers, yield, maintenance, intake and orders. Nameplate capacity alone cannot prove that the facility can absorb the region's surplus.

## Lessons for Clark's National Food Hub

CIAC said in 2025 that 47 hectares had been designated for the Clark National Food Hub, with an initial 12 hectares to be fast-tracked. The concept combines storage, processing and distribution.

Batangas serves Southern Luzon while Clark serves Central Luzon. Tarlac and Pampanga produced a combined 10.1% of Philippine chicken eggs in 2025, giving the Clark area a relevant raw-material base.

No BEPCO-equivalent plant in Clark has been confirmed. The transferable lesson is to design producer organisation, buyers, specifications and utilisation before buying equipment.

## Eight diligence checks for Japanese companies

1. Measure raw-material surpluses by week and season.
2. Secure buyer specifications with hotels, airlines, bakers and factories.
3. Separate whole egg, whites, yolks and powder markets and yields.
4. Verify FDA, HACCP, microbial, allergen and labelling compliance.
5. Model utilisation after cleaning, maintenance and waste.
6. Design cold-chain liquid logistics and moisture-controlled powder storage separately.
7. Find compliant uses for shells, manure and off-specification product.
8. Link raw-material and industrial selling prices in contracts.
