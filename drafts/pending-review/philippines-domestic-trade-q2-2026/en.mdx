# Philippine domestic trade value falls 21.9% as sea trade halves

## Introduction

Philippine interregional freight sent a split signal in the second quarter of 2026: total volume and value fell sharply year on year, road-trade value rose, and sea trade roughly halved.

The Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) recorded 10.57 million tonnes, down 37.9%, and PHP745.70 billion in value, down 21.9%. These are commodity flows crossing regional or provincial boundaries, not a measure of the whole economy.

## What this statistic covers

Domestic trade in this dataset means commodities moved from one Philippine region or province to another by road, water or air. It is best understood as reported interregional freight.

It does not equal consumer retail sales, same-region last-mile delivery, services, inventory changes or total GDP. A 21.9% decline in this series therefore does not mean that Philippine GDP fell by the same rate.

The year-on-year decline persisted from Q1 into Q2. Quarter to quarter, however, volume edged up from 9.85 million tonnes in Q4 2025 to 10.17 million in Q1 2026 and 10.57 million in Q2. A weak annual comparison and a modest sequential recovery can both be true.

Road accounted for 72.8% of value, water 27.1%, and air about 0.1%. Sea freight was the main drag. Road moved less tonnage but more value, which may reflect prices or a change in product mix, but the published aggregate does not prove the cause.

## Products and regions

Machinery and electrical equipment led outflow value at PHP183.41 billion, or 24.6%. Prepared food, beverages and tobacco followed at PHP147.64 billion, or 19.8%, then optical, measuring and medical instruments at PHP93.11 billion, or 12.5%.

CALABARZON accounted for PHP331.63 billion, or 44.5%, in outflows. Central Luzon recorded PHP108.41 billion, or 14.5%. Their domestic trade surpluses were PHP249.50 billion and PHP71.94 billion, respectively.

## What it means for Clark

Central Luzon's surplus is consistent with its role as a production and distribution base, but it is not a Clark-only result. Clark manufacturers and logistics operators benefit from road access toward NCR and CALABARZON, while products requiring inter-island distribution still depend on reliable port and sea connections.

## Five checks for Japanese companies

1. Track volume, delay and cost by exact origin-destination lane and carrier.
2. Compare road and sea on total landed cost, including port charges, inventory days, damage and stockouts.
3. Identify single-island and single-port supplier concentration and set alternatives.
4. Separate price and mix effects when volume falls but shipment value rises.
5. Reconcile preliminary PSA data with company shipment, customs and inventory records before assigning a cause.
