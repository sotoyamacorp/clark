# Philippine Government Debt Reaches PHP19.07tn: Three Metrics for Investors

## Introduction

Philippine national government debt reached PHP19.07 trillion at end-June 2026, up PHP518.98 billion or 2.8% from May. The headline is large, but investors need to separate the size of the economy, domestic and foreign exposure, maturity and interest cost before drawing conclusions.

The balance almost doubled between end-2020 and June 2026. Nominal GDP also grew, however, so balance growth alone is not a measure of repayment capacity. Debt-to-GDP is the first metric because it scales debt against the economy that generates the tax base.

## Metric 1: 67.3% Is Domestic

Domestic debt was PHP12.84tn and external debt PHP6.23tn. By currency, 66.7% was local currency and 33.3% foreign currency.

A large domestic share limits the direct rise in total debt caused by peso depreciation. Yet heavy government borrowing at home can attract bank and investor funds into government securities and affect private credit pricing or availability—the potential crowding-out channel.

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">

    External debt still creates FX sensitivity
  </h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed">
    Of external government debt, 77.8% is in US dollars, 11.2% in yen and 9.5%
    in euros. Peso depreciation raises the peso value and servicing cost.
    Importers should model a period in which their own FX cost and the
    government's foreign-currency burden rise together.
  </p>

## Metric 2: 84.1% Is Long-Term

By original maturity, short-term debt was 6.1%, medium-term 9.7% and long-term 84.1%. External debt alone was 98.2% long-term. That reduces dependence on immediate refinancing.

Long maturity does not eliminate rate risk: future refinancing reflects then-prevailing yields. Government bond yields can flow into bank loans and corporate bond benchmarks, so capital budgeting should monitor them alongside the policy rate.

## Metric 3: First-Half Interest Was PHP483.69bn

Debt service in January-June 2026 was PHP1.23tn: PHP743.00bn of principal and PHP483.69bn of interest. Interest consumed 20.3% of first-half government revenue of PHP2.39tn.

A rising interest share leaves less room for infrastructure, education and health. For business planning, interest-to-revenue may say more about future fiscal room than a record nominal balance alone.

## Crisis or Manageable?

The IMF's 2025 Article IV assessment placed the Philippines at low risk of sovereign stress and supported gradual medium-term consolidation. Separately, BSP put total Philippine external debt—including public and private borrowers—at $147.35bn or 30.0% of GDP at end-March 2026. It is a different series from national government debt.

PHP19.07tn therefore does not prove an immediate crisis. But a 63.2% debt-to-GDP ratio and a higher interest burden reduce room if growth remains weak or funding costs rise.

## Five Items for a Quarterly Dashboard

1. Debt-to-GDP and monthly drivers
2. Domestic/external, currency and maturity shares
3. Interest-to-revenue and fiscal deficit
4. Government yields, bank lending rates and the peso
5. Changes in infrastructure budgets, taxes and regulated charges
