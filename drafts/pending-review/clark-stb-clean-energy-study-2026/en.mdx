# New Clark City opens 12-month battery and e-mobility study

## Introduction

Is New Clark City's smart-city vision moving from concept to deployment?

The Bases Conversion and Development Authority (BCDA), the government developer of New Clark City, and Australian-backed StB GigaFactory Inc. have agreed to spend 12 months studying renewable energy, Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS) and electric mobility across BCDA-managed properties.

The conclusion comes first: no deployment has been approved. The parties are evaluating sites, technical feasibility and commercial viability. What makes the study notable is that StB already manufactures batteries in New Clark City, creating a potential link between local industrial production and local infrastructure demand.

## What was agreed, and what remains open

BCDA will provide access to candidate locations, project information and administrative support. StB will perform site surveys, technical studies and financial modelling. The scope covers New Clark City and other BCDA properties.

## The industrial anchor: StB's battery plant

StB GigaFactory is the first locator at Filinvest Innovation Park in New Clark City. It makes lithium iron phosphate (LFP) batteries and storage products for residential, commercial, industrial, utility and electric-vehicle applications. In plain terms, the study partner is already a local manufacturer, not a company considering a future factory.

BOI equated the initial 300 MWh line to roughly 6,000 EV battery packs or 60,000 home systems. The 2 GWh target corresponds to about 18,000 EV packs or 400,000 home systems. The cited 2,500 jobs by 2030 include direct and indirect employment; it should not be presented as factory headcount alone.

## How it fits New Clark City's existing stack

BCDA has already been testing smart mobility and pursuing open-access ICT, water-management and decarbonisation initiatives. The latest study is best read as an attempt to connect energy, transport and local manufacturing rather than as an isolated factory announcement.

New Clark City's practical difference from a stand-alone urban project is that a government master developer controls a large greenfield area where a battery plant, industrial park and mobility pilots coexist. That does not yet mean district-wide energy orchestration is in place.

## Six diligence items for prospective suppliers

1. Define whether the offer covers cells, power conversion, energy management, charging, grid connection, construction or maintenance.
2. Model factory peak shaving, backup power, renewable integration and EV charging as separate commercial cases.
3. Map fire protection, thermal-runaway detection, isolation, insurance and end-of-life responsibilities.
4. Confirm distribution-utility, grid-interconnection and retail-power requirements for each site.
5. Test how locally made batteries would integrate with control, quality and service capabilities from Japan or elsewhere.
6. Monitor BCDA disclosures and do not treat an MOU as an awarded project.
