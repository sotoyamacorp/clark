# Philippine FDI falls to $210m in May, an 11-year low

## Introduction

Net foreign direct investment (FDI) into the Philippines fell to $210 million in May 2026, its lowest monthly level in more than 11 years. Yet BOI and PEZA reported rising approved investment over roughly the same period.

The difference is fundamental: BSP FDI is actual cross-border funding recorded on a net basis, while an approval is a future project plan. A company should not treat the two as interchangeable.

## Monthly trend

| Month | Net FDI | Year-on-year |
|---|---:|---:|
| January | $469m | -35.7% |
| February | $638m | -25.4% |
| March | $611m | +26.1% |
| April | $250m | -58.8% |
| May | $210m | about -64.7% |

The weakness is not only a one-month story. Cumulative January-May inflows fell from about $3.27bn in 2025 to about $2.18bn in 2026.

## What FDI includes

| Component | Example | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Net equity | New shares less withdrawals | Closest to new ownership capital |
| Reinvested earnings | Local profit retained | Continued investment by existing firms |
| Debt instruments | Intercompany loans | Often volatile month to month |

The sharp April decline was largely tied to weaker debt-instrument flows. It does not mean every greenfield project stopped.

## Why approvals can rise while FDI falls

| Indicator | 2026 period | YoY | Measures |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| BOI approvals | PHP461.84bn, 124 projects in H1 | +21% | Approved domestic and foreign plans |
| PEZA approvals | PHP140.688bn, 157 projects in H1 | +94.42% | New and expansion ecozone plans |
| BSP net FDI | About $2.18bn in Jan-May | -33.4% | Actual net cross-border funding |

Approvals may take months or years to become land purchases, construction, equipment and funding. BOI and PEZA data also include Philippine-owned capital.

## Historical context and scenarios

Annual net FDI declined from $7.79bn in 2024 to a preliminary roughly $6.19bn in 2025. The first five months of 2026 remained below the prior year. Rather than invent a point forecast, companies can use three scenarios.

| Scenario | Assumption | Preparation |
|---|---|---|
| Recovery | H1 approvals convert into funding | Watch labor and site competition |
| Flat | Equity holds but intercompany lending stays weak | Phase capex and diversify financing |
| Downside | Geopolitics, FX or rates delay plans | Add contingency and delay rights |

## Five actions for investors

1. Compare parent funding, local debt and equity.
2. Stress-test capex across multiple peso exchange rates.
3. Do not count approved projects as operating customer demand.
4. Add delay and termination conditions to site and equipment contracts.
5. Read FDI together with approvals, permits and actual construction.
