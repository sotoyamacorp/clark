# Pampanga's ₱94.22B agri-fisheries base and Clark logistics

## Introduction

For food processors and logistics operators evaluating Clark, the surrounding supply base matters as much as airport and expressway access. New Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) data show that Pampanga's agriculture and fisheries production was worth **₱94.22 billion in 2025**, second among all provinces. Its fisheries output was worth **₱38.97 billion**, the country's highest provincial total.

In plain terms, Clark sits beside a large food-producing economy. But these are current-price values, not physical output volumes. Investment decisions still require commodity-level checks on volume, quality, seasonality, aggregation and logistics.

Bukidnon's advantage is strongly crop-based, while Pampanga stands out in fisheries. Companies should compare the commodity mix with their own bill of materials, not treat every high-value province as interchangeable.

## The Clark National Food Hub connection

Clark International Airport Corporation (CIAC) plans an approximately 60-hectare Clark National Food Hub within Clark Aviation Capital. In October 2025, CIAC and Food Terminal Inc. formalized cooperation to develop a wholesale hub with storage, cold-chain, processing, research, quality-control and trading functions.

The hub remains a publicly announced development project; the full facility is not yet operating. Companies should confirm the current schedule, operator, plot terms, fees and service commencement directly with CIAC.

## What this means for Japanese companies

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8"><h3 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Most relevant to</h3><p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed">Seafood processors, frozen-food companies, hotel and restaurant wholesalers, food-testing laboratories, packaging suppliers, cold-storage operators, logistics software providers and aquaculture-input companies.</p></div>

An initial feasibility study should verify commodity volumes by month, supplier concentration, required temperature zones, permits from agencies such as FDA and BFAR, and the full landed cost for both domestic distribution and exports.
