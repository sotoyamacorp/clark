# Philippine Salary Increases Projected at 5.1% in 2027: How to Build a Labor-Cost Budget

## Introduction

How much salary growth should a Philippine market-entry model assume? A WTW survey puts the median company salary-increase budget for 2027 at 5.1%, slightly above 5.0% in 2026. That is a useful benchmark, but applying it uniformly to every employee can create a misleading cost model.

## What the 5.1% Figure Means

The figure is the median salary budget companies expect for 2027. It edges up from 5.0% in 2026, reflecting the balance between cost pressure and talent retention.

Across Southeast Asia, the forecast is below Vietnam's 7% and Indonesia's 6%, but above Malaysia's 4.7%, Thailand's 4.6%, and Singapore's 4%.

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8"><h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">A salary budget is not an individual pay raise</h4><p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed">Even with a 5.1% company budget, promotion increases, scarce-skill adjustments, performance awards, and pay freezes will differ. New-hire offers can move on a separate market curve. Model existing employees and new recruitment separately.</p></div>

## Keep Minimum Wages Separate

The Philippines does not have one nationwide minimum wage. Regional wage boards set and review rates, and DOLE began the next review cycle in May 2026. Timing and amounts vary by region.

Operations with many near-minimum-wage employees are more exposed to wage orders. Specialist-heavy teams are more exposed to market pay and retention pressure. The same 5.1% benchmark can therefore mean very different things across employers.

## Three Scenarios for Japanese Companies

### Base case

Use roughly 5.1% for existing-employee salary budgets, with normal hiring and attrition assumptions.

### Upside-cost case

Add separate allowances for scarce skills, wage orders, and benefits. Semiconductor, cloud, data-center, finance, and legal roles may run above the company average.

### Currency case

Local payroll is paid in pesos, while Japanese headquarters often report in yen. Treat salary inflation and PHP/JPY as separate variables.
