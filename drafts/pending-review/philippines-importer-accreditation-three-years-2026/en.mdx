# Philippine Importer Accreditation Now Lasts 3 Years, but Annual Filing Remains

## Introduction

Customs Administrative Order (CAO) No. 01-2026 extends Philippine Bureau of Customs importer accreditation from one year to three years. The three-year fee is PHP5,000, down from PHP6,000 across three annual renewals.

The larger benefit is operational continuity, not the PHP1,000 saving. A lapsed accreditation can interrupt imports. Yet three-year validity does not remove annual compliance.

## Key points

<div class="bg-navy-50 border border-navy-200 rounded-lg p-8 my-12"><div class="space-y-6">
<p class="text-gray-800"><strong>1.</strong> Accreditation validity increases from one to three years.</p>
<p class="text-gray-800"><strong>2.</strong> The three-year fee falls from PHP6,000 to PHP5,000.</p>
<p class="text-gray-800"><strong>3.</strong> Annual Reportorial Compliance (ARC) is still required.</p>
<p class="text-gray-800"><strong>4.</strong> Six clean consecutive years, AEO or Super Green Lane status may support automatic renewal.</p>

## Annual compliance remains

An accredited importer must file ARC within 30 days of its accreditation anniversary, including confirmation when business information has not changed.

If the ARC is late, BOC may issue a Notice of Non-Compliance with a further 15-day response period. Continued non-compliance can lead to suspension for a first violation and revocation for a second violation.

Track the ARC, company changes and the eventual renewal in one register.</p></div>

## Automatic renewal is not passive

Eligible categories include importers with six consecutive years of accreditation and no suspension, revocation or cancellation, plus Authorized Economic Operator and Super Green Lane accredited importers.

Even eligible importers must apply online at least seven working days before expiry and upload the prescribed documents. “Automatic” does not mean no action is needed.

## What this means for foreign companies

Manufacturers, retailers and distributors that routinely import equipment, components or products can reduce renewal-related disruption. They should:

1. Track accreditation anniversaries, annual ARC dates and the three-year expiry.
2. Connect SEC, address, director, warehouse and product changes to customs compliance.
3. Define what the customs broker handles and what remains the importer's responsibility.
4. Evaluate AEO or SGL based on shipment volume and clearance frequency.

The reform reduces paperwork but does not transfer accountability away from the importer. Verify the current BOC Accounts Management Office checklist before the first shipment.

## Conclusion

The three-year validity removes a recurring renewal bottleneck. Companies should treat it as a chance to build better annual compliance, not as permission to ignore BOC for three years.
