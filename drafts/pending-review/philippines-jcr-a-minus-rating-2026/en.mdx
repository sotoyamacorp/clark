# Philippines keeps JCR A- rating: what investors should read

## Introduction

Japan Credit Rating Agency (JCR) affirmed the Philippines' foreign- and local-currency long-term issuer ratings at **A- with a Stable outlook** on August 28, 2026. The rating has remained at A- since an upgrade in June 2020.

A sovereign rating is an opinion about the national government's ability to service debt. It does not approve a foreign investment or guarantee a project. It can, however, inform country funding conditions, bank credit discussions, and foreign-exchange and interest-rate scenarios.

Stable does not mean permanent. It means JCR does not currently see a strong directional bias over its normal outlook horizon.

## Four factors behind JCR's decision

### Domestic-demand base

JCR points to population and private consumption and expects recovery in the second half of 2026 and over the medium term. That is a forecast, not an observed outcome.

### External resilience

The government relies heavily on domestic funding, external debt is contained, and foreign reserves provide a buffer against shocks.

### Gradual fiscal consolidation

The fiscal deficit narrowed from 5.7% of GDP in 2024 to 5.6% in 2025. The direction improved, but the balance remains weaker than before the pandemic.

### Manageable government debt

Government debt was 63.2% of GDP at end-2025. JCR sees this as relatively low among A-range sovereigns, though higher rates, depreciation or revenue shortfalls could change the burden.

All are investment grade, but their symbols are not one common ruler. Even JCR's and R&I's identically written A- ratings use different methodologies. Investors should compare strengths, downside triggers and assumptions.

JCR's sovereign list places Singapore at AAA, Korea at AA, Malaysia at A+, Thailand at A and the Philippines at A-. That is a comparison of sovereign credit, not a ranking of city-level investment opportunities.

## What it means for Japanese companies

### Financing

A stable investment-grade rating can support the broader funding environment. A company's actual borrowing rate will still depend on currency, tenor, bank, parent guarantee, collateral and project cash flow.

### FX and interest-rate scenarios

The rating does not remove peso or rate volatility. Budgets should include base, weaker-peso/higher-rate, and stronger-peso/lower-rate cases.

### Counterparties and approvals

Sovereign credit does not guarantee a local partner, PEZA/BOI/CDC approval, land title, power supply, tax outcome or disaster exposure. Those require separate due diligence.

## Five items for a board paper

1. Agency, rating, outlook and date.
2. Strengths: domestic demand, low external debt and reserves.
3. Watch items: deficit, debt, infrastructure execution and energy costs.
4. Transmission to the company: rates, FX, bank credit and demand.
5. Project risks not covered by the sovereign rating.
