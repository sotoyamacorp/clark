# ASEAN Connectivity 2035: six Philippine opportunities

## Introduction

The ASEAN Connectivity Strategic Plan 2026–2035 is not simply a program for building roads and ports.

It connects power grids, submarine cables, customs, regulation and workforce mobility so ASEAN can function more effectively as one business region. In August 2026, the Philippine government called for faster implementation and the creation of “Prosperity Corridors.”

For Japanese and other international companies, the central message is that **projects linking Philippine logistics, data, power and skills with the wider region will carry greater policy relevance than isolated domestic assets**.

## What changed from the previous master plan

The Master Plan on ASEAN Connectivity 2025, or MPAC 2025, had five areas: sustainable infrastructure, digital innovation, seamless logistics, regulatory excellence and people mobility.

The new plan establishes smart and sustainable urban development as a separate sixth area. It brings decarbonization, urbanization, supply-chain change, digital technology including AI, and disaster and geopolitical resilience into the next decade.

### 1. Sustainable infrastructure

The plan covers priority-project pipelines, air, land and maritime transport, the ASEAN Power Grid, the Trans-ASEAN Gas Pipeline and mobilization of private investment.

As an archipelago, the Philippines needs to improve domestic and international links at the same time. Opportunities include corridors connecting ports and airports with industrial zones, power transmission, and PPP project preparation, engineering and operations.

### 2. Smart and sustainable urban development

This is the newly independent area. It treats congestion, water, waste, disaster preparedness and energy efficiency as connected urban operations, rather than isolated equipment purchases.

Beyond Manila, growth centers such as Clark, Cebu and Davao can require integrated sensors, control systems, billing and asset maintenance.

### 3. Digital innovation

The Philippines has emphasized resilient submarine cables, digital infrastructure and trusted data connectivity. A data-center investment therefore involves more than the building: landing stations, domestic backbone, power, cooling and cybersecurity form one decision.

### 4. Seamless logistics and supply chains

New ports and roads cannot reduce lead times when customs, quarantine, documents and tracking remain fragmented. The plan includes streamlined customs, immigration and quarantine procedures and better cross-border processes.

This creates demand not only for logistics networks, but also e-customs, visibility tools, cold chain, warehouse systems and transport insurance.

### 5. Regulatory excellence and cooperation

The plan does not create a single ASEAN legal system. It promotes transparent regulations, consultation, interoperability and reduced administrative friction.

RegTech and professional services can help companies handle differences in product certification, electronic signatures, data transfers and permits.

### 6. People connectivity

Infrastructure cannot operate without engineers, technicians and managers. Skills development, education links and mobility are common requirements across construction, IT, tourism, healthcare and manufacturing.

Companies should include qualifications, training, local-management development and cross-border deployment in investment plans—not simply headcount.

## USD210 billion is a needs estimate, not a market order book

The plan estimates annual ASEAN infrastructure needs of at least USD210 billion from 2023 through 2030. It is not a committed procurement amount.

Companies should not read the estimate as “ASEAN will procure USD210 billion.” They must identify the Philippine agency, project sponsor, funding source and maturity of each opportunity.

## The Philippine emphasis: corridors, power and digital links

At ASEAN meetings in August 2026, the Philippines called for faster implementation and promoted Prosperity Corridors linking regional growth centers. It also highlighted the ASEAN Power Grid, digital infrastructure and submarine-cable resilience.

These statements are not legally binding project awards, but they are policy signals about the Philippine projects the government wants to connect with regional priorities.

## Seven questions for investors and suppliers

1. Which of the six areas, 14 objectives and 49 measures supports the project?
2. Which national or local agency actually procures or authorizes it?
3. Does it rely on the national budget, ODA, a PPP or private investment?
4. Are counterpart facilities and regulations ready in other countries?
5. What are the land, power, data and environmental prerequisites?
6. Who will operate, maintain, collect revenue and transfer skills after construction?
7. What will annual reviews and the 2026–2030 midterm review measure?
