# Boeing supplier Heatcon establishes a composite repair base in Clark

## Introduction

When you hear of the aerospace industry, you may think of airframe manufacturing and aircraft maintenance, but the supply chain also includes highly specialized materials, equipment, and repair processes. In June 2026, Heatcon Asia, a supplier to Boeing and other aviation companies, signed a 25-year lease with Clark International Airport Corporation (CIAC). The company plans to establish manufacturing, materials distribution, and in-shop composite repair functions in Clark.

## What is Heatcon?

Heatcon was founded in 1978 and provides hot bonders, heat blankets, process materials, and composite repair solutions for commercial and military aviation. A hot bonder is a device that controls temperature, time, and vacuum during the curing of adhesives and resins used in composite repairs. This is a field where equipment and work records that can reproduce specified conditions are directly linked to quality.

CIAC has announced that the new facility will be located within the Clark Civil Aviation Complex and will serve the company's growing customer base in the Asia-Pacific region. The length of the lease is 25 years, indicating Heatcon's intention to establish a long-term base rather than simply a sales office.

| Planned function | What it means | Related business opportunities |
|---|---|---|
| Manufacturing | Manufacturing of equipment and related products for composite material repair | Precision parts, control equipment, jigs and tools, quality control |
| Materials distribution | Inventory and supply of repair materials for customers in the Asia-Pacific region | Low-temperature and humidity-controlled storage, bonded logistics, inventory management |
| In-shop repair | Repair work on composite parts removed from aircraft | Inspection, non-destructive testing, technician training, repair records |

## Why are composite materials important in aircraft?

Advanced composites are materials made from a combination of carbon fiber, glass fiber, resins, and other materials. According to the US Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), they are lighter, stronger, and more corrosion-resistant than traditional materials and are used in important aircraft structures.

However, internal delamination and bond defects are not always visible from the surface. Repair requires standardized steps such as damage assessment, surface preparation, material layup, vacuum bagging, heat curing, and inspection. Because it is not enough to simply have the equipment, experience, procedures, temperature and humidity control, traceability of materials, and qualified personnel become barriers to entry.

## What Heatcon's entry means for Clark

Clark already has aircraft MRO companies, parts manufacturers, cargo airlines, and logistics companies. Heatcon's addition to the mix will bring the functions of repair equipment, materials, and in-shop repair closer together geographically.

This is important because the aerospace supply chain is becoming more localized. If repair materials, equipment, and technical support are available locally, airlines and MRO companies can shorten lead times and reduce the risk of aircraft being out of service waiting for parts or materials to arrive. Clark's competitive advantage should therefore be evaluated not only in terms of airport and tax incentives, but also in terms of the range of suppliers that can support operations.

The Philippine Board of Investments (BOI) states that the country already has Tier 1 and Tier 2 companies supplying flight control systems, galleys, oxygen systems, seats, and other products to Boeing and Airbus. Heatcon's entry is a move to fill in the surrounding repair and support functions.

## Opportunities for Japanese companies

Areas with potential compatibility include the following:

1. Manufacturers of precision machined parts, jigs, and temperature control equipment
2. Inspection equipment and non-destructive testing service providers
3. Suppliers of adhesives, films, release materials, and other process consumables
4. Logistics companies that handle materials requiring temperature, humidity, and expiration date control
5. Educational institutions and training companies that train aircraft maintenance and composite material technicians

However, the announcement does not disclose the investment amount, site area, start of operations, or local procurement targets. It would be premature to assume that a contract will be awarded based solely on proximity to Clark. Potential suppliers should first confirm Heatcon's approved supplier requirements, aerospace quality certifications such as AS9100, material traceability, and export control requirements.
