# SEC proposes one business start date with parallel permits

## Introduction

Incorporation is not the same as being ready to operate. Companies entering the Philippines may still need tax, employment, local-government and sector approvals after the SEC issues a certificate of incorporation.

Securities and Exchange Commission Chair Francis Lim has proposed a “One Business Start Date” under which a company could begin commercial operations after SEC registration or the required primary regulatory licence while completing other government registrations in parallel.

The conclusion is important: this is not yet in force. An executive order is expected to be required, and key operating safeguards have not been published. An SEC certificate alone should not be treated as permission to bypass current requirements.

## Current process versus the proposal

Philippine incorporation is already substantially digital. For eligible domestic stock corporations, OneSEC automates steps from name verification to the digital certificate of incorporation and targets one-day processing. After incorporation, the Philippine Business Hub connects businesses with tax, social-security and local permit processes.

In plain language, OneSEC reduces the time required to create the legal entity. One Business Start Date is a separate proposal aimed at reducing the period before that entity can start commercial activity.

## What has not been decided

The reported proposal does not yet answer several questions needed for an operating plan.

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8"><h3 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">It is not a permit-waiver programme</h3><p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed">Until the executive order defines coverage, effectivity, deadlines and exclusions, companies should continue to satisfy BIR, local-government, fire, environmental, employment and sector-specific rules.</p></div>

Outstanding issues include:

1. Coverage of domestic corporations, foreign branches, partnerships and other entities.
2. Additional conditions for food, health, finance, education, construction and environmentally sensitive operations.
3. Treatment of business, occupancy and fire-safety permits issued locally.
4. Invoicing, books, withholding and VAT before BIR registration is complete.
5. Hiring and payroll before SSS, Pag-IBIG and PhilHealth registration is complete.
6. Deadlines, suspension and penalties if parallel registrations are not completed.

## Relationship with the 3-7-20-day rule

Republic Act No. 11032, the Ease of Doing Business law, sets default processing ceilings after a complete application: three working days for simple transactions, seven for complex transactions and 20 for highly technical transactions. It also includes deadlines and streamlining provisions for fire and business permits.

Previous reforms focused on shortening each individual government process. The new proposal is structurally different: it would allow selected processes to run at the same time instead of waiting for each preceding registration to finish.

The SEC says it has also expanded digital services and internal processing deadlines, including a deemed-approved policy for applications that exceed prescribed periods. Corporate document-request fees have been reduced by a cumulative 62.5% from 2023 levels, producing about PHP211 million in business savings by June 2026.

Foreign branches and representative offices currently use eSPARC Regular Processing. They should not assume that OneSEC's one-day route or the proposed operating rule automatically applies. Entity type and regulated activity must remain separate lines in the project plan.

No reduction in safety, environmental or sector regulation has yet been approved.</p></div>
