# Government Confident on H2, World Bank Cautious: A Growth Outlook Gap

## Introduction

[Yesterday we covered the World Bank's downward revision](/en/articles/philippines-worldbank-growth-outlook-2026/) of its Philippine growth outlook, delivered in a fairly cautious tone against the backdrop of the flood-control infrastructure corruption scandal. Around the same time, though, the Philippine government struck a notably different tone. Economic Planning Secretary Arsenio Balisacan of the National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA) said the second half would see "much improved" infrastructure and government spending, expressing confidence the full-year target can still be met.

Why the gap between two institutions looking at the same economy? This article covers the government's reasoning and what it means for companies operating in the Philippines.

## Q1 Growth Was Just 2.8%; Hitting the Target Needs 3.7% Average for the Rest of the Year

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">The weakest Q1 since 2021</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    As [previously reported](/en/articles/philippines-gdp-growth-q1-2026/), the Philippines' real GDP grew just 2.8% year-on-year in Q1 2026 — the weakest first quarter since 2021. The government has progressively lowered its full-year 2026 growth target from an original 6-7% down to 3.5-4.5%. According to Secretary Balisacan, just hitting the low end of that range (3.5%) requires average growth of 3.7% across Q2 through Q4.
  </p>
</div>

---

## A Sharp Slowdown in Government Spending Is the Main Culprit: 18.7% Growth to Just 4.8%

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">"Growth would have hit 3.8% otherwise"</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    Balisacan explained that cautious budget execution in response to the [flood-control infrastructure corruption scandal](/en/articles/philippines-worldbank-growth-outlook-2026/) weighed on Q1 growth. Government spending growth slowed sharply, from 18.7% a year earlier to just 4.8% in Q1 2026. He noted that "had government spending proceeded as expected, growth would have reached 3.8%." It's worth noting the World Bank cited the same corruption scandal as a factor behind its own downward revision — both institutions are looking at the same underlying phenomenon.
  </p>
</div>

---

## Balisacan: "H2 Will Be Much Improved," Banking on Catch-Up Spending

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Normalizing budget execution, plus a favorable base effect</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    Balisacan expects the second half to see "much improved" infrastructure and government spending. Budget execution — including the stricter project verification and payment validation by the Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) put in place after the corruption allegations — is gradually normalizing, and the government is pursuing a "catch-up spending" plan to fully disburse the year's budget. Economists have also pointed to a favorable base effect (last year's weaker comparison period boosting relative growth rates) as a reason to expect a second-half turnaround.
  </p>
</div>

---

## Inflation Is Also Running at 6.4%, Above the BSP's Target for Four Straight Months

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">A sharp jump from 1.7% in 2025 to the 6% range</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    Alongside slower growth, inflation is also under upward pressure. Inflation hit 6.4% in June 2026, exceeding the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas' (BSP) 2-4% target range for a fourth consecutive month. Full-year inflation is expected to average around 6.0% — a sharp jump from 1.7% in 2025 — and is projected to stay above the BSP's target through 2027. Some observers have flagged the risk of slower growth and elevated inflation occurring simultaneously.
  </p>
</div>

## Why This Matters for Companies

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Don't plan around the optimistic scenario alone</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    With the World Bank taking a more cautious view (3.7% for 2026) and the government expressing confidence it can defend the low end of its target range, there's a real gap between institutions. For companies operating in the Philippines, it's worth continuously monitoring how quickly the government's catch-up spending plan actually materializes, while keeping a contingency plan ready in case the optimistic scenario doesn't pan out. For businesses tied to public works or infrastructure specifically, the pace of budget-execution normalization is effectively a leading indicator of real business opportunity.
  </p>
</div>
