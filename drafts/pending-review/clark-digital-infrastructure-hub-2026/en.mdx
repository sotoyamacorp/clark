# Clark Freeport Becomes a Digital Infrastructure Hub: Data Center Investment

## Introduction

When people think of Clark, they usually picture manufacturing, IT-BPO, and semiconductor-related export operations. In 2026, another side of the area is starting to emerge: Clark as a hub for digital infrastructure — data centers, fiber backbones, and subsea cable networks.

On March 20, 2026, the Clark Development Corporation (CDC) and the Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) launched a digital infrastructure program in Clark Freeport Zone. Alongside it, a local company is pursuing a ₱1 billion data center project. This article covers what's actually happening and what it means for Japanese companies.

## What Is Clark Freeport Zone, Exactly?

Before diving in, a quick primer on how the area came to be.

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">From a US military base to a special economic zone</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    Clark Freeport Zone sits on what was once one of Southeast Asia's largest US military bases, Clark Air Base. After the 1991 eruption of Mount Pinatubo damaged the base and the US withdrew that June, President Fidel Ramos designated the area the "Clark Special Economic Zone" via Presidential Proclamation No. 163 on April 3, 1993. That same year, the Clark Development Corporation (CDC) — a subsidiary of the Bases Conversion and Development Authority (BCDA) — was established to manage and develop it. Its Freeport status was formalized in 2007 under Republic Act 9400. Sources vary on the exact size depending on whether they mean the core area CDC directly manages or the broader zone including nearby affiliated developments, so we're not citing a specific hectare figure here.
  </p>
</div>

We've covered Clark's growth as a manufacturing, IT-BPO, and semiconductor export hub in a <a href="/en/articles/clark-history-and-value/" class="text-accent-700 underline hover:text-accent-500">separate article</a>. The digital infrastructure push covered here is a new layer building on top of that existing industrial base.

## What CDC and DICT's Digital Infrastructure Launch Involves

On Friday, March 20, 2026, CDC, DICT, and telecom operator Converge jointly launched the "Converge National Digital Infrastructure" program at an event in Angeles City, home to Clark Freeport Zone.

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">What is DICT?</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    The Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) is the Philippine government agency overseeing national ICT policy. It traces back to the Commission on Information and Communications Technology (CICT), a transitional body created by Executive Order 269 in 2004, and was formally established as a full department under Republic Act 10844, which took effect May 23, 2016. It absorbed the communications-related units of the former Department of Transportation and Communications, including the National Computer Center and the Telecommunications Office. Today it's the primary agency responsible for planning and implementing the country's national ICT development agenda.
  </p>
</div>

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Who was at the ceremony</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    The event drew officials at the national, provincial, and city levels: CDC President and CEO Agnes Devanadera, DICT Secretary Henry Rhoel Aguda, Pampanga Governor Lilia Pineda, Angeles City Mayor Carmelo Lazatin II, and First Lady Louise Araneta-Marcos.
  </p>
</div>

The program aims to accelerate the Philippines' digital transformation and connectivity through data center expansion, fiber-optic backbones, and subsea cable networks. Secretary Aguda said at the event: "Today's launch is more than just infrastructure. It is a clear signal that the Philippines is no longer thinking small when it comes to connectivity."

CDC echoed that framing, saying "with the whole-of-government push for digital infrastructure gaining momentum, the Freeport is ready to host more data centers through public-private collaborations" — positioning Clark Freeport Zone as one of the landing spots for the country's broader digital infrastructure policy.

<div class="grid grid-cols-1 md:grid-cols-2 gap-6 my-12">
  <div class="bg-white border-2 border-gray-200 rounded-lg p-6 shadow-sm">
    <div class="text-3xl mb-3">🖥️</div>
    <h4 class="text-base font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Angeles Data Center: 12MW, roughly ₱5 billion invested</h4>
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-700 mb-4">Converge's newly built, AI-ready, scalable facility supporting data processing, storage, and digital services. Converge already operates an existing "Clark Data Center" as well, giving it multiple facilities in the area.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="bg-white border-2 border-gray-200 rounded-lg p-6 shadow-sm">
    <div class="text-3xl mb-3">🌐</div>
    <h4 class="text-base font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Part of a nationwide fiber and subsea cable buildout</h4>
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-700 mb-4">Converge operates nearly 900,000km of fiber nationwide plus a growing low-earth-orbit satellite network. It's also involved in the trans-Pacific Bifrost Cable System (20,000km, 260+Tbps capacity, landing in Indonesia, the Philippines, Singapore, Guam, and the US) and the SEA-H2X Cable System (160Tbps initial capacity, landing in Hong Kong, Hainan, the Philippines, Thailand, and Singapore).</p>
  </div>
</div>

<p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed mb-8">
Converge CEO Dennis Anthony Uy described it this way: "This is the complete stack digital infrastructure that we have built. This is infrastructure designed for full coverage, reliability, and scale." Clark's data center is one node within this nationwide network buildout. According to CDC, approved investment in the ICT industry within Clark Freeport Zone reached ₱83 million for full-year 2025. That's still a modest figure for the data center sector specifically, but this launch is meant to prime the pump for more.
</p>

## A Concrete Project: A ₱1 Billion AI-Ready Data Center

Separate from the government-led program, there's also a concrete private-sector investment underway.

<div class="space-y-6 my-8">
  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">1. IITI is investing ₱1 billion on a 3-hectare site</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      Island Information and Technology, Inc. (IITI) is developing a ₱1 billion data center on a 3-hectare site along Centennial Road within Clark Freeport Zone, Angeles City. It's positioned as an "AI-ready" facility, meant to support IT operations, software development, and AI model training.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">2. IITI resumed PSE trading in July 2025 after a four-year suspension</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      IITI's trading on the Philippine Stock Exchange (PSE) had been suspended since March 2021 over non-submission of financial reports, resuming on July 7, 2025 — roughly four years later. This data center investment marks one of the company's flagship projects since its turnaround.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">3. A separate initiative from New Clark City's "Pax Silica"</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      We previously covered the <a href="/en/articles/clark-pax-silica-ai-hub/" class="text-accent-700 underline hover:text-accent-500">"Pax Silica" initiative</a>, a 1,618-hectare semiconductor and AI industrial hub planned for New Clark City, just north of Clark, with Taiwan's Foxconn as anchor investor. This latest push from CDC, DICT, and IITI is a separate, parallel effort to attract data centers to Clark Freeport Zone itself (the Angeles City side). The similar area names can cause confusion, so it's worth noting: New Clark City and Clark Freeport Zone are adjacent but distinct development zones.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

This momentum is also building at the cabinet level: in June 2026, reports indicated the Philippine government's cabinet had discussed positioning Clark as a semiconductor and AI hub under the Luzon Economic Corridor initiative (see also our earlier coverage of the <a href="/en/articles/clark-luzon-economic-corridor/" class="text-accent-700 underline hover:text-accent-500">Luzon Economic Corridor and Clark</a>).

## Other Companies Showing Interest

Beyond CDC, DICT, Converge, and IITI, other major players are also eyeing the data center opportunity around Clark.

<div class="space-y-6 my-8">
  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Major telecom and data center firms reportedly designing facilities</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      With demand from hyperscalers, enterprise IT, and AI workloads in mind, reports indicate ST Telemedia Global Data Centers, PLDT, VITRO, FLOW Digital Infrastructure, and Ayala Land Logistics Holdings Corp. are all designing data center facilities in the broader Clark area.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">An $890 million project also underway in New Clark City</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      Endec Group, partnering with Narra Technology Park Development Inc., has secured a lease on 47 hectares within New Clark City for "Narra Technology Park," a hyperscale data center campus planned in three development phases, with reported total investment of roughly $890 million. (Note: this is a New Clark City project, not Clark Freeport Zone — see the area background section below for the distinction.)
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Sovereign wealth fund Maharlika eyeing Pax Silica's power needs</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      The <a href="/en/articles/clark-pax-silica-ai-hub/" class="text-accent-700 underline hover:text-accent-500">"Pax Silica" initiative</a> we covered earlier is projected to need up to 5 gigawatts of power at scale. The Philippines' sovereign wealth fund, Maharlika Investment Corporation, signed an agreement with BCDA back in 2024 to explore investment opportunities in New Clark City, and reports indicate it's now interested in helping supply power to Pax Silica.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

## Temper Expectations: Where the Market Actually Stands

Amid the positive headlines, there's also data worth keeping in view for a balanced picture.

<div class="grid grid-cols-1 md:grid-cols-2 gap-6 my-12">
  <div class="bg-white border-2 border-gray-200 rounded-lg p-6 shadow-sm">
    <div class="text-3xl mb-3">📈</div>
    <h4 class="text-base font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">APAC data center demand projected to grow 22% annually</h4>
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-700 mb-4">According to research firm IDC, Asia-Pacific data center capacity is projected to grow at roughly 22% per year through 2029.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="bg-white border-2 border-gray-200 rounded-lg p-6 shadow-sm">
    <div class="text-3xl mb-3">⚠️</div>
    <h4 class="text-base font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Yet the Philippines was called "the most immature" in Southeast Asia</h4>
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-700 mb-4">As of August 2025, real estate firm Cushman &amp; Wakefield classified the Philippines as the least mature Southeast Asian market for data center development, citing high real estate, construction, and maintenance costs.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed mb-8">
In other words, strong regional tailwinds (robust APAC demand) coexist with country-specific headwinds (high costs, an immature development ecosystem). Most of the CDC/DICT program and IITI's investment plan covered here only got underway in 2026 and are not yet operational. It's important to distinguish "announced plans" from "facilities actually running" when evaluating this trend.
</p>

## Area Background: Pampanga, Angeles City, and New Clark City

We've mentioned "Pampanga," "Angeles City," "Clark Freeport Zone," and "New Clark City" throughout this article. Since the geography can get confusing, here's how they relate to each other.

<div class="space-y-6 my-8">
  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Pampanga province: population 2.6 million, GDP growing 5.1%</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      The Clark area sits within Pampanga province, in Central Luzon. As of the 2024 census, Pampanga's population was 2,586,446 (9th largest among the Philippines' 82 provinces). Its capital is San Fernando City (population roughly 377,000, about 66km north of Manila). Pampanga's GDP grew 5.1% year-on-year in 2024 to ₱595.36 billion, driven by growth in financial and insurance services (+19.4%) and construction (+15.8%), alongside its traditional agricultural base of rice, corn, and sugarcane.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Angeles City: geographically in Pampanga, administratively independent, population ~460,000</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      Most of Clark Freeport Zone sits within Angeles City. Geographically it's part of Pampanga province, but administratively it's a "highly urbanized city" independent from the provincial government. Its population was 462,928 at the 2020 census (an estimated ~616,000 as of 2026). Home to Clark International Airport, its economy spans aviation, IT-BPO, manufacturing, and entertainment/tourism.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">New Clark City: actually about 20km away, in Tarlac province</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      "New Clark City," which has come up repeatedly in this article and past ones, is actually a separate 9,450-hectare development roughly 20km north of Clark Freeport Zone, spanning the municipality of Bamban and the city of Capas in neighboring Tarlac province. It's a BCDA-led master-planned city that reserves 60% of its land for forest, open space, and parks, developing only the remaining 40%. BCDA has floated a long-term vision of a city housing 1.2 million residents and 800,000 workers — but that's a long-range aspiration, not a current reality. Because it's often grouped under the broader "Clark Freeport and Special Economic Zone" umbrella, it's easy to conflate with Clark Freeport Zone itself, but geographically the two are distinct. Both Pax Silica and Narra Technology Park, mentioned above, are projects within New Clark City.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

## Why This Matters for Companies

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">For cloud-dependent businesses or companies weighing business continuity planning (BCP)</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    Clark's data center market is still in its early stages, but it's becoming a real option for diversifying digital infrastructure that has historically been concentrated in Metro Manila. Companies in IT services, BPO, or e-commerce that are sensitive to local data processing and latency — or those looking for a disaster recovery (DR) site to complement Manila operations — may find it worth tracking how these projects actually come online. As noted above, most projects are still at the planning stage, so it's best to keep expectations measured for now.
  </p>
</div>
