# Lufthansa Technik Breaks Ground on Clark MRO Hub, Plans 1,200 Jobs

## Introduction

Can Clark develop from an airport location into a deeper industrial cluster that services aircraft, supplies certified components and trains aviation specialists?

Lufthansa Technik Philippines (LTP) broke ground on a new widebody aircraft facility at Clark International Airport on 6 August 2026. MRO stands for Maintenance, Repair and Overhaul—in plain terms, the inspections, repairs and heavy maintenance that keep commercial aircraft safe and in service.

## What suppliers should evaluate

An MRO facility needs more than a hangar. It requires certified tooling and parts, non-destructive testing, coatings and chemicals, work platforms, environmental controls, waste handling, bonded logistics and specialist training.

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8"><h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">An addressable market is not a purchase order</h4><p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed">The facility plan is confirmed, but individual specifications, approvals, local-presence requirements and procurement dates are not. Suppliers should verify vendor-registration and certification requirements with LTP, LIPAD and the relevant authorities before committing capital.</p></div>

Five practical questions for Japanese suppliers:

1. Does the product require EASA, FAA, CAAP or customer-specific approval?
2. Can the supplier provide local maintenance, calibration, spares and technical support?
3. Can it manage bonded-zone imports and predictable lead times into Clark?
4. When must vendor registration and trials begin ahead of the planned 2028 start?
5. Will the operating model require partnerships with Philippine schools or training providers?
