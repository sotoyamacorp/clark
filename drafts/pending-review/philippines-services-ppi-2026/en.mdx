# Philippine Services PPI: Telecom Down 14.8%, Air Transport Up 9.0%

## Introduction

Services Producer Price Index data released by the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) in August 2026 showed that telecommunications prices fell 14.8% year on year in the second quarter, while air transport prices rose 9.0%.

The services PPI measures prices received by providers for services supplied to domestic customers. It is not an individual quotation, but it offers a market benchmark for telecom renewals and travel and logistics budgets.

Year-on-year compares with the same quarter a year earlier; quarter-on-quarter compares with the immediately preceding quarter. Air transport was more expensive than a year earlier but fell 1.0% from Q1 2026. A positive annual rate does not necessarily mean prices are still rising now.

## Telecom: a larger decline in wired services

Telecommunications PPI fell 14.8% year on year. Wired telecommunications fell 29.4% and wireless fell 7.4%. Quarter on quarter, the overall index fell 0.9%; wired fell 3.3% and wireless was unchanged.

The index covers the wider price movement across business lines, voice, and data services. An individual contract that includes a dedicated line, backup connection, cloud access, cybersecurity, and an SLA—a service-quality commitment such as restoration time—will not necessarily fall at the same rate.

At renewal, compare speed, redundancy, installation cost, incident response, and minimum term on like-for-like specifications, not price alone.

## Aviation: passenger prices rose while freight fell

Air transport services PPI rose 9.0% year on year. Passenger air transport rose 9.0%, while air freight fell 16.0%. Quarter on quarter, the overall index fell 1.0%, and freight fell 11.6%.

Applying one aviation rate to both travel and freight would be misleading. Route, booking window, fuel, exchange rates, weight, volume, and dangerous-goods handling can materially change a Japan–Philippines quotation.

The services PPI uses 2024 as its base year. Telecommunications and air transport are still short quarterly series, so there is not yet enough like-for-like history for a robust long-term or ASEAN ranking. Businesses should accumulate future quarters and compare indices with the same scope.

## Practical implications for Japanese companies

### 1. Test the market before renewing telecom contracts

The decline in wired services supports obtaining multiple quotations instead of automatically renewing. Branches, factories, and BPO sites should assess primary and backup connections separately.

### 2. Budget passenger travel separately from air freight

Keep room for higher travel costs while testing air-freight pricing across forwarders. The two components moved in different directions, so one budget adjustment rate is not appropriate.

### 3. Name the reference index in price-adjustment clauses

A clause that only says “adjusted for inflation” creates ambiguity between CPI and PPI. Specify the service, index, reference period, review frequency, and floor and ceiling.

### 4. Verify with company-specific quotations

PPI is a starting point for negotiation. Connection conditions and airport access differ between Clark and Metro Manila. Final decisions require quotations with the same location, specification, and quantity.
