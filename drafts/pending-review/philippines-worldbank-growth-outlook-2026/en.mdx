# World Bank Cuts Philippine Growth Outlook, Citing Flood Control Graft

## Introduction

On August 4, 2026, the World Bank released its updated growth outlook for the Philippines, cutting its 2027 forecast. What stands out is the reasoning: alongside rising energy prices, the Bank cited "uncertainty around infrastructure procurement" — a reference to a major flood-control infrastructure corruption scandal that has been unfolding since mid-2025.

This article covers the World Bank's latest outlook, the corruption scandal behind it, and what companies operating in the Philippines should keep in mind.

## The World Bank's Latest Outlook: 3.7% for 2026, Cut to 5.2% for 2027

On August 4, 2026, the World Bank released its updated growth forecast for the Philippines.

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">2026 held at 3.7%, 2027 cut from 5.4% to 5.2%</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    The Bank kept its full-year 2026 growth forecast unchanged at 3.7% — near the lower end of the government's own downwardly revised 3.5-4.5% target range. Its 2027 forecast, however, was cut from 5.4% to 5.2%, a 0.2 percentage point downward revision.
  </p>
</div>

<p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed mb-8">
World Bank Division Director Zafer Mustafaoglu said "the country right now is navigating some headwinds," pointing to hesitancy among both public and private investors. Senior Country Economist Jaffar Al-Rikabi explained that "a fall in foreign direct investment...combined with a slowdown in public investment" is weighing on growth. Lead Economist Gonzalo Varela noted that, having just reached <a href="/en/articles/philippines-upper-middle-income-2026/" class="text-accent-700 underline hover:text-accent-500">upper-middle-income status</a>, the Philippines will need "more of an effort" toward "productivity, technology adoption, better jobs" to sustain development going forward.
</p>

## What the Flood-Control Infrastructure Corruption Scandal Is

Behind the "infrastructure procurement uncertainty" the World Bank cited as a drag on growth is a major corruption scandal that has been unfolding since mid-2025.

<div class="space-y-6 my-8">
  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">₱54.5 billion in flood-control funds allocated since 2022 under scrutiny</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      Investigators found that ₱54.5 billion (roughly $9.5 billion) allocated for flood management since 2022 had failed to adequately protect communities from recurring disasters, exposing systemic weaknesses in public procurement, accountability, and climate adaptation. Audits and investigations into infrastructure projects have intensified since mid-2025.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Government construction spending down 45.2% year-on-year, 16% over 11 months</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      Government infrastructure spending plunged 45.2% year-on-year to ₱48 billion in November 2025 alone, with the 11-month total down 16% to ₱991 billion. That contributed to Q4 2025 GDP growth slowing to just 3%. ANZ Research's Sanjay Mathur, chief economist for Southeast Asia and India, said "the governance issues surrounding public infrastructure projects have not only led to a significant deceleration in government capital spending, but have also taken a toll on business and household confidence."
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Private fixed investment has also contracted for three straight quarters</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      The slowdown hasn't been limited to government construction — private fixed capital formation has contracted for three consecutive quarters since Q3 2025. Mathur said "we are not certain at this stage whether growth in the Philippines will inflect for the better anytime soon," while stressing that "a more expansionary fiscal stance is critical in the present environment."
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

## What It Means for Companies: Stalled Public Investment, and a Silver Lining

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">There's a risk side, but also a potential upside</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    For companies involved in public infrastructure bidding or procurement, heightened scrutiny could mean longer, less predictable approval processes in the near term. At the same time, the current wave of audits and investigations could translate into more transparent procurement over the medium term. <a href="/en/articles/philippines-oecd-economic-survey-2026/" class="text-accent-700 underline hover:text-accent-500">The OECD's February 2026 Economic Survey</a> also recommended improving the Philippines' competition policy and governance quality — suggesting institutional pressure to reform is already building. Combined with the <a href="/en/articles/philippines-energy-crisis-2026/" class="text-accent-700 underline hover:text-accent-500">energy price spike tied to Middle East tensions</a>, this is worth tracking as one of two major headwinds facing the Philippine economy in 2026.
  </p>
</div>
