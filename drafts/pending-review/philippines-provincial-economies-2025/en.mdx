# Philippines local economies in 2025: what 115 areas show

## Introduction

A national GDP growth figure cannot tell a company which Philippine location fits its business. The Philippine Statistics Authority's Provincial Product Accounts (PPA) show that 76 of 82 provinces and all 33 Highly Urbanized Cities (HUCs) expanded in 2025.

In plain terms, 109 of 115 local economies grew, but their pace and scale differed sharply. Fifty-two grew faster than the national rate of 4.4%. Companies should therefore treat the Philippines as a portfolio of distinct local markets and production bases.

## What the PPA measures

The PPA estimates GDP for provinces and HUCs using a framework aligned with the national accounts. It shows local economic size, annual growth and the value added of agriculture, industry and services. It does not directly measure addressable demand, household income or a company's market share.

Fast growth and large scale are different attributes. Consumer businesses need population, income and retail catchments; manufacturers need clusters, power and logistics; BPO companies need talent, connectivity and office economics.

## How Clark fits the comparison

Clark's economic area spans Pampanga and Angeles City, so PPA administrative boundaries do not perfectly match the operating market. A Clark assessment should combine Pampanga, Angeles, Tarlac and Bulacan statistics with locator and employment data from the Clark Freeport.

In the 2024 industry GVA release, Pampanga contributed 3.9% of national industry GVA and ranked among the country's major industrial locations. That industrial and logistics base matters when evaluating Clark as an alternative to Metro Manila.

## A practical site-selection method

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8"><h3 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Use PPA as the first screen</h3><p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed">Separate high-growth, large-scale and industry-cluster candidates. Then score talent, wages, rent, power, logistics, disaster risk and permitting on the same basis. GDP is an input, not the final answer.</p></div>
