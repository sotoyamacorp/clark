# Philippine GDP Growth Slows to 2.8% in Q1 2026: A 5-Year Low and What It Means for Japanese Companies

## Introduction

If your expansion plan is built purely on "the Philippines is a high-growth market," it's worth a closer look at the latest numbers. On May 7, 2026, the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) reported that real GDP grew 2.8% year-on-year in the first quarter of 2026 — the slowest pace since Q1 2021.

This article breaks down what's behind the slowdown and what companies considering the Philippines should keep in mind.

## The actual Q1 2026 GDP figure

According to the PSA, real GDP growth came in at 2.8% year-on-year in Q1 2026, down from 3.0% in the prior quarter (Q4 2025) — the weakest pace in roughly five years, since Q1 2021.

<div class="space-y-6 my-8">
  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">1. Well below the government's target</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      The Philippine government had set a full-year 2026 growth target of 5.0-6.0%. The Q1 result fell well short of that range.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">2. Below market expectations too</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      Local economists' median forecast had been 3.4%, so the actual figure missed that as well.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">3. PSA calls it a 5-year low</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      PSA National Statistician Claire Dennis Mapa noted this is the slowest growth rate since Q1 2021, when the economy contracted 3.8% amid the pandemic.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

## What's driving the slowdown

Socioeconomic Planning Secretary Arsenio Balisacan pointed to several factors converging at once.

<div class="grid grid-cols-1 md:grid-cols-2 gap-6 my-12">
  <div class="bg-white border-2 border-gray-200 rounded-lg p-6 shadow-sm">
    <div class="text-3xl mb-3">🏗️</div>
    <h4 class="text-base font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Weak public investment</h4>
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-700 mb-4">
      Gross fixed capital formation declined 3.3%. Fallout from the flood control budget controversy and delays in passing the 2026 national budget held back public works execution.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="bg-white border-2 border-gray-200 rounded-lg p-6 shadow-sm">
    <div class="text-3xl mb-3">🛒</div>
    <h4 class="text-base font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Soft domestic demand</h4>
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-700 mb-4">
      Household final consumption grew 3.0% year-on-year — still positive, but not enough to drive stronger overall growth.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="bg-white border-2 border-gray-200 rounded-lg p-6 shadow-sm">
    <div class="text-3xl mb-3">🛢️</div>
    <h4 class="text-base font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Middle East tensions pushing up energy prices</h4>
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-700 mb-4">
      Rising oil prices and supply chain pressure tied to escalating Middle East tensions were also cited as an external drag on growth.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="bg-white border-2 border-gray-200 rounded-lg p-6 shadow-sm">
    <div class="text-3xl mb-3">🏭</div>
    <h4 class="text-base font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Agriculture and industry both contracted</h4>
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-700 mb-4">
      Agriculture, forestry, and fishing fell 0.2% year-on-year, and industry fell 0.1%. Services, by contrast, grew 4.5% and remained the main pillar of growth.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

## Regional performance isn't uniform

**This 2.8% figure is a national average, and conditions vary significantly by region.** As we covered in an earlier article on [Metro Manila's minimum wage increase](/en/articles/manila-ncr-minimum-wage-2026/), average household income sits at ₱513,520 in Metro Manila versus ₱375,240 in Central Luzon — which includes Clark. National indicators are a useful starting point for reading the macro environment, but if you're evaluating a specific area like Clark, it's worth checking regional data too.

---

## What this means for Japanese companies

**Reading the slowdown alone as "the Philippines has lost its appeal" would be premature.** Services grew 4.5% and household consumption stayed positive at 3.0% — domestic demand itself hasn't collapsed. The main driver of the slowdown was a public-spending execution bottleneck, not a deterioration in the Philippines' underlying fundamentals (demographics, English-language talent pool, BPO industry competitiveness, and so on).

That said, if your business plan depends on the timing of government-led infrastructure or public works projects (construction, infrastructure-linked contracts, etc.), it's worth building in some buffer for execution delays. Given the current sensitivity to external factors like oil prices and supply chains, it's also worth continuing to track these alongside currency and inflation trends.
