# Philippine vehicle sales fall 10.2% as xEVs jump 136%

## Introduction

The Philippine automotive market is contracting, yet electrified vehicles are growing rapidly. Is that a temporary reaction to fuel prices or a structural change?

January-July 2026 sales reported by the Chamber of Automotive Manufacturers of the Philippines (CAMPI) and Truck Manufacturers Association (TMA) fell 10.2% year on year to 241,725 units. Electrified vehicles, or xEVs, increased 136.4% to 38,286 and represented 15.84% of member sales, up from 6.02% a year earlier.

xEV is an umbrella category covering non-plug-in hybrid electric vehicles (HEVs), battery electric vehicles (BEVs) and plug-in hybrids (PHEVs). In plain terms, fewer vehicles are being sold overall, but the mix of what people buy is changing quickly.

Scope matters. Total July industry sales, including estimates for non-member brands, were reported at 42,880. Detailed xEV numbers cover CAMPI-TMA members. Combining those different scopes would distort market share, so the percentages above use member sales for both numerator and denominator.

## HEV, BEV and PHEV mix

HEVs led January-July volume with 20,716 units, followed by 10,476 BEVs and 7,094 PHEVs. PHEVs posted the largest percentage increase, while BEVs passed 10,000 units.

HEV leadership suggests buyers want lower fuel use without depending on public charging. BEVs and PHEVs together nevertheless reached 17,570 units, increasing the need for charging and after-sales support at dealerships, workplaces, condominiums and commercial sites.

## Did July mark a bottom?

CAMPI-TMA members sold 37,319 vehicles in July, 0.6% above June's 37,079 but about 3% below July 2025. xEV sales reached 7,086, up 162% year on year.

One monthly gain does not establish a recovery. Interest rates, the peso, fuel prices, inventory, new models and loan approvals need to be monitored over several months.

## The ASEAN context

The International Energy Agency reports that Southeast Asian electric-car sales more than doubled in 2025 to nearly one in five new cars. Viet Nam, Indonesia and Thailand led, while Malaysia and the Philippines had relatively lower electric-car shares.

Definitions need care: the Philippine xEV series includes HEVs, while the IEA's electric-car measure focuses mainly on BEVs and PHEVs. The Philippine pattern is a two-layer transition—HEVs as the largest entry route, with BEVs and PHEVs following.

## Separate sales from local production

Executive Order No. 121 introduced the Electric Vehicle Incentive Strategy (EVIS) in July 2026. It has a maximum PHP60 billion support ceiling and requires at least PHP5 billion in new investment and market launch within three years of registration, among other conditions.

Imported xEV sales do not automatically deepen local supply chains. Conversely, an EVIS-registered factory will not guarantee demand if affordability, financing, charging and service remain weak.

## Seven diligence items for companies

1. Separate HEVs, BEVs, PHEVs and commercial vehicles.
2. Do not mix member sales, non-member estimates and registrations.
3. Split private, corporate-fleet, logistics and public-transport use cases.
4. Design charging, parts, technicians, diagnostics, warranties and residual value.
5. Test EVIS investment, model, timing and Tax Payment Certificate conditions.
6. Map opportunities in batteries, motors, inverters, thermal systems, harnesses, chargers and software.
7. Run demand scenarios for fuel prices, interest rates and peso depreciation.
