# Philippine ports handle 154M tons in H1 2026

## Introduction

Philippine port volumes are growing, but higher throughput does not automatically mean that ports are becoming more efficient.

The Philippine Ports Authority, or PPA, reported **154.03 million metric tons** of cargo in the first half of 2026, up 3.08% year on year. Container traffic rose 2.65% to **4.30 million TEUs**, while PPA revenue increased 11.31% to ₱16.34 billion.

In practical terms, activity and the authority's capacity to reinvest are increasing. But the national totals do not reveal customs time, terminal queues, storage costs or congestion at the specific port a company will use.

Revenue growth reflects more than volume. PPA has cited storage income, tariff adjustments and gains from dollar-denominated tariffs among the drivers. The authority exceeded its first-half revenue target by 7.61%, or ₱1.16 billion. That may support investment capacity, but it does not mean shippers' logistics costs rose at the same rate.

## Imports outpace exports

Of the 4.30 million TEUs handled in the first half, domestic containers accounted for 1.497 million and foreign containers for 2.803 million. Foreign imports reached 1.428 million TEUs while exports reached 1.375 million.

Import growth was faster than export growth. TEUs measure container capacity, however, not cargo value or weight, and may include empty containers. Trade competitiveness still needs to be assessed with PSA product-level trade data.

## Concentration in Luzon and Manila

Luzon ports handled 76.3% of first-half containers, or 3.281 million TEUs. Manila International Container Terminal accounted for 1.55 million TEUs, 36% of the national total, followed by South Harbor with 742,514 and North Harbor with 685,339.

Manila's scale supports route frequency and service-provider depth, but concentration can expose companies to road congestion, gate queues and storage deadlines. A Clark-based company should compare Manila with Subic, Batangas and Clark air cargo by product and shipping lane, not assume one gateway fits every flow.

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8"><h3 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Know what the dataset covers</h3><p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed">These figures describe the PPA-managed network. They do not directly measure congestion or customs speed, and Clark air cargo is not part of maritime TEUs. Sea and air options require separate operating metrics.</p></div>

## The 2024–2026 direction

PPA reported 289.41 million tons of cargo in 2024 and 308.5 million tons in 2025, a 6.6% increase. First-half 2026 volume reached 154.03 million tons. Doubling a half-year number would produce 308.06 million tons, but seasonality makes that unsuitable as a full-year forecast.

PPA revenue reached a record ₱30.09 billion in 2025 and ₱16.34 billion in the first half of 2026. Both volume and revenue momentum remain positive, while service quality still needs separate evidence.

## What this means for Japanese companies

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8"><h3 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Who should pay attention</h3><p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed">Manufacturers dependent on imported inputs, food and retail companies, machinery and construction suppliers, e-commerce operators, 3PLs and warehouse providers should monitor the trend. Higher activity can support market demand, but it also makes lead-time buffers, storage charges, demurrage and trucking capacity more important.</p></div>

Compare these items for each candidate port:

1. direct-sailing and transshipment frequency;
2. actual time from vessel arrival to cargo release;
3. customs, inspection and bonded-warehouse capacity;
4. trucking time, restrictions and alternative routes;
5. freight, terminal, storage and demurrage charges; and
6. contingency routes through Subic, Batangas or air cargo.
