# OFW Remittances Hit a Record $35.6B, But Growth Slows to a 4-Year Low

## Introduction

As we covered in our [look at the Philippines' GDP composition](/en/articles/philippines-worldbank-growth-outlook-2026/), the Philippine economy is domestic-demand-driven, with household consumption accounting for over 70% of GDP. One of the biggest pillars propping up that consumption is remittances from overseas Filipino workers (OFWs). Full-year 2025 remittances hit a fresh record, but growth has visibly slowed since the start of 2026.

This article covers the latest remittance trends and what they mean for consumer demand — a point worth tracking for companies operating in the Philippines.

## 2025 Hit a Record $35.6 Billion, Equal to 7.3% of GDP

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Also 6.4% of GNI</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    According to the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP), full-year 2025 cash remittances from OFWs hit a record $35.63 billion — equal to 7.3% of GDP and 6.4% of Gross National Income (GNI). BSP is forecasting 3% growth for 2026, to $36.6 billion.
  </p>
</div>

---

## But May 2026 Growth Was Just 2% — the Slowest Pace in Four Years

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Absolute levels remain solid, but the growth pace is cooling</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    Cumulative remittances for January-May 2026 reached $14.11 billion, up 2.5% from $13.766 billion in the same period of 2025 — a solid absolute level. But in May alone, remittances grew just 2% year-on-year to $2.713 billion, the slowest pace of growth in four years. Contributing factors cited include rising living costs abroad, a sluggish global economy, and the lingering shadow of Middle East tensions. This isn't a collapse in remittances — it's a slowdown in the growth rate — but it signals a shift away from the pace seen through 2025 that's worth keeping an eye on.
  </p>
</div>

---

## The Top Sources Are the US, Singapore, and Saudi Arabia

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">The US leads, followed by Singapore and Saudi Arabia</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    By country, the United States remains the largest source of remittances, followed by Singapore and Saudi Arabia. That reflects two distinct groups of OFWs — professional and IT talent in North America, and frontline workers across the Middle East and Singapore — both contributing to the flow. This diversification across regions and job types is one factor supporting the overall resilience of remittances, reducing dependence on any single country's economic conditions.
  </p>
</div>

---

## Remittances Feed Directly Into Consumption, Which Drives Over 70% of GDP

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">More likely to go toward daily expenses than savings or investment</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    As [previously covered](/en/articles/philippines-worldbank-growth-outlook-2026/), household consumption accounts for 72.6% of Philippine GDP — a domestic-demand-driven structure. OFW remittances tend to be spent on everyday needs — food, education, healthcare — rather than saved or invested, meaning household consumption is heavily reliant on this inflow of income from abroad. Most economists expect a moderation in consumption rather than a collapse, but combined with [inflation, which BSP projects will climb to 6.4% in 2026](/en/articles/philippines-h2-growth-target-2026/), the headwinds facing consumer spending are starting to stack up.
  </p>
</div>

## Why This Matters for Companies

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">A leading indicator for consumer goods, retail, and e-commerce</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    For companies in consumer goods, retail, or e-commerce, remittance trends are a meaningful leading indicator of real purchasing power. While the absolute remittance total keeps setting records, the slowing growth rate suggests the pace of consumption growth seen in recent years may moderate going forward. When building demand forecasts or pricing strategy, it's worth looking not just at the absolute size of remittances, but at the momentum — how the growth rate itself is trending.
  </p>
</div>
