# PEZA approvals double to PHP216.5bn in eight months

## Introduction

Do rapidly rising economic-zone approvals tell the same story as weak realised foreign direct investment?

The Philippine Economic Zone Authority (PEZA), which registers and administers export-manufacturing, IT-BPM and other economic-zone projects, approved PHP216.466 billion in planned investment from January through August 2026. That was 104.53% above the PHP105.834 billion approved a year earlier and equal to 72.16% of its PHP300 billion annual target.

The critical distinction is that an approval is a project commitment, not money already spent or a factory already operating. This article separates momentum in the pipeline from the steps required to turn it into production, exports and jobs.

In plain language, the value increase came from larger projects rather than a doubling in the number of investors. Thirty-four big-ticket approvals totalled PHP193.713 billion, or approximately 89.5% of the eight-month total. This concentration also means that a delay or scale reduction in a small number of projects could materially affect realised investment.

The remaining gap is PHP83.534 billion. Dividing that evenly across four months gives PHP20.884 billion per month, but PEZA Board approvals are lumpy and depend on the timing of large projects. This is a pacing benchmark, not a forecast.

August alone contributed 22 projects worth PHP64.565 billion, 334.13% above PHP14.872 billion in August 2025. Nine big-ticket August projects totalled PHP62.05 billion, including two facilities projects in Tarlac worth a combined PHP35.39 billion and four ecozone developments in Cavite, Bataan, Davao del Sur and Cebu worth PHP22.39 billion.

By location, 161 projects are planned for Luzon (82.1%), 23 for the Visayas (11.7%) and 12 for Mindanao (6.1%). The project count therefore remains concentrated in the broad Luzon industrial corridor.

The Philippines was the largest investment source, followed by the Netherlands, South Korea, Singapore and Taiwan. Japan was not in this top-five list. That says more about the composition of current approvals than the large installed base of Japanese companies already operating in the country.

PEZA separately reported first-half actual ecozone exports of USD32.89 billion, up 2.35%, and direct employment of 1.82 million, up 1.56%. Those figures cover operating ecozones and should not be confused with the projections attached to the 196 newly approved projects.

The Philippine Statistics Authority's Approved Foreign Investments series also has different coverage: it aggregates the foreign-capital portion approved by multiple investment-promotion agencies. It is not directly comparable with PEZA's total approvals, which can include Philippine capital.

## Five items for investors and suppliers

1. Track groundbreaking and equipment orders for the large Tarlac facilities and new ecozones.
2. Map demand from the 80 manufacturing projects in automation, components, maintenance, quality and logistics.
3. Identify sector and customer relationships among Korean, Taiwanese, Dutch and Singaporean entrants.
4. Compare technical labour, wages and commuting catchments in Calabarzon and Central Luzon.
5. Confirm that incentives under PEZA and CREATE MORE require qualifying activities, registration and continuing compliance.
