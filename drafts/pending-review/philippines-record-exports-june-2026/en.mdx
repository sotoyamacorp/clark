# Philippine Exports Hit Record $8.77B in June, Led by Electronics

## Introduction

The Philippines is often described as a consumption-led economy, but its export mix matters greatly to manufacturers evaluating the country. Merchandise exports reached $8.77 billion in June 2026, the highest monthly value since the statistical series began in 1991. Imports also climbed, however, leaving a sizeable deficit.

## Exports and Total Trade Set Records

According to the Philippine Statistics Authority, June merchandise exports rose 24.1% from $7.07 billion a year earlier. Imports reached $13.71 billion, taking total goods trade to a record $22.48 billion.

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8"><h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Composition matters more than the headline alone</h4><p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed">A monthly record is a strong signal, but not proof of a lasting trend. Investors should track seasonally adjusted figures, orders, imported inputs, and demand in major export markets before treating the result as a new baseline.</p></div>

## Electronics Accounted for 59.9%

Electronic products generated $5.25 billion, or 59.9% of exports. Semiconductors and components benefited from demand linked to AI, data centers, and communications equipment.

The Philippines is particularly established in assembly, packaging, and testing. Clark's own electronics cluster connects to this national value chain, so nationwide export momentum is relevant to suppliers assessing demand around the Freeport.

The government and industry are targeting $110 billion in electronics exports by 2030, with a push into IC design, advanced packaging, and other higher-value functions.

## A $4.94 Billion Trade Deficit Remained

Imports exceeded record exports, widening the June goods deficit by 12.3% year on year to $4.94 billion.

<div class="border-l-4 border-navy-600 bg-navy-50 p-6 shadow-sm my-8"><h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Higher imports are not automatically a sign of weakness</h4><p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed">Imports of components, machinery, and other inputs can precede export-production growth. They also expose operators to exchange-rate, logistics, and energy-cost risks. Both demand and input costs belong in the same investment model.</p></div>

## What It Means for Japanese Companies

The opportunity extends beyond finished-product manufacturers. Japanese firms are strong in inspection equipment, precision components, chemicals, factory automation, quality assurance, calibration, and maintenance.

Semiconductor demand is cyclical, so a single customer's expansion should not carry the entire business case. Test access to multiple customers, imported-input lead times, electricity and logistics costs, and incentive eligibility before committing fixed costs.
