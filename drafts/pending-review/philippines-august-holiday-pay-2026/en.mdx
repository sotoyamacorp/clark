# Philippine August Holiday Pay: 260% When August 31 Is a Rest Day

## Introduction

The Philippines observes Ninoy Aquino Day on August 21 and National Heroes Day on August 31, 2026. Both are holidays, but payroll treatment differs because the first is a special non-working day and the second is a regular holiday.

This article translates DOLE Labor Advisory No. 13-26 and the basic Labor Code rules into a payroll checklist for employers.

## Separate “special” from “regular” first

In plain English, a special non-working day generally follows “no work, no pay,” while a covered employee generally receives holiday pay on an unworked regular holiday.

  <tbody>
    <tr class="border-b border-navy-100 bg-white"><td class="p-3 font-semibold">August 21</td><td class="p-3">Special non-working day</td><td class="p-3">Generally 0%</td><td class="p-3">130%</td><td class="p-3">150%</td></tr>
    <tr class="border-b border-navy-100 bg-navy-50"><td class="p-3 font-semibold">August 31</td><td class="p-3">Regular holiday</td><td class="p-3">Generally 100%</td><td class="p-3">200%</td><td class="p-3">260%</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table></div>

These are percentages of the regular daily wage. A collective agreement, employment contract or established company practice may provide more favorable terms.

## Example using a PHP755 daily wage

PHP755—the NCR non-agricultural minimum daily rate effective July 25, 2026—is used only as an illustration. Use the employee's actual regular daily wage.

The 260% rate is 200% regular-holiday pay multiplied by the 130% rest-day factor.

## Work beyond eight hours

For overtime, first establish the hourly rate for the first eight hours on that holiday/rest-day combination. Each overtime hour then carries an additional 30% of that hourly rate.

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8"><h3 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Payroll-system risk</h3><p class="text-gray-700">Do not apply a single 130% factor directly to the ordinary hourly wage. The system must first include the applicable holiday and rest-day rate, then add the overtime premium.</p></div>

## Preceding-day and monthly-pay checks

The 100% unworked regular-holiday entitlement is affected by whether the employee worked or was on paid leave on the preceding workday. If the immediately preceding day was a company non-working day or the employee's rest day, review the prior scheduled workday.

For monthly-paid staff, the treatment depends on the annual divisor and whether regular holidays are already included in monthly pay. “Monthly paid” does not mean excluded from holiday-pay protection, but payroll should also avoid paying the same unworked-holiday component twice.

Potential exclusions—including managerial employees, field personnel, domestic workers and certain small retail/service establishments—require individual legal classification. Do not batch-classify employees without a documented basis.

## Seven pre-close checks

1. Correct holiday type in the payroll calendar
2. Employee rest-day overlap
3. Overtime and night differential calculated separately
4. Preceding-workday attendance and leave status
5. Monthly-pay divisor and embedded holiday pay
6. More favorable CBA or company practice
7. Documented basis for any exclusion

August 31 falls close to month-end, so early payroll cutoffs require advance configuration.</p></div>
