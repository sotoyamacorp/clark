# Clark seeks waste-to-resource partners for 1,285 locators

## Introduction

As Clark's investment and visitor economy grows, the Freeport says it needs more companies that can turn waste into usable resources.

Clark Development Corporation (CDC), the administrator and investment-promotion authority for Clark Freeport, said in August 2026 that it is expanding partnerships with technology and service providers for food and industrial waste. This was a statement of direction, not a tender notice. This article separates the opportunity from the unconfirmed commercial details and maps the permits a prospective operator must check.

## What CDC actually announced

CDC President and CEO Agnes Devanadera said at an Eco-Governance Summit that Clark needs more firms capable of turning industrial waste into resources. She pointed to Clark-based SRCI Manufacturing Inc., which recovers usable material from electronic waste, as an operating example.

She also identified food waste as an emerging issue as Clark's hotels and restaurants expand. CDC wants technology and service partnerships that can support this larger economic ecosystem.

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8"><h3 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Direction is not procurement</h3><p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed">The August 24 announcement did not state a project value, waste volume, contract term, bid deadline or procurement package. Companies should treat it as a demand signal and validate specific opportunities directly with CDC and accredited local operators.</p></div>

## The economic base behind the demand

As of December 31, 2025, Clark Freeport hosted 1,285 locator companies with 151,593 workers and PHP372 billion in actual investments. First-quarter 2026 exports reached USD1.48 billion. In 2025, Clark recorded 1.51 million overnight guests and 29.75 million same-day visitors.

## Different waste streams, different requirements

In plain language, a general waste-hauling permit does not automatically authorize a company to handle every waste stream.

RA 9003 prioritizes source segregation, reuse, recycling and resource recovery. Hazardous waste follows a separate chain under RA 6969 and DAO 2013-22, including generator registration, authorized transport and transfer to a registered Treatment, Storage and Disposal (TSD) facility.

## What is different in Clark

Outside special zones, companies generally coordinate with the relevant local government and DENR-EMB. Inside Clark Freeport, operators must also check CDC environmental guidelines and its lists of accredited residual-waste, hazardous-waste transport and treatment providers.

This national-regulation-plus-CDC layer is the practical Clark difference. Technology suppliers that also plan to collect, transport or treat waste should map permissions for each activity rather than assuming one approval covers the full chain.

## Six diligence questions for market entry

1. Measure monthly volume and seasonality by factory, hotel and commercial source.
2. Model collection, sorting, utilities, residual disposal and recovered-material revenue together.
3. Secure an offtaker and quality standard for compost, recovered metals or other outputs.
4. Map CDC, DENR-EMB and, where relevant, local-government approvals by activity.
5. Build auditable measurement, recovery-rate, destination and emissions data for customers' ESG reporting.
6. Define responsibilities with locally accredited transporters and treatment facilities.
