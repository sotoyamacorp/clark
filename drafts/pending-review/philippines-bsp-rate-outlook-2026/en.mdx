# BSP's August 27 Meeting: Rate Hold Now Expected, Reversing Prior Hike Call

## Introduction

Following [BSP's (the Philippine central bank's) pivot from rate cuts to hikes, which we covered in late July](/en/articles/philippines-peso-inflation-bsp-2026/), market expectations for the next policy meeting on August 27 have shifted. Standard Chartered has revised its forecast from an additional hike to a hold. This article covers what's behind that shift, along with the growth and inflation forecasts released alongside it.

## Standard Chartered Revised Its August 27 Call From "Hike" to "Hold"

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">The current policy rate is 4.75%, after a 25bp hike at the June 18 meeting</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    Standard Chartered's Jonathan Koh and Edward Lee now expect BSP (Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas) to hold its policy rate at the August 27 meeting, reversing their earlier call for an additional hike. BSP raised its policy rate (the reverse repurchase rate) by 25 basis points to 4.75% at its June 18, 2026 meeting. As [we covered in late July, BSP had pivoted from a cutting cycle to a hiking one](/en/articles/philippines-peso-inflation-bsp-2026/) — this latest revision from a private bank is a fresh sign that hiking path may be pausing, at least for now.
  </p>
</div>

---

## 2026 GDP Growth Forecast Cut From 4.0% to 3.5%

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">A revision reflecting the H1 growth slowdown</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    Standard Chartered cut its full-year 2026 GDP growth forecast from 4.0% to 3.5%. That's broadly in line with [other major institutions' forecasts (3-3.9%), which we covered earlier](/en/articles/philippines-analysts-growth-forecast-2026/), and reflects first-half (January-June) growth coming in below market expectations. The figure lands right at the floor of the government's target range (3.5-4.5%), suggesting private-sector views are converging on a fairly demanding downside scenario relative to that target.
  </p>
</div>

---

## 2026 Inflation Forecast Cut From 6.5% to 5.9%

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Actual inflation has run lower than expected so far</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    At the same time, the inflation outlook was revised down from 6.5% to 5.9%. Given [July inflation came in at 6.2% (down from June's 6.4%), as we covered earlier](/en/articles/philippines-rice-tariff-inflation-2026/), actual inflation running somewhat below prior expectations appears to be behind this downward revision. A lower growth forecast paired with a lower inflation forecast means BSP's "reason to keep tightening" (high inflation) has weakened somewhat, while its "reason to ease up" (a slowing economy) has grown stronger — and that combination is exactly what's driving the shift from "hike" to "hold."
  </p>
</div>

---

## Rate Cuts Are Now Projected for Q2-Q3 2027, but the Tone Stays Hawkish

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Even holding rates, BSP's rhetoric is expected to stay tight</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    Standard Chartered also lowered its policy rate forecasts — to 4.75% (from 5%) for end-2026, and 4.25% (from 4.5%) for end-2027. It expects the actual pivot to cuts to wait until inflation is projected to fall below 4% in Q2 2027, with 25bp cuts then following in both Q2 and Q3 of that year. Notably, though, the bank's analysis suggests that even if BSP holds rates at the August meeting, its rhetoric and tone will likely remain hawkish (tightening-oriented). In other words: rates may not move, but the stance won't loosen — a combination that could persist for a while.
  </p>
</div>

## Why This Matters for Companies

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Rate and FX assumptions shouldn't be treated as fixed — plan for multiple scenarios</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    For companies weighing financing or local operations in the Philippines, the fact that the rate outlook itself has swung this quickly is important information in its own right. Rather than locking in plans around a simple "BSP has pivoted to hiking" narrative, it's worth scenario-planning around the possibility of either a hold or a further hike, depending on incoming data. The actual outcome on August 27 will directly shape assumptions about the peso and borrowing costs going forward, making it worth watching closely whether you're already operating in the Philippines or still evaluating entry.
  </p>
</div>
