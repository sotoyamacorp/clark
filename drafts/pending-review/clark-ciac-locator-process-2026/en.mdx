# Six steps to become a locator in Clark Aviation Capital

## Introduction

Setting up a factory, MRO facility, logistics base or office around Clark International Airport involves more than leasing a site. Companies must distinguish the land and lease process of Clark International Airport Corporation (CIAC) from registration and permits administered by Clark Development Corporation (CDC) and other agencies.

## CIAC and CDC have different roles

CIAC is the government corporation responsible for developing and managing the Clark Civil Aviation Complex. CDC administers the Clark Freeport and Special Economic Zone, including locator registration, business regulation and application forms. A company should therefore plan for both interfaces rather than treating “Clark approval” as one document.

| Body | Main role | What investors assess |
|---|---|---|
| CIAC | Aviation-complex land, proposal evaluation and lease | Site, use, lease terms and development plan |
| CDC | Economic-zone registration and regulation | Registration, construction and operating permits |
| Other agencies | Environmental, fire and sanitary matters | Project-specific clearances and certificates |

## The six-stage process

### 1. Submit a letter of intent

The letter should identify the company, proposed activity, site requirement and investment. A useful submission quantifies floor area, infrastructure needs and logistics flows instead of merely stating an interest in Clark.

### 2. Provide business and corporate documents

CIAC evaluates the proponent's legal standing, business proposal, financial capacity and technical capability. A Japanese parent should prepare a consistent English explanation of ownership, funding, project experience, decision makers and the timetable for establishing the Philippine entity.

### 3. Negotiate the term sheet

The parties discuss the site, rent, term and development obligations. Compare total occupancy cost, including site preparation, utility capacity, construction-period charges, road access and reinstatement—not headline rent alone.

### 4. Execute the lease

After terms are agreed, the parties proceed to the lease agreement. Before signing, reconcile the contract with internal approvals and address what happens if a permit required for the intended operation cannot be obtained.

### 5. Complete CDC registration and permits

CIAC's guide states that it endorses the locator to CDC after the lease. The following stage may include an environmental compliance certificate, fire, occupancy and sanitary permits, and authority to operate. Requirements vary by activity and building, so obtain a project-specific matrix.

### 6. Build and commence operations

The company then moves through construction, equipment installation, recruitment, commissioning and operations. CIAC's page shows sequence, not a guaranteed standard duration. Design changes, utilities and permits should be managed as separate critical-path items.

## Investor checklist

1. Check ownership structure and foreign-investment restrictions.
2. Quantify land, power, water, telecommunications and access needs.
3. Prepare a five-year investment, employment and operating plan.
4. Put the CIAC lease, CDC registration and construction permits on separate tracks.
5. Confirm incentive eligibility in writing based on the proposed activity.
6. Budget for permit delays and maintain a fallback schedule.

For aviation, logistics and manufacturing projects, airport proximity is only one variable. Bonded operations, hazardous materials, night access, power quality and availability of skilled labor can determine operating cost. A site visit should follow the actual inbound and outbound route and inspect utility connection points.
