# BSP rate reaches 5%: borrowing, FX and pricing implications

## Introduction

Companies funding Philippine inventory, receivables and capital expenditure need to translate a central-bank headline into their own cash flow.

On August 27, 2026, the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) raised its target reverse repurchase rate—the benchmark that signals its monetary-policy stance—by 25 basis points to 5.00%. It was the third consecutive hike after moves in April and June.

In plain terms, the BSP is making economy-wide financing conditions tighter to prevent high inflation from becoming entrenched. That does not mean every corporate loan rises by exactly 25 basis points on the same day. The result depends on the contractual reference rate, repricing cycle and bank credit spread.

## The 2026 rate path reversed sharply

The policy rate fell from 4.50% at end-2025 to 4.25% in February 2026. As the inflation outlook deteriorated, the BSP raised it by 25 basis points in April, June and August.

The reversal reflected higher oil and non-oil prices linked to Middle East conflict, peso depreciation, agricultural costs, and potential second-round effects through transport fares and wages.

## Inflation eased—but remains too high

Headline inflation slowed from 6.4% in June to 6.2% in July. Core inflation eased from 4.4% to 4.2%. Both remained above the upper end of the BSP's 2-4% target range.

The BSP projected August inflation in a 5.5-6.5% range. Rice, vegetables, fruit, fish and fuel were upside drivers, while meat, electricity and the peso could provide some relief. The government's DBCC assumes average inflation of 6-7% in 2026 and 4-5% in 2027.

## The difficult growth trade-off

Real GDP growth slowed to 2.3% year on year in the second quarter. Lower rates would normally support a weak economy, but additional demand can worsen inflation when prices are already far above target.

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8"><h3 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">The BSP's sequencing</h3><p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed">The decision prioritizes keeping inflation expectations and second-round effects from becoming embedded, which protects real incomes and currency stability. The BSP also expects fiscal support to help growth recover in the second half and said it remains ready to act as needed.</p></div>

The next scheduled policy meeting is October 22, 2026. Inflation, oil, the peso, wages, transport fares and growth data will shape the next decision.

## Interest-cost sensitivity

Policy-rate transmission to bank loans is not one-for-one. A budget can still test 25, 50 and 75 basis-point increases.

In Clark, tax incentives administered by CDC or PEZA are separate from financing conditions. A project may reduce tax expense but still face cash-flow pressure if construction, inventory and receivables are funded with floating-rate peso debt.

## Six actions to take now

1. List every facility by currency, fixed/floating status, benchmark and reset date.
2. Calculate annual interest under +25, +50 and +75 bp scenarios.
3. Quantify the cash released by cutting inventory and receivable days by ten.
4. Review whether price-escalation clauses cover fuel, FX and financing pressure.
5. Compare parent funding, local debt, equity and fixed-rate options on an all-in basis.
6. Update inflation, oil, peso and wage assumptions monthly before the October meeting.

The useful stress test combines rates, FX and inflation rather than treating them separately.</p></div>
