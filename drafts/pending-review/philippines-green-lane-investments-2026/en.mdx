# Philippine Green Lane investments reach PHP351 billion

## Introduction

Permits across national agencies and local governments can become a critical path for large Philippine investments. Created under Executive Order No.18, Green Lanes use the Board of Investments' One-Stop Action Center for Strategic Investments (OSAC-SI) to coordinate and expedite strategic projects.

## H1 2026 results

BOI reported 17 certifications from January to June 2026, with PHP351.02 billion of investment and 39,368 expected jobs. Renewable energy accounted for 14 projects and PHP349.38 billion, or 99.53% of the value. Two food-security projects represented PHP980 million.

Since the initiative began in February 2023, 239 projects worth PHP6.32 trillion and approximately 421,000 expected jobs have been certified. Renewable energy accounts for 183 projects, 76.57% of the total, and 85.68% of certified project cost.

| Indicator | H1 2026 | Since launch |
|---|---:|---:|
| Certified projects | 17 | 239 |
| Project cost | PHP351.02bn | PHP6.32tn |
| Expected jobs | 39,368 | About 421,000 |

The H1 list included a PHP670-million industrial-zone project by Una Grande Tierra Development Corporation in Pampanga, making the program relevant to the industrial ecosystem around Clark and Central Luzon.

## What Green Lane changes

OSAC-SI serves as the single point of entry and assigns coordination for permits across agencies and LGUs. EO18 calls for agency Green Lanes, simultaneous processing and sector investor guidebooks.

It does not remove substantive environmental, land-use, construction or grid requirements. Certification should not be read as final approval of every permit or a guarantee that the announced investment will be completed.

## Eligibility and documents

Strategic investments align with the Philippine Development Plan, SIPP or related national priorities. Cumulative certifications cover renewable energy, digital infrastructure, PPPs, infrastructure and water, food security, manufacturing and pharmaceuticals.

The 2026 BOI guidance identifies these core documents:

1. Green Lane application form
2. CEO-signed letter of intent to the DTI Secretary
3. Company profile
4. Project brief covering technology, jobs, impacts, suppliers, timetable and funding
5. SEC registration and latest GIS
6. List of permits being applied for
7. Secretary's Certificate for the authorized representative
8. Sector documents such as a DOE service contract for renewable energy

## Implications for Japanese investors

Green Lane is most relevant to projects such as large manufacturing, data centers, renewable energy and food value chains that depend on multiple agencies. A small sales subsidiary or ordinary office does not automatically qualify as strategic.

Green Lane certification and investment-incentive registration have different purposes. Manage corporate and foreign-ownership rules, incentive registration, Green Lane, sector licenses and LGU permits as separate decision lines.
