# Philippines weather work suspensions: the 2026 rules

## Introduction

During typhoons and floods, Philippine private employers need a clear answer to three questions: who suspends work, how employees are paid, and when a worker may refuse unsafe work.

The Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) issued Labor Advisory No. 14, Series of 2026 (LA 14-26) on August 19. In plain terms, it organizes the decision, pay and safety rules for weather disturbances and similar occurrences.

This article is a general explanation of official guidance, not legal advice for a specific case. Contracts, collective bargaining agreements, established practices and the cause of a stoppage can change the result.

## A government suspension is not always automatic for private firms

Private-sector suspension does not necessarily follow government-office or school closures automatically. The employer exercises management prerogative in coordination with safety and health personnel.

The advisory identifies PAGASA weather information, NDRRMC disaster advisories and LGU announcements as decision inputs. Companies should also consider employee routes, night shifts, building conditions, industrial operations and transport availability.

    <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 pl-4"><strong>Stoppage caused by employer violation or fault</strong><p class="text-gray-700">Section 23 of RA 11058 contains a wage-payment rule; obtain case-specific advice.</p></div>
  </div>
</div>

The final scenario is an important exception. If imminent-danger stoppage results from an employer's violation or fault, RA 11058 provides for payment to affected workers. Payroll teams should not treat every disruption as an ordinary weather closure.

## Unsafe-work refusal

RA 11058 defines imminent danger and establishes a right to refuse unsafe work in specified circumstances. LA 14-26 states that employees who fail or refuse to work because weather creates imminent danger should not be administratively sanctioned.

Companies should record the location, time and hazard reported; objective alerts; the supervisor and safety officer's assessment; alternatives such as remote work; and the time normal work resumed.

## Clark-specific operating considerations

Clark has strong airport and expressway access, but employees commute from Angeles, Mabalacat, wider Pampanga and Tarlac. The Freeport may be operational while home-to-work routes are flooded or public transport is unavailable.

Factories, BPOs, hotels and logistics centers should separate on-site critical roles from remote-capable work; day and night shift cutoffs; shuttle suspension; safe shutdown of manufacturing or cold-storage equipment; and client-service communications.
