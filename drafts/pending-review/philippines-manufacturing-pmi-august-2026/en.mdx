# Philippine manufacturing PMI hits 54.9, strongest since 2016

## Introduction

The S&P Global Philippines Manufacturing Purchasing Managers' Index rose to **54.9 in August 2026**, from 51.8 in July. It was the highest reading since December 2016 and the fourth consecutive month above 50.

The breadth matters. New orders accelerated, export orders rose for the first time in six months and employment increased for the first time in five months. This is a useful early demand signal, but PMI is a business survey rather than an official measure of production value or volume.

The reversals in export orders and employment suggest broader improvement. Slower increases in input and selling prices may support margins, but companies still need to check their own foreign-exchange, power, raw-material and freight costs.

## Regional context

Japan's August manufacturing PMI was also 54.9, while Malaysia registered 49.7 and China's official PMI 49.8. The Philippine reading was strong in that context.

The surveys are not perfectly comparable: China uses an official survey, while S&P Global compiles the Philippines, Japan and Malaysia series, with different samples and industry weights. Treat them as directional context, not a definitive ranking.

## Do not use PMI alone

PMI converts monthly purchasing-manager responses into a diffusion index. Above 50 means improvement was more prevalent than deterioration; it does not mean output grew by 54.9%.

The Philippine Statistics Authority is scheduled to release August production, sales and capacity-use data on September 4. Confirmation in actual values and volumes would increase confidence in the survey signal.

## What it means for businesses

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8"><h3 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Who should pay attention</h3><p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed">Electronics, food, chemicals, packaging and logistics suppliers—and companies evaluating a Philippine factory—should pay attention. The simultaneous improvement in orders, exports and jobs supports a firmer demand case. Capacity should still be expanded in stages after checking official statistics and company order books.</p></div>

Management teams should ask:

1. Do order backlogs and inquiries match the PMI direction?
2. Is growth coming from export or domestic customers?
3. Have inventory days and component lead times changed?
4. Are hiring fill rates, overtime and wages tightening?
5. Do the September 4 official data confirm volume and value growth?
