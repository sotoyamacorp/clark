# Philippine retail power threshold falls to 100 kW

## Introduction

The Philippines is progressively expanding the ability of larger customers to choose a Retail Electricity Supplier (RES) instead of relying only on regulated distribution-utility supply. On June 26, 2026, the eligibility threshold for Retail Competition and Open Access (RCOA) and the Retail Aggregation Program (RAP) fell to 100 kW, opening the option to more factories, hotels, schools, hospitals and offices.

Why do two separate pricing tracks — regulated rates and contestable-market contracts — exist in the first place? The answer goes back to the Electric Power Industry Reform Act (EPIRA) of 2001, which unbundled generation, transmission, distribution and supply and set a path to gradually introduce competition into the Philippine power sector. Under EPIRA, customers whose average monthly peak demand exceeds a set threshold qualify as a "contestable customer" — an end-user eligible to contract directly with an RES instead of buying only from its distribution utility at the regulated rate. The threshold started at 1 megawatt (1,000 kW) and the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) has lowered it in stages as the market matured. The move to 100 kW is simply the latest step in that long-running liberalization process. Customers below the threshold continue to buy at the distribution utility's regulated rate as before.

## RCOA and RAP

RCOA allows one qualifying customer to contract with an RES. RAP combines demand from multiple sites that would not qualify alone. Meralco's guidance says the RAP threshold was previously 500 kW and fell to 100 kW under ERC Resolution No.22, Series of 2025.

| Program | Typical use | Main checks |
|---|---|---|
| RCOA | A factory or hotel qualifying on its own | Historical peak demand and supply contract |
| RAP | Multiple smaller sites or facilities | Combined demand, contiguity, franchise area and ownership |

ERC reported 77 contestable customers—58 in Luzon and 19 in the Visayas—and 11 aggregated groups at the initial stage of the new threshold. Participation still requires customer-specific eligibility and switching work.

## RAP conditions

For the Meralco area, the FAQ describes at least two end-users in a contiguous area within the same franchise area that belong to the same owner or business franchise. Aggregated average monthly peak demand over the preceding three months must meet the threshold.

Conditions should be confirmed for the relevant distribution area. The customer submits a letter of intent to its chosen RES, which then processes the request with the distribution utility.

## How switching actually works, step by step

Neither RCOA nor RAP is a process the customer completes alone — it involves the chosen RES and the distribution utility at different stages. The typical sequence is:

1. **Customer**: Review 12 months of bills and peak-demand data to confirm eligibility against the 100 kW threshold.
2. **Customer**: Request quotes from multiple RES providers and compare terms.
3. **Customer to distribution utility**: Submit a Letter of Intent (LOI) signaling the switch.
4. **Customer and RES**: Sign a Retail Supply Contract (RSC).
5. **RES to distribution utility**: Coordinate the metering setup and other technical requirements for the switch.
6. **Distribution utility and ERC**: Approve the switch and reflect the change of supplier.

As a general guide, the process takes roughly 90 days from LOI submission to completion, and power supply is not interrupted during the transition. For RAP, customers must also confirm upfront that the sites being aggregated sit within the same franchise area and a contiguous location, among other conditions.

## A simple worked example (illustrative only)

Numbers make the comparison easier to picture, so here is an illustrative example built on assumed figures. **This is not a real company, and the rates shown are not a guarantee of actual pricing or savings — it is a simplified exercise to show how the comparison works, not investment guidance.**

Assumed scenario: a food-processing company ("Company A", a hypothetical name) in Pampanga with an average peak demand of 150 kW and an assumed monthly consumption of 90,000 kWh.

| Item | Regulated rate (distribution utility) | Contestable-market contract (RES, assumed terms) |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly consumption (assumed) | 90,000 kWh | 90,000 kWh |
| Unit rate (assumed figure) | Assume PHP 11.50/kWh | Assume a fixed component of PHP 10.50/kWh |
| Estimated monthly bill (assumed) | Roughly PHP 1,035,000 | Roughly PHP 945,000 (fixed component only; pass-throughs are separate) |
| Price-movement driver | Tracks the distribution utility's regulated rate resets | Fixed component set by contract, plus fuel, FX and other pass-throughs |
| Contract flexibility | Free to stay, no penalty | Contract term and early-termination conditions may apply |

In this assumed scenario the contestable-market unit rate looks lower, but the real comparison has to include pass-through charges, minimum volume commitments, early-termination terms, and upfront metering or deposit costs. A lower quoted unit rate does not automatically mean a lower total cost — which is exactly what the next section, on what to compare, addresses.

## What to compare

Do not decide on the quoted PHP/kWh alone. Compare:

1. Fixed and market-linked price components
2. Fuel, foreign-exchange, transmission and other pass-throughs
3. Contract term, renewal and termination
4. Minimum volume and demand tolerances
5. Metering, switching and deposit costs
6. Renewable attributes or certificates
7. Billing, outage support and service standards

Twelve months of interval or time-of-use data provides a better basis than annual consumption alone. Suppliers may price the same annual volume differently depending on the load shape.

## Practical steps for investors

1. Obtain 12 months of bills and peak-demand data.
2. Assess stand-alone RCOA versus multi-site RAP.
3. Review notice and termination terms in the current contract.
4. Request comparable offers from multiple RES providers.
5. Model base, high and low consumption scenarios.
6. Have legal, finance and facility teams review the contract.

The choice can be material for manufacturing, cold storage, logistics, hotels, BPO and hospitals. New projects should include both regulated supply and contestable-market options in their sensitivity analysis.
