# Pax Silica Community Consultation: ESG Due Diligence

## Introduction

The proposed Pax Silica hub in New Clark City is now facing questions from Aeta Indigenous residents and farmers about ancestral land, farms, and water. The Bases Conversion and Development Authority (BCDA) says the site sits within buildable zones in the existing master plan and that the project will not displace farmers or Indigenous People.

Neither position should be presented as a settled outcome. The government says the plan is not final and that consultation and field assessment continue. Companies linked to the project should therefore examine land rights, community participation, environmental and water impacts, and grievance mechanisms before committing capital.

Officials say initial consultations have taken place with local governments and agencies. In August, Aeta leaders and farmers publicly raised objections, while Senator Francis Pangilinan called for genuine public consultation. Holding a briefing is not the same as completing a process in which affected people understand the project, participate, and provide any legally required consent.

For investors, this is more than a communications issue. It can affect permitting, construction schedules, lender ESG (Environmental, Social, and Governance) review, supplier audits, recruitment, and the project's relationship with its host community.

## What FPIC means

Republic Act 8371, the Indigenous Peoples' Rights Act (IPRA), defines Free and Prior Informed Consent (FPIC) as consensus obtained according to customary laws, free from manipulation or coercion, after full disclosure of the activity's intent and scope in a language and process understood by the community.

It would also be wrong to assume that every parcel automatically requires FPIC. Companies must determine whether a parcel overlaps an ancestral domain and whether field investigation, certification, or consent from the National Commission on Indigenous Peoples (NCIP) is required for that specific activity.

## The representation lesson from CADT 025-A

In July 2026, NCIP validated Aeta community representation and governance of benefits under a joint arrangement with Clark Development Corporation (CDC) in CADT 025-A, the Certificate of Ancestral Domain Title (CADT) that covers part of Clark. This does not establish that FPIC for the Pax Silica site has been completed.

It does show why the identity of the people consulted matters. Elders, farmers, actual land users, women, and young people may not hold identical views. Treating agreement with a narrow group as consent from an entire community can create later legal and social conflict.

## Eight checks before investing

1. Parcel coordinates, title, legal encumbrances, and actual land users
2. Overlap with any CADT or other ancestral-domain claim
3. NCIP field investigation, certification, and FPIC requirements
4. Environmental impact assessment, Environmental Compliance Certificate (ECC), water, and discharge plans
5. The affected-person register, consultation records, and languages used
6. Resettlement, livelihood restoration, benefit-sharing, and local jobs
7. An independent grievance channel and deadlines for remedy
8. Lease and procurement exit clauses if consultation or permitting is delayed
