# Philippines Launches National Single Window: What Importers Should Prepare

## Introduction

Customs declarations are only one part of importing into the Philippines. Depending on the product, a company may also need permits from tax, food, agriculture, health, environment, or other regulators. In June 2026, the government placed the National Single Window–Integrated Trade Facilitation Platform into pilot operation to connect these requirements through one digital gateway.

## What the NSW Is Designed to Fix

The NSW allows traders to submit standardized import, export, and transit information through a single electronic entry point. The goal is not merely to convert paper into PDF files. It is to let government agencies securely reuse data and transmit permit decisions into customs clearance.

| Area | Fragmented process | Target state under NSW |
|---|---|---|
| Submission | Separate application for each agency | One digital entry point |
| Data | Repeated entry of company and shipment details | Secure interagency reuse |
| Status | Separate follow-up with each regulator | Online status visibility |
| Clearance | Waiting for physical or separate permits | Electronic permit exchange |

The Bureau of Customs says most of its own processes are already digital, but paper remains where partner agencies issue permits, licenses, and certificates outside an integrated platform. The NSW targets this interagency gap.

## What Went Live in June 2026

Selected pilot clients began live transactions on June 22. The initial processes are the Bureau of Internal Revenue's Authority to Release Imported Goods and the National Tobacco Administration's Import Commodity Clearance. The government plans to onboard 72 trade-related and other agencies in four phases through 2028.

Legacy systems, separate portals, and paper requirements may coexist during the transition. Companies should confirm the current procedure for each product before shipment.</p></div>

DICT Secretary Henry Rhoel Aguda used stronger language at the June 22, 2026 pilot go-live ceremony:

BOC Commissioner Ariel F. Nepomuceno frames modernization as more than digitization:

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8"><h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Ariel F. On the industry side, the Federation of Philippine Industries (FPI) was reported in January 2026 as expecting the NSW-ITFP to help exporters compete regionally. With roughly 40 agencies involved in issuing import and export permits, licenses, and clearances, FPI's view, as reported, is that connecting them to a single digital portal would cut the number of agency visits, lower transaction costs, and make faster clearance a boost to supply chain reliability.

As of this writing, we have not found a specific public comment on the NSW from Japanese business groups such as the Japanese Chamber of Commerce and Industry of the Philippines, Inc. (JCCIPI), or from customs broker associations. We will follow up with on-the-ground reactions from brokers and traders as the pilot expands to more agencies.

## Connection to ASEAN Trade

National systems connect through the ASEAN Single Window. All ASEAN members joined live exchange of ATIGA electronic certificates of origin in 2019, enabling digital proof for preferential tariffs. The Philippines also exchanges ASEAN Customs Declaration Documents.

The ASEAN Investment Report cites an estimate of up to six days and about US$150 saved per electronic Form D compared with paper. This is a regional estimate, not a guaranteed clearance time for an individual Philippine shipment.

## Five Actions for Businesses

1. Map HS codes, responsible regulators, and permits for every product
2. Standardize legal names, addresses, tax numbers, and importer registrations
3. Store invoices, packing lists, and origin data in consistent formats
4. Assign internal application owners instead of relying entirely on brokers
5. Prepare continuity procedures while old and new systems coexist

The impact will be greatest for food, chemicals, medicines, tobacco, agricultural goods, and regulated electrical products involving multiple agencies. Digitalization cannot compensate for incorrect source data or a misunderstood permit requirement.
