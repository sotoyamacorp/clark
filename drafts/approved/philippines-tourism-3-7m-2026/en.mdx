# Philippines reaches 3.7m visitors: where business demand is

## Introduction

The Philippines welcomed about 3.7 million international visitors from January through July 2026, up 4.25% year on year. The recovery is real, but it remains incomplete against 2019 and modest beside major ASEAN destinations.

For investors, the practical conclusion is that national growth does not translate evenly into every city, island, hotel or retail site.

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8"><h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Visitor arrivals include business and MICE travel</h4><p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed">DOT/PSA visitor-arrival statistics use the UNWTO's international standard definition of a "visitor" (IRTS 2008). Under this standard, business, meeting and training trips count as a qualifying "main purpose" alongside leisure and other personal travel, and are included in the headline total — unless the traveler is entering to take up paid employment with a local employer (PSA technical notes). DOT separately publishes a "purpose of travel" breakdown that includes Business/Convention categories, but this is a subset of the total, not an addition to it. So the figures in this article most likely already include business and MICE travelers alongside leisure tourists. That said, we could not find a DOT document stating this explicitly in plain language, so treat it as a well-supported inference from the statistical design (IRTS 2008, arrival-card methodology) rather than a directly confirmed fact. The total also includes overseas Filipinos (balikbayans), which DOT has recently begun reporting as a separate line item alongside foreign-passport holders.</p></div>

## What it means for Clark

Clark can combine direct airport access, MICE, golf, family travel, business trips, Pampanga food culture and connections to New Clark City and Subic. Published early-2026 schedules showed 86 weekly seats-bearing services across four airlines between Philippine cities including Clark and Bangkok.

The region should not simply replicate Manila. Its advantage is a shorter airport transfer and a multi-destination Central Luzon product.

## Six metrics for investment decisions

1. Foreign visitors versus overseas Filipinos.
2. Airport and route seats and load factors.
3. Local room supply, occupancy and average daily rates.
4. Spend and stay length by nationality.
5. MICE, corporate and group mix.
6. Online booking and payment-method mix.

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8"><h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Don't apply the national 3.7 million figure directly to a sales forecast</h4><p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed">Demand varies widely across Boracay, Cebu, Palawan, Manila and Clark. For retail siting or hotel investment, build up local walk-in and drive-in catchment demand, competing supply and seasonality region by region rather than working top-down from the nationwide total.</p></div>

## Frequently asked questions

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8"><h3 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-2">Who is included in Philippine international visitor arrivals?</h3><p class="text-gray-700">DOT visitor-arrival totals may include foreign visitors and overseas Filipinos. Match definitions before comparing them with foreign-tourist-only series.</p></div>

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8"><h3 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-2">Do rising arrivals automatically make a hotel investment attractive?</h3><p class="text-gray-700">No. Check city- and island-level room supply, occupancy, average daily rate, seasonality, air seats, and business or group demand.</p></div>

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8"><h3 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-2">Are business travelers included in visitor-arrival figures?</h3><p class="text-gray-700">Most likely yes. DOT/PSA statistics use the UNWTO international standard (IRTS 2008) definition of a "visitor," under which business, meeting and training trips count as a qualifying purpose unless the traveler is taking up paid employment with a local employer. We could not find a plain-language DOT statement confirming this explicitly, so treat it as a well-supported inference from the statistical design rather than a directly confirmed fact.</p></div>
