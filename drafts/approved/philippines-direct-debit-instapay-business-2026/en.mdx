# Philippines Launches Three Payment Services; Business InstaPay Limit Rises to PHP500,000

## Introduction

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) and Philippine Payments Management Inc. (PPMI), the industry-led payment system management body, launched Direct Debit PH, InstaPay Cash-In, and InstaPay for Business on July 29, 2026.

This is not simply another consumer app. The initiatives address three operational gaps: recurring collections, transfers between a customer's own accounts, and higher-value instant transfers by registered businesses.

## What changes under the three services?

Under Direct Debit PH, a customer gives the biller a formal mandate to collect from an account on scheduled dates. It can improve collection predictability for sellers and reduce missed due dates for customers.

The initial InstaPay Cash-In participants announced were AllBank, BPI, China Bank, GoTyme Bank, G-Xchange (GCash), MariBank, ShopeePay, and UnionBank. Not every bank or wallet supported the service at launch.

InstaPay for Business raises the limit for registered businesses from PHP50,000 to PHP500,000 per transfer. This is not a universal increase for all retail users; businesses must confirm registration and product conditions with their bank or e-money issuer.

The 2024 share was also 59.0% by value. BSP targets a 60%–70% digital share by volume by 2028.

Adoption differs by use case. In 2024, person-originated payments (P2X) were 72.2% digital by volume, while business-originated payments (B2X) were only 19.8%. Digital B2B transaction volume nevertheless rose 28.1%, from about 160 million transactions in 2023 to 205 million in 2024.

InstaPay launched in 2018, close in timing to Thailand's PromptPay (2017) and India's UPI (2016). By annual transaction volume, however, InstaPay processed about 160 million transactions in 2024—far smaller than UPI (about 172 billion), PromptPay (about 24.3 billion), or DuitNow and BI-FAST (both in the billions). Population differences are large (India roughly 1.4 billion, Indonesia roughly 280 million, the Philippines roughly 110 million, Thailand roughly 71 million, Malaysia roughly 34 million, Singapore roughly 6 million), so raw transaction counts alone should not be read as a simple ranking. By growth rate, InstaPay rose 64.9% year-on-year from 2023 to 2024—one of the higher growth rates among the countries compared.

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8"><h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Growth rate and business adoption matter more than raw scale</h4><p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed">More telling than the absolute transaction count is that the Philippines' business-facing features (InstaPay for Business, Direct Debit PH) only began taking shape in 2026. Compared with markets like India and Malaysia, where high-frequency use by both businesses and consumers is already established, the Philippines still has considerable room to grow in both B2B and B2C digital payments.</p></div>

## Seven checks before adoption

1. Whether the company's bank supports each service
2. Corporate onboarding and per-transaction or daily limits
3. How customer mandates are captured, stored, and revoked
4. Retry and notification rules for failed collections
5. Reconciliation with ERP records, invoices, and bank statements
6. Transfer fees and pricing for small payments
7. Fraud controls, segregation of duties, refunds, and audit logs

In June 2026, BSP introduced a framework for zero-fee small merchant payments and pricing parameters for person-to-person transfers. Actual charges still depend on the institution and product conditions. Companies should separate technical availability from the total economic benefit to their operation.

## Which companies should pay attention?

- Telecom, education, insurance, and SaaS companies collecting fixed recurring amounts
- E-commerce, retail, and logistics operators using multiple bank and e-money accounts
- Philippine entities making urgent supplier or expense payments above PHP50,000
- Shared-service teams seeking to reduce checks and manual reconciliation

Start by measuring today's transaction count, average value, failure rate, and reconciliation time. A limited pilot with selected customers or suppliers makes the operational benefit easier to evaluate.
