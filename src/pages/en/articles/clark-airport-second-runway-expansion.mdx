## The second runway plan and its progress

On April 27, 2026, BCDA (Bases Conversion and Development Authority) announced it had awarded the detailed engineering design contract for Clark International Airport's second runway to a joint venture between local firm Schema Konsult, Inc. and South Korea's Yooshin Engineering Corp. The contract is worth ₱206.9 million.

### Japan route details, plus domestic and regional connections

The Clark-Narita route is Cebu Pacific's third Narita route overall, and right now it's the only airline offering a direct Japan-Clark connection. The flights run as 5J5068 (Clark 7:00am → Narita 12:30pm) and 5J5069 (Narita 1:15pm → Clark 5:00pm). Cebu Pacific has said it's targeting a bigger share of the Japan market in 2026, with further frequency increases on Narita-connected routes expected going forward.

On the domestic side, Clark International Airport is currently served by six airlines flying to 13 destinations across the Philippines, including Cebu (about 1h25m-2h15m flight time), Davao (about 2 hours), Malay (the gateway airport for Boracay), and Naga — connecting Clark directly to the country's major tourism and business hubs. Regional connections are growing too, building on the Hanoi route mentioned above (three times weekly) as part of a broader expansion into Southeast Asia.

## What this means for Japanese companies

**Airport access is foundational infrastructure that touches everything — logistics, business travel, and staff mobility — for companies considering Clark.** More passenger flights make it easier to travel from Japan for business trips and site visits, and expanded cargo capacity is an important consideration for manufacturers and logistics companies moving goods in and out.

The second runway itself won't be operational until 2029, so it doesn't directly bear on near-term expansion decisions. That said, the fact that passenger numbers are growing and new routes keep launching right now is a sign that Clark's air connectivity is improving over the medium term — a positive data point when evaluating Clark's fit as a logistics base.
