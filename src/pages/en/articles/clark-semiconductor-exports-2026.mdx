# Clark's Semiconductor Exports Hit $3.8B: CDC Talks with Locators, What's Next

## Introduction

Clark's existing footprint in the semiconductor and electronics supply chain is bigger than many people realize. According to the Clark Development Corporation (CDC), semiconductor and electronics exports from Clark Freeport Zone reached $3.8 billion in 2025. On July 7, 2026, CDC also convened semiconductor and electronics locator companies in the zone for a direct dialogue on workforce, importation procedures, and regulatory issues.

This article looks at what's behind that export figure, what came out of CDC's conversation with locators, and what it means for companies considering Clark.

## What's Behind the $3.8B Figure

  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">

### Where Do the Exports Go, and How Big Is This Nationally?

CDC hasn't published a Clark-specific breakdown by destination country, but national-level data for the Philippines' electronics sector gives a reasonable proxy for the pattern and puts Clark's scale in context.

<div class="space-y-6 my-8">
  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Destination countries (Philippines-wide, electronics, 2025)</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      Hong Kong (19.5%), the US (14.8%), China (9.5%), Japan (6.4%), and Singapore (6.1%) led. Hong Kong also functions as a re-export hub into mainland China, so the effective share flowing to China is likely larger than the direct figure suggests. Clark's own export mix is likely broadly similar to this national pattern.
    </p>
  </div>
  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Share of national electronics exports</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      The Philippines' total electronics exports reached $49.64 billion in 2025. Clark's $3.8 billion works out to roughly 7.7% of that total ($3.8B ÷ $49.64B).
    </p>
  </div>
  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Share of total Philippine exports</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      Electronics account for 58.8% of the Philippines' total exports, which implies total exports of roughly $84.4 billion. On that basis, Clark's $3.8 billion works out to about 4.5% of the country's entire export economy — not a small slice.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

## What CDC Discussed with Locators

On July 7, 2026, CDC convened semiconductor and electronics locator companies in the zone for an industry consultation. CDC President Agnes Devanadera said the input gathered will be used to improve services, and that policy or regulatory issues requiring national-government action will be pursued through ongoing talks with relevant agencies.

### How This Differs from Pax Silica

Our earlier article on New Clark City's ["Pax Silica" AI hub](/en/articles/clark-pax-silica-ai-hub/) covered a large-scale project still being built from the ground up. The $3.8 billion figure here, by contrast, reflects semiconductor and electronics companies **already operating** within Clark Freeport Zone. The two sit in adjacent but distinct areas (Clark Freeport Zone vs. New Clark City), and together they show the Philippines pursuing a two-track semiconductor strategy: strengthening an existing cluster while also building a new, larger-scale hub.

---

## What It Means for Japanese Semiconductor and Electronics Firms

**Entering Clark cold is a different proposition from entering a Clark that already has an established cluster in your industry.** A cluster of 22 companies and 4,289 workers implies that the local labor market, supplier base, and logistics network have already developed to some degree. On the hiring side, that also means easier access to a talent pool with relevant industry experience and cross-company knowledge.

At the same time, the fact that CDC raised workforce, importation, and regulatory issues as discussion points means these are still areas being actively improved, not solved problems. If you're seriously evaluating Clark, it's worth checking what CDC's Red Carpet Services can offer for your specific case, and talking directly with companies already operating there to get a current, on-the-ground read.
