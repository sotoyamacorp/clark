# A 1,618-Hectare AI Hub Takes Shape Near Clark Under "Pax Silica": Foxconn Investing, What It Means for Japanese Companies

## Introduction

Many people still picture Clark primarily as a manufacturing and IT-BPO destination. But 2026 has brought a much bigger story into view: under the US-led semiconductor and AI supply chain initiative "Pax Silica," plans are underway to develop a 1,618-hectare AI industrial hub in New Clark City.

This article explains what Pax Silica is, how it connects to Clark, and what it means for Japanese companies.

## What is Pax Silica?

Pax Silica is a US-led framework aimed at building a secure, resilient silicon supply chain spanning critical minerals, semiconductors, AI, and advanced manufacturing. It's designed to reduce dependence on China for technology manufacturing, and the Philippines announced it was joining the initiative in April 2026.

## A note on geography

**New Clark City is a different area from the "Clark Freeport Zone" (in Pampanga, under CDC) that we've covered in previous articles.** New Clark City sits in Tarlac province and, while also under BCDA, is being developed as an administratively separate planned city. The two get lumped together as "Clark" in casual conversation, but if you're evaluating an expansion, it's worth confirming exactly which zone you're talking about and which agency has jurisdiction (CDC, or BCDA directly).

On the workforce side, the American agency involved has signed an MOU with Stanford University, and a parallel public-private workforce program is being prepared with the Semiconductor and Electronics Industries in the Philippines Foundation (SEIPI).

---

## What this means for Japanese companies

**At this stage, the framework agreement itself hasn't even been finalized.** Local media have also raised environmental concerns, and details like land acquisition, permitting, and the actual roster of participating companies are still being worked out. This isn't information that should drive an immediate investment decision — it's better understood as a medium-to-long-term regional trend to watch.

That said, the fact that a $10 billion-scale investment is being planned in semiconductors and AI infrastructure could meaningfully affect the supplier network and labor market around Clark. Even companies not considering direct participation may find it worth tracking, given the potential ripple effects on adjacent industries like construction, logistics, and staffing.
