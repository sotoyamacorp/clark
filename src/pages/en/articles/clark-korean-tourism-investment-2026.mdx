# Korean Developers Accelerate Hotel and MICE Investment in Clark: Growth Beyond Industry

## Introduction

Clark tends to conjure images of semiconductor factories and industrial parks. But in 2026, a series of hotel and serviced-apartment investments led by Korean developers show that Clark's growth isn't limited to manufacturing — tourism and MICE (meetings, incentives, conferences, and exhibitions) are developing right alongside it.

This article looks at two Korean-led investment deals and what they mean for companies considering Clark.

## JnH's ₱840 Million Mixed-Use Project

### Who Is JnH?

JnH Philippines Development Corp. is a Korean hospitality investor led by President Lee Sangte. It's actually an established player in the Clark market — the company entered before COVID-19 upended global travel and construction. Despite pandemic-era disruptions, JnH maintained its commitment to the Philippine market, and this latest agreement and compressed timeline reflect a renewed push forward. The fact that it's an investor that stuck around through the pandemic is itself a small data point in favor of Clark's underlying resilience as a market.

<div class="space-y-6 my-8">
  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">1. Amended lease agreement with CDC signed April 28, 2026</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      Korean hospitality investor JnH Philippines Development Corp. signed an amended lease agreement with CDC for a 17,373-square-meter site along C.M. Recto Highway within Clark Freeport Zone.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">2. Timeline compressed from 5 years to 30 months</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      What was originally a five-year construction plan has been shortened to 30 months, reflecting a push to capture growing demand in the Clark area sooner. Phased delivery is also planned, with some portions opening ahead of full completion.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">3. 17 pool villas plus three towers</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      Phase one includes 17 pool villas, to be followed by a 15-story mixed-use tower and two 12-story buildings. Total investment: ₱840 million.
    </p>

## Luxia's ₱4.4 Billion Hotel and MICE Development

### Who Is Luxia?

Luxia Corporation is part of Luxia Holdings, Inc., a Seoul-based upscale property developer. The agreement was signed by Luxia Corp. CEO Ji-won Chae and CDC President Agnes Devanadera at the Clark Visitors Center. Where JnH is a pandemic-tested incumbent, Luxia represents fresh Korean capital entering Clark's high-end real estate and MICE market for the first time.

## Clark's Tourism Investment Isn't Actually New

Looking only at JnH and Luxia, this might seem like a brand-new trend. In reality, hotel, casino, and resort investment in Clark has a real history behind it.

<div class="space-y-6 my-8">
  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Widus Hotel and Casino: a $250M expansion in 2021</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      One of Clark's original major hotels. In 2021 it completed roughly a $250 million expansion across 130,000 square meters, adding a large gaming floor, the Philippines' first Swissôtel property, multiple restaurants, a pool, and event/ballroom space.
    </p>
  </div>
  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Royce Hotel and Casino: one of Clark's largest gaming floors</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      A 27,000-square-meter gaming floor — among the largest in Clark — alongside a 297-room hotel, dining, retail, MICE space, and a pool.
    </p>
  </div>
  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Fontana: a cautionary tale</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      Once one of Clark's most successful casinos, Fontana was shut down in 2016 over the hiring of undocumented Chinese workers. It has since fallen into disrepair, and CDC is now seeking investors to revive it. It's a reminder that, like any sector, tourism and hospitality investment in Clark hasn't been uniformly smooth sailing.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

<p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed mb-8">
In other words, JnH and Luxia aren't Clark's "first wave" of tourism investment — they're building on momentum that dates back to around 2010. Given how mixed the track record has been among existing players, how each deal is structured with CDC, its location, and its target market will likely matter a great deal to whether these newer projects succeed.
</p>

## The Case for Going Through CDC, and What Incentives Apply

**Any hospitality or real estate business in Clark has to go through CDC as the entry point.** Here's what that relationship typically offers.

<div class="grid grid-cols-1 md:grid-cols-2 gap-6 my-12">
  <div class="bg-white border-2 border-gray-200 rounded-lg p-6 shadow-sm">
    <div class="text-3xl mb-3">📝</div>
    <h4 class="text-base font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">A transparent lease and registration process</h4>
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-700 mb-4">CDC is generally regarded as having clear, predictable land-lease and business-registration procedures — a meaningful advantage for foreign investors.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="bg-white border-2 border-gray-200 rounded-lg p-6 shadow-sm">
    <div class="text-3xl mb-3">✈️</div>
    <h4 class="text-base font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Access to a 2,500-hectare aviation complex</h4>
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-700 mb-4">CDC-registered businesses gain access to extensive aviation-related infrastructure, including Clark International Airport.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="bg-white border-2 border-gray-200 rounded-lg p-6 shadow-sm">
    <div class="text-3xl mb-3">🏛️</div>
    <h4 class="text-base font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Tax incentives under the CREATE MORE Act</h4>
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-700 mb-4">CDC is one of the IPAs that administers incentives under the CREATE MORE Act framework. See our [CREATE MORE Act explainer](/en/articles/clark-create-more-tax-incentives/) for details.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="bg-white border-2 border-gray-200 rounded-lg p-6 shadow-sm">
    <div class="text-3xl mb-3">⚠️</div>
    <h4 class="text-base font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Whether hotels qualify needs individual confirmation</h4>
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-700 mb-4">Incentives like the income tax holiday (ITH) are primarily designed around export-oriented enterprises. How much of that same benefit a domestic-market business like a hotel or serviced apartment can access depends on how it's registered (export vs. domestic market enterprise), so this needs to be confirmed case by case.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed mb-8">
Neither JnH nor Luxia has publicly disclosed the specific tax treatment of their deals. What CDC tends to emphasize isn't tax incentives per se, but rather Clark's location (airport access, road infrastructure, security) and the growth trajectory of MICE demand, alongside a transparent contracting process. If you're considering a hospitality or real estate move into Clark, it's worth confirming incentive eligibility directly with CDC rather than assuming it mirrors what export manufacturers receive.
</p>

## What It Means for Japanese Companies

**Clark isn't only about semiconductors and industrial land.** Alongside the manufacturing cluster we've covered in pieces on [Clark's semiconductor exports](/en/articles/clark-semiconductor-exports-2026/) and [industrial land demand](/en/articles/clark-industrial-real-estate-2026/), hotel and MICE investment is steadily advancing too.

For Japanese companies, this has a practical, if indirect, relevance. As business trips and site visits to Clark increase, a growing supply of quality hotels and meeting facilities directly affects how easy it is to operate here day to day. It also broadens the menu of options for companies weighing an entry into Clark: it's no longer just "lease space in an industrial park" — real estate and hospitality investment is becoming a genuinely viable path too.

That said, both deals covered here are Korean-backed, and Japanese hospitality investment in Clark hasn't shown similarly visible momentum yet. Japanese manufacturers like Enplas have already established a presence on the industrial side, but real estate and tourism remain an area with considerably more room for Japanese companies to move in.
