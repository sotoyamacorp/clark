# Clark Local Tax Clarified: A Practical Guide to the 2% RBELT Cap

## Introduction

Clark tax explainers often mix two messages: a 5% tax in lieu of national and local taxes, and a separate local tax. Both can be true because the answer depends on the incentive regime and registration history. Joint Memorandum Circular No. 01-2026, effective March 30, 2026, gives investors a clearer decision framework.

## How Local Taxation Works in the Philippines

To place RBELT in context, it helps to start with how local taxation itself works in the Philippines.

Provinces, cities, and municipalities (LGUs) have the power to pass their own ordinances and tax businesses operating within their jurisdiction. This power comes from Section 5, Article X of the 1987 Constitution, with the specific tax types and rate ceilings set out in the Local Government Code of 1991 (Republic Act No. 7160). The two main local taxes are:

- **Local Business Tax**: imposed by cities and municipalities on a business's gross sales, with rate schedules set by ordinance and varying by industry.
- **Real Property Tax**: imposed by provinces and cities on the assessed value of land, buildings, and other real property.

For years, the common understanding among companies registered in an economic zone (PEZA zones, or freeports like Clark and Subic) was that local taxes did not really apply to them. This traces back to the Investment Incentives Act framework of the 1960s, which introduced income tax holidays, and to the 5% Gross Income Earned tax that PEZA began applying to registered enterprises in the 1990s (later renamed the Special Corporate Income Tax, or SCIT). That 5% was structured as being "in lieu of" both national and local taxes — pay the 5%, and there was no separate local business tax or real property tax to worry about.

That basic premise carried over into the CREATE Act of 2021 (Republic Act No. 11534), which renamed the 5% regime SCIT. It shifted with the CREATE MORE Act of 2024 (Republic Act No. 12066). For registered enterprises choosing an Income Tax Holiday (ITH) or the newly introduced Enhanced Deductions Regime (EDR), CREATE MORE made clear that national income tax is exempted, but local taxes, fees, and charges imposed by LGUs are a separate matter — meaning they can, in principle, be taxed locally. SCIT-electing enterprises kept the broader in-lieu-of-all-taxes treatment, but ITH and EDR enterprises ended up facing potential local tax exposure that SCIT enterprises did not. (Some practitioners note that the original CREATE Act text was itself ambiguous on fees and charges, which led to inconsistent treatment across LGUs.)

RBELT was created to fill that gap. The CREATE MORE Act amended Section 294 of the National Internal Revenue Code to give LGUs the option of imposing, by ordinance, a local tax capped at 2% of gross income on registered projects or activities during the ITH or EDR period. Critically, this is not a tax that takes effect automatically — it only applies once the relevant LGU's Sanggunian (legislative council) passes its own ordinance. There is no single, uniform rollout; each LGU decides separately whether to adopt RBELT and at what rate. That is the most important starting point for any investor evaluating Clark or elsewhere.

On March 23, 2026, the DILG, DOF, and DTI jointly issued Joint Memorandum Circular No. 01-2026 (effective March 30, 2026) to standardize how this framework is implemented. With more than 426 registered ecozones under PEZA alone, full nationwide rollout across LGUs is expected to take time.

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8"><h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">A Clark-specific wrinkle: the RA 9400 "2% share" is a different mechanism</h4><p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed">Separate from RBELT, Clark (and Subic) has long had another way local governments benefit from registered enterprises: a share of SCIT revenue. Under Republic Act No. 9400 (amending RA 7227), 2 percentage points of the 5% SCIT collected from registered enterprises is channeled through CDC (Clark Development Corporation) or SBMA (Subic Bay Metropolitan Authority) to nearby host LGUs — Angeles City, Mabalacat City, and Porac in Pampanga, plus Bamban and Capas in Tarlac. This is a national-level revenue-sharing arrangement tied to SCIT enterprises, and it is a distinct mechanism from RBELT, which LGUs impose by ordinance on ITH/EDR enterprises. For a Clark investor, which local-tax framework actually applies depends on whether the enterprise elects SCIT or ITH/EDR.</p></div>

## What RBELT Means

The Registered Business Enterprise Local Tax is a local tax introduced under the CREATE MORE Act. During an Income Tax Holiday or Enhanced Deductions Regime, the relevant LGU may impose a rate of no more than 2% on gross income from the registered project or activity.

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8"><h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">RBELT replaces covered local taxes once imposed</h4><p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed">JMC 01-2026 treats RBELT as being in lieu of local taxes, fees, and charges, including local business and real property taxes. Once an LGU adopts RBELT by ordinance, it cannot stack separate covered local levies on the same registered activity.</p></div>

## The Answer Changes Under ITH, EDR, and SCIT

| Incentive regime | Basic national-tax treatment | RBELT |
|---|---|---|
| ITH | Income-tax exemption for the registered activity | Up to 2%, subject to LGU ordinance |
| EDR | 20% income-tax rate plus enhanced deductions | Up to 2%, subject to LGU ordinance |
| SCIT | 5% of gross income in lieu of covered national and local taxes | Not imposed |

SCIT is generally an option for qualifying export enterprises. Domestic-market enterprises and firms choosing EDR may face a different local-tax profile. A Clark registration alone does not answer the question; the approved project and elected regime do.

## Three Checks for a Clark Investment

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8"><h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Set the tax classification before finalizing the site</h4><p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed">CDC handles investment registration in Clark, while relevant LGUs remain part of local-tax administration. Define the registered activity, incentive regime, and property-use structure, then ask CDC and tax advisers to assess the same fact pattern.</p></div>

1. Confirm the activities covered by the Certificate of Registration and Tax Incentives
2. Determine when ITH, EDR, or SCIT applies to each project
3. Check the relevant LGU's RBELT ordinance, rate, filing, and payment procedure

JMC 01-2026 also addresses beneficial use: where an investment promotion agency owns land but an RBE uses it, the RBE may be treated as the taxable person. Leasing does not automatically remove real-property-tax questions.

## How Batangas, Cebu, and Pampanga Compare on RBELT Ordinances

Because RBELT only takes effect once the relevant LGU passes its own ordinance, actual rates and implementation status vary by location. As a reference point for Clark-bound investors, we checked the status in Batangas and Cebu, two other major economic-zone hubs, alongside Pampanga, the province Clark sits in.

| Region | Key LGUs potentially involved | RBELT ordinance status (as of August 2026) | Actual rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Batangas | Batangas Province, Batangas City, and others (home to numerous PEZA ecozones such as Lima Technology Center) | No public information confirming an enacted ordinance | Not publicly disclosed |
| Cebu | Cebu City, Cebu Province, Lapu-Lapu City, and others (an IT-BPO and manufacturing hub) | No public information confirming an enacted ordinance | Not publicly disclosed |
| Pampanga (Clark area) | Angeles City, Mabalacat City, Porac | No public information confirming an enacted ordinance | Not publicly disclosed |

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8"><h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">The absence of public rate data is itself a practical takeaway</h4><p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed">JMC 01-2026 itself states that RBELT is not self-executing and requires a Sanggunian ordinance before it can be imposed. Tax practitioners have also noted that some LGUs have attempted to collect RBELT-like amounts without a formal ordinance in place, which can lead to disputes and refund claims later. Given how recently this framework took effect, we could not find public documentation confirming an enacted RBELT ordinance or specific rate for Batangas, Cebu, or the Pampanga municipalities around Clark as of this writing (August 2026). In other words, assuming your rate will resemble "whatever a neighboring LGU charges" is not a safe assumption right now. Investors should confirm ordinance status and rates directly with the specific LGU where they plan to register, rather than skip that step.</p></div>
