# New Philippines-Japan Tax Treaty: 5% Dividend Rate and What to Check Before It Takes Effect

## Introduction

Japan and the Philippines signed a fully revised income tax treaty in May 2026, replacing a framework first concluded in 1980. It matters for dividends, interest, royalties, services, and branch operations—but signature does not mean the new rates can already be used.

A tax treaty coordinates the claims of the source country and the recipient's residence country so the same income is not taxed twice without relief. If a Philippine subsidiary pays a dividend to its Japanese parent, the Philippines may withhold tax at source, while Japan applies its domestic rules to the receipt. The treaty caps source-country tax; Japanese foreign-tax-credit or foreign-subsidiary-dividend rules address the residence-country side.

The “5% treaty rate” is a ceiling on tax withheld from the dividend. It does not turn the subsidiary's corporate income tax rate into 5%, and it does not complete the Japanese tax analysis automatically.

## Current Treaty Versus the New Treaty

| Income | Current treaty before signing | New treaty | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dividends: 90%+ held six months | 10% | 5% | New 5% tier |
| Dividends: 10%+ but below 90%, held six months | 10% | 10% | No rate change |
| Other dividends | 15% | 15% | No rate change |
| Interest received by qualifying governments | Exempt | Exempt | No change |
| Other interest | 10% | 10% | No change |
| Film-related royalties | 15% | 10% | Five-point reduction |
| Other royalties | 10% | 10% | No change |

For ownership tests, voting power applies when a Japanese company pays; capital ownership applies when a Philippine company pays.

## How to Read the Investment-Income Rates

### Dividends

The new convention caps source-country tax at 5% for qualifying dividends where the recipient has held at least 90% of voting power or capital for at least six months. A 10% ceiling applies at the 10% ownership threshold, while other dividends remain at 15%. A wholly owned subsidiary previously fell under the 10% tier, so the ceiling can be cut in half.

For a PHP100 million dividend that qualifies for the 5% tier, the treaty ceiling would be PHP5 million, versus PHP10 million under the current 10% tier—a PHP5 million difference. Actual cash tax still depends on domestic law, treaty procedures, beneficial ownership, and the Japanese tax treatment.

Beneficial ownership, domestic filing procedures, and the principal-purpose anti-abuse test must also be considered before a company relies on the lower rate.</p></div>

### Dividend-rate checklist

1. Confirm treaty residence of payer and recipient
2. Confirm that the recipient is the beneficial owner
3. Test the 90%, 10%, or other ownership tier
4. Confirm the ownership was maintained for six months
5. Check whether the holding is effectively connected with a local PE
6. Complete anti-abuse and domestic filing requirements

### Interest and royalties

Interest remains exempt when received by qualifying governments and generally capped at 10% in other cases. Royalties, previously split between 15% for certain film-related payments and 10% for others, are standardized at 10%.

## How the Dividend Rate Compares Across ASEAN

| Investment country | Representative treaty ceiling on dividends paid to Japan | Practical reading |
|---|---|---|
| Philippines, new treaty | 5% (90%+, six months), 10% (10%+, six months), 15% other | The 5% tier has a high ownership threshold |
| Singapore | 5% (25%+, six months), 15% other | Lower ownership threshold for the parent-subsidiary tier |
| Malaysia | 5% (25%+, six months), 15% other | Domestic dividend rules must also be checked |
| Vietnam | 10% | No 5% ownership tier |
| Indonesia | 10% (25%+, 12 months), 15% other | Longer holding period |

The Philippines' new 5% ceiling is competitive, but its 90% ownership test is stricter than the 25% tests in Singapore and Malaysia. Joint ventures should therefore budget at 10% or 15% unless they clearly qualify. This table simplifies treaty ceilings; domestic law, beneficial ownership, and MLI anti-abuse rules still apply.

## Business Profits and Permanent Establishments

When an enterprise operates through a permanent establishment (PE), the host country may tax only profits attributable to that PE. The new convention expressly applies arm's-length principles and recognizes internal dealings between a head office and branch in calculating attributable profits.

Companies sending personnel across the Japan-Philippines corridor should look beyond invoicing language and document what people actually do on the ground, especially in sales, technical support, and project supervision.

## Dispute Resolution and Anti-Abuse Rules

Unresolved mutual-agreement cases may proceed to arbitration after two years, subject to the treaty's timing rules. At the same time, treaty benefits may be denied where obtaining the benefit was one of the principal purposes of an arrangement.

## When Will It Apply?

In general, withholding and other income-tax provisions apply from January 1 of the following calendar year. Companies should wait for an official effective-date announcement.</p></div>

A useful preparation step is to inventory all cross-border dividends, interest, royalties, and service payments. Compare the current treaty, new treaty, and eligibility conditions, while keeping a scenario in which the current rules continue.
