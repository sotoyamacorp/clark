# UPS's New Clark Hub Opens in September: LIPAD vs. CIAC, Explained

## Introduction

Following [FedEx's $80 million investment in Clark International Airport, which we covered earlier](/en/articles/clark-fedex-southeast-asia-hub-2026/), rival logistics giant UPS is set to open its own new Clark hub in September 2026 — another sign that Clark's air cargo footprint keeps getting deeper. This article covers what's behind UPS's investment, and clears up a distinction that's easy to mix up: the difference between "LIPAD" and "CIAC," two separate airport-related organizations at Clark.

## UPS's New Clark Hub Opens September 2026, With President Marcos Expected at the Opening

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Part of a $250M+ Asia-Pacific investment plan</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    US logistics giant UPS is set to open a new hub at Clark International Airport in September 2026, with President Marcos reportedly expected to attend the opening ceremony. The Clark facility is part of UPS's broader Asia-Pacific investment commitments, which have topped $250 million since 2023, aimed at strengthening multiple service lines including express delivery, supply chain, and healthcare logistics. Construction reportedly began in February 2025, putting the project at roughly a year and a half from groundbreaking to opening.
  </p>
</div>

## UPS's Partner Is Airport Operator LIPAD, Not CIAC

<div class="border-l-4 border-navy-600 bg-navy-50 p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">A practical breakdown of what each organization actually does</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    In [our earlier article on Jamco's investment](/en/articles/clark-jamco-aerospace-investment-2026/), we covered CIAC (Clark International Airport Corporation). But the organization UPS signed its strategic partnership agreement with this time is a different one: LIPAD (Luzon International Premiere Airport Development Corporation). For any company considering Clark, understanding the difference between these two organizations matters in practice.
  </p>
</div>

---

## What LIPAD Is: A Private Consortium of Filinvest, JG Summit, and Others

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Holding a 25-year operating concession since 2019, LIPAD runs the day-to-day terminal</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    LIPAD is a private consortium made up of four companies: Filinvest Development Corp., JG Summit Holdings, Changi Airports Philippines, and Philippine Airport Ground Support Solutions. In 2019, it signed a 25-year concession agreement with the BCDA (Bases Conversion and Development Authority) and now handles the actual day-to-day operation and maintenance of the Clark International Airport terminal. CIAC, also a BCDA subsidiary, retains an oversight role over the airport as a whole, but — [as we explained previously](/en/articles/clark-jamco-aerospace-investment-2026/) — has shifted its core focus toward developing and leasing out the remaining 1,608 hectares of government-owned land surrounding the airport (the Clark Aviation Capital). In short: LIPAD (private) runs the terminal itself, while CIAC (government) oversees and leases out the surrounding land. That's why a new air cargo facility like UPS's is an agreement with LIPAD — the entity that actually manages runway and terminal operations.
  </p>
</div>

## How the 25-Year Concession Was Decided: A December 2018 Public Bid

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">The Philippines' first hybrid-PPP airport project</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    LIPAD's predecessor entity, the North Luzon Airport Consortium (NLAC), won the rights to operate Clark International Airport through a public bidding process run by the BCDA in December 2018. The agreement was formally finalized in February 2019, and operations were officially handed over from the BCDA to LIPAD on August 16, 2019. This was the first airport project tendered under the Philippine government's "hybrid PPP" (public-private partnership) policy — a model where the government (BCDA) retains asset ownership while entrusting operations, maintenance, and commercial development to a private consortium for a 25-year term.
  </p>
</div>

## How LIPAD and CIAC Actually Work Together, and Who Else Matters

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Close coordination on safety/security, plus alignment on land development planning</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    LIPAD and CIAC coordinate closely on a day-to-day basis, following handover protocols (subject to BCDA approval) established when operations were transferred. The two areas of coordination that get the most emphasis are matters of passenger safety and security, and aligning medium-to-long-term plans for how to develop the surrounding government-owned land — not just the international airport itself, but land suited to commercial and economic development. CIAC itself has described the LIPAD partnership as "key to Clark airport's continued takeoff."
  </p>
</div>

## How This Connects to the Luzon Economic Corridor and Pax Silica Plans

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Clark's strengthening logistics function reinforces the broader vision</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    UPS's new hub is also being positioned as an important piece of the broader development plans centered on Clark, including the [Luzon Economic Corridor](/en/articles/clark-luzon-economic-corridor/) and the [Pax Silica initiative](/en/articles/clark-pax-silica-ai-hub/). It's an investment that translates into shorter logistics lead times and a stronger regional delivery network — reported as one more step in cementing Clark's position as the Philippines' premier logistics gateway.
  </p>
</div>

---

## Why This Matters for Companies

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">With both FedEx and UPS now based there, logistics options at Clark are expanding</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    Having two of the world's biggest logistics players — FedEx and UPS — both establish hubs at Clark expands the import/export logistics options for manufacturing, e-commerce, and retail companies weighing Clark. It's also worth understanding that LIPAD and CIAC divide airport operations and land development between them, since that shapes which entity you'd actually work with for facility leasing or partnerships going forward. Keeping three entities straight in your head — CDC (the Clark Freeport Zone administrator), LIPAD (the airport terminal operator), and CIAC (the surrounding government land) — will save you practical confusion down the line.
  </p>
</div>
