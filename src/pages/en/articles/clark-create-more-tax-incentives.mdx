# The CREATE MORE Act Explained: Tax Incentives for Companies Registered with CDC in Clark

## Introduction

"I've heard the Philippines has tax incentives, but what do they actually cover?" "I was told to register with PEZA — is that the same for Clark?" We get questions like these a lot from companies looking into a Clark expansion.

Here's the short answer: **tax incentives in the Clark Freeport Zone are administered by CDC (Clark Development Corporation), not PEZA**, and the law behind them was substantially revised in 2024. This article breaks down what changed under the 2024 "CREATE MORE Act," and what benefits companies expanding into Clark can actually expect.

---

## What is an "IPA" (Investment Promotion Agency)?

To access tax incentives in the Philippines, you need to register with an Investment Promotion Agency (IPA) appropriate to your business and location. There isn't just one IPA — several operate in parallel.

    <p class="text-sm text-gray-700 mb-4">
      No geographic restriction — covers companies operating anywhere in the Philippines. Open to a broad range of industries, including domestic-market-oriented businesses and agriculture.

    <p class="text-sm text-gray-700 mb-4">
      Covers export-oriented businesses operating inside designated economic zones. This is the IPA most commonly used by Japanese companies.

    <p class="text-sm text-gray-700 mb-4">

    <p class="text-sm text-gray-700 mb-4">
      Region-specific IPAs also exist, such as the Cagayan Economic Zone Authority (CEZA) and the Zamboanga City Special Economic Zone Authority (ZCSEZA).
    </p>
  </div>

Which IPA you register with is largely determined by where your business operates. Outside a special economic zone, that's BOI; inside the Clark Freeport Zone, that's CDC. This article focuses on the latter — companies registering with CDC in Clark.

## What is the CREATE MORE Act?

The CREATE MORE Act (formally, Republic Act No. 12066) was signed into law by President Marcos on November 11, 2024. Its goal is to make the Philippines' investment incentive regime more internationally competitive. The implementing rules and regulations (IRR) were signed on February 17, 2025 by the Department of Finance and the Department of Trade and Industry, with CDC President Agnes Devanadera present at the signing.

### Key changes

<div class="space-y-6 my-8">
  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">1. A longer incentive period</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      The maximum period a company can hold incentives was extended from 17 years to 27 years. For manufacturers planning long-term capital investment, that makes it much easier to plan out a return-on-investment timeline.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">2. A lower corporate tax rate</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      Registered enterprises that choose the Enhanced Deductions Regime now pay a corporate tax rate of 20%, down from 25%.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">3. A bigger power expense deduction</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      The additional deduction on power costs was expanded to 100%. For manufacturing and other power-intensive industries, this translates into a real cost saving.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

## What Clark-based companies actually get

Under the CREATE MORE Act, Registered Business Enterprises (RBEs) in the Clark Freeport Zone are generally eligible for the following benefits.

<div class="grid grid-cols-1 md:grid-cols-2 gap-6 my-12">
  <div class="bg-white border-2 border-gray-200 rounded-lg p-6 shadow-sm">
    <div class="text-3xl mb-3">🏛️</div>
    <h4 class="text-base font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Income Tax Holiday (ITH)</h4>
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-700 mb-4">
      Depending on location and industry priority (as defined under the SIPP), companies get 4-7 years of income tax exemption. After that period ends, a 5% special corporate tax on gross income applies instead of the standard 25% rate.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="bg-white border-2 border-gray-200 rounded-lg p-6 shadow-sm">
    <div class="text-3xl mb-3">📦</div>
    <h4 class="text-base font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Duty-free capital equipment imports</h4>
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-700 mb-4">
      Import duties are waived on capital equipment used directly for your registered project. That's a meaningful reduction in upfront costs if you're bringing in new factory equipment or production lines.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="bg-white border-2 border-gray-200 rounded-lg p-6 shadow-sm">
    <div class="text-3xl mb-3">🧾</div>
    <h4 class="text-base font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">VAT zero-rating on local purchases</h4>
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-700 mb-4">
      VAT is zero-rated on local goods and services purchased directly for your registered project. That said, as explained below, you need a certificate to actually claim this.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="bg-white border-2 border-gray-200 rounded-lg p-6 shadow-sm">
    <div class="text-3xl mb-3">🏗️</div>
    <h4 class="text-base font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Extra incentives for SIPP priority sectors</h4>
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-700 mb-4">
      Projects in priority sectors under the government's Strategic Investment Priority Plan (SIPP) — green technology, for example — may qualify for up to 8 years of ITH.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

## Easy-to-miss details when registering and operating

### CDC and PEZA are two different organizations

Companies expanding into the Clark Freeport Zone register with **CDC (Clark Development Corporation), not PEZA**. CDC is an Investment Promotion Agency (IPA) under BCDA (Bases Conversion and Development Authority), and it's a separate organization from PEZA. The CREATE MORE Act applies to companies registered with CDC and other IPAs, not just PEZA — but if you only go by the general assumption that "expanding into the Philippines means registering with PEZA," you risk approaching the wrong agency entirely.

### VAT zero-rating requires a certificate

A 2025 PEZA circular (Memorandum Circular No. 2025-052) clarified that VAT zero-rating only applies to purchases that are directly necessary for a registered project. RBEs need to obtain a VAT zero-rating certificate from their IPA — without it, suppliers may charge the standard 12% VAT. The same principle applies to CDC-registered companies, so it's worth confirming this before signing contracts with suppliers.
