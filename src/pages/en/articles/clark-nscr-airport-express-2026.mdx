# The 'Airport Express' Will Link Clark and Makati in Under an Hour

## Introduction

Depending on traffic, the trip between Clark and Metro Manila can easily take two to three hours by road. The project meant to solve that is the "Airport Express" — a rail line under construction that will connect Clark International Airport to Makati's central business district (CBD) in under an hour. It's also a project with deep ties to Japan: it's largely funded by yen loans from JICA (the Japan International Cooperation Agency).

This article covers what the plan involves, the JICA financing details, and construction progress — plus what it means for Japanese companies.

## What the "Airport Express" Plan Involves

The "Airport Express" is a high-speed service planned as part of the Philippine government's North-South Commuter Railway (NSCR), directly connecting Clark International Airport to Makati's CBD.

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">What is the NSCR?</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    The NSCR is a 147.26km commuter rail project connecting Clark, Pampanga to Calamba, Laguna, with a total project cost of ₱873.6 billion — one of the largest infrastructure investments in the Philippines. Once fully operational, it's projected to carry roughly 800,000 passengers a day. DOTr Assistant Secretary Irish Calaguas said: "The Clark Station will also be the terminal stop of the upcoming Airport Express service which directly connects CRK airport to the Makati Central Business District within an hour."
  </p>
</div>

Clark Station will sit within the airport terminal complex, connected via an elevated walkway. LIPAD, the operator of Clark International Airport, is expected to operate the station, and AECOM — the same firm behind the masterplan for the <a href="/en/articles/clark-aerodistrict-aerospace-hub-2026/" class="text-accent-700 underline hover:text-accent-500">759-hectare "AeroDistrict" we covered earlier</a> — is also involved in this project.

## The ¥377 Billion JICA Loan and Japan's Role

One thing worth flagging for companies operating in the Philippines: where the money for this project is actually coming from.

<div class="grid grid-cols-1 md:grid-cols-2 gap-6 my-12">
  <div class="bg-white border-2 border-gray-200 rounded-lg p-6 shadow-sm">
    <div class="text-3xl mb-3">🇯🇵</div>
    <h4 class="text-base font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">JICA signed a ¥377 billion loan agreement in February 2023</h4>
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-700 mb-4">On February 23, 2023, the Philippine government and JICA signed loan agreements worth a combined ¥377 billion (roughly $2.8 billion) for the NSCR. ¥107 billion went to the Malolos-Tutuban segment, and ¥270 billion to the extension from Malolos toward the Clark Economic Zone plus the Solis-Calamba segment.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="bg-white border-2 border-gray-200 rounded-lg p-6 shadow-sm">
    <div class="text-3xl mb-3">🔧</div>
    <h4 class="text-base font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">STEP terms require the use of Japanese technology and firms</h4>
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-700 mb-4">The loan for the Malolos-Tutuban segment carries Japan's "Special Terms for Economic Partnership" (STEP), which requires a portion of the financing be spent on Japanese technology and equipment procurement — a direct business opportunity for Japanese rail-related companies.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed mb-8">
In other words, the NSCR — including the Airport Express — isn't just domestic Philippine infrastructure. It's a project deeply tied to Japanese official development assistance (ODA), with Japanese technology actually being deployed on the ground.
</p>

## The Contractors: Japan, Spain, Australia, Indonesia, and Local Firms

The NSCR is bid out and contracted segment by segment (package by package), with different international consortiums handling construction on each. It's not widely known that companies from far more countries than just Japan are involved.

<div class="space-y-6 my-8">
  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">The final 6.5km to Clark airport: Spain's ACCIONA and the Philippines' EEI Corporation</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      The segment most relevant to this article — the final 6.5km connecting directly to the airport — is being built by a joint venture between Spanish infrastructure giant ACCIONA and EEI Corporation, one of the Philippines' oldest construction firms, founded in 1931.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Malolos-Clark extension (Blumentritt segment): ACCIONA and DMCI</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      Civil works for the Blumentritt extension of the Malolos-Clark Railway Project went to a joint venture of ACCIONA and D.M. Consunji, Inc. (DMCI), worth roughly ₱11.29 billion plus $6.88 million. DMCI, known as the "Builder of Landmarks," is one of the Philippines' major construction companies.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Southern segments (S02, S03B): Japan's Taisei, Hong Kong/Australia's Leighton, and the Philippines' First Balfour</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      Elsewhere, a joint venture between Japan's Taisei Corporation and DMCI is building the elevated track, six stations, and the Malanday depot in Valenzuela. A joint venture between Leighton Contractors Asia (Hong Kong-based, under Australia's CIMIC Group) and First Balfour (a Philippine firm under the Lopez Group/First Philippine Holdings, founded in 1969) is handling a segment that includes tunnel works connecting to the Metro Manila Subway.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Indonesian contractors, and Japanese-led signaling systems</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      The 14.9km segment spanning Manila to Taguig went to a joint venture of Indonesian state construction firms PT Adhi Karya (Persero) Tbk and PT PP (Persero) Tbk. On the systems side, Mitsubishi Corporation awarded a turnkey contract to a consortium of France's Alstom and Colas Rail to integrate rolling stock and signaling — including Southeast Asia's first deployment of ERTMS Level 2 signaling.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

<p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed mb-8">
Put together: Japan (via JICA) plays the leading role on the financing side, while on the construction side, companies from Spain, Australia (via Hong Kong), Indonesia, and Japan are each paired with a Philippine partner (EEI Corporation, DMCI, or First Balfour) on different segments. This is a multinational consortium project, not one dependent on a single country or company.
</p>

## What DOTr Is, and Its Surprising History With Clark

There's also useful context around the Department of Transportation (DOTr), the agency overseeing this project.

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">What is DOTr?</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    DOTr is the Philippine government agency responsible for the country's land, sea, and air transportation infrastructure. Its predecessor, the Department of Transportation and Communications (DOTC), was created under Presidential Decree No. 1 in 1972 during the Marcos Sr. administration. In May 2016, its ICT-related functions were spun off to form the Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT), and the remaining agency was renamed the Department of Transportation (DOTr). It's the executive body responsible for planning, coordinating, and implementing national transportation policy.
  </p>
</div>

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">DOTr itself has been headquartered in Clark since 2017</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    It's not widely known, but DOTr — the very agency overseeing the Airport Express — was itself the first national government agency to relocate its headquarters to Clark, moving from Metro Manila (Ortigas) starting in July 2017. That move was an early precursor to what later became the broader "National Government Administrative Center" (NGAC) concept within New Clark City — part of a national push to ease congestion in Metro Manila and decentralize government functions.
  </p>
</div>

<p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed mb-8">
NGAC sets aside 220 of New Clark City's 9,450 hectares for a cluster of government offices. In November 2021, then-President Rodrigo Duterte signed Executive Order 119, directing all executive branch departments, bureaus, and offices to establish field offices there. Plans and progress include a central office for the Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH), an agro-industrial hub for the Department of Agriculture, a minting facility for the central bank (BSP), and the Supreme Court's first regional judicial center. In other words, the Airport Express isn't just a standalone transport project — it's part of a larger national strategy to disperse government functions themselves toward Clark. The fact that DOTr chose to headquarter itself there is one indicator of how seriously the government has taken this shift.
</p>

## Construction Progress and Opening Timeline

<div class="space-y-6 my-8">
  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">The 6.5km Clark extension combines elevated viaducts with a tunnel into the terminal</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      The roughly 6.5km extension linking the NSCR mainline to Clark International Airport is designed with elevated viaduct sections plus an underground tunnel running directly into the airport terminal. In February 2026, the first structural girder was installed and rail welding began at the Mabalacat depot in Pampanga. Acting Transportation Secretary Giovanni Lopez said the project has moved "from planning to execution," and called on contractors to "maintain close coordination with the DOTr to keep timelines intact."
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Right-of-way is 56% cleared in the north, with a mid-2026 target for completion</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      As of 2025, right-of-way clearance (land acquisition and relocation) for the northern segment had reached 56%, with DOTr targeting full clearance of that segment by mid-2026. The Malolos-Manila segment alone carries an estimated cost of roughly ₱149 billion ($2.7 billion).
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Opening estimates range from "within 2026" to 2028, depending on the source</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      Transportation Secretary Jaime Bautista (at the time of that statement) said partial operations between Malolos and Clark would begin in Q3 2026, with full operations targeted for 2029. Other reporting has offered a more conservative estimate for the Clark-to-West Valenzuela segment specifically, suggesting partial operations could begin as early as December 2027 or as late as Q1 2028 in a worst-case scenario. Timelines vary by segment and even official statements aren't fully consistent, so it's worth watching for further updates on exactly when the Airport Express segment itself will open.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

## Why This Matters for Companies

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">For companies weighing a split between a Manila base and a Clark base</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    Today, travel between Clark and Metro Manila is heavily exposed to road traffic, making commute times hard to predict — one factor that's held back some expansion decisions. Once the Airport Express is actually running, keeping leadership and sales functions in Makati while locating manufacturing or back-office operations in Clark becomes a more realistic split from a time-management standpoint. The STEP-driven use of Japanese technology is also an indirect opportunity for companies in rail equipment and engineering services. That said, as noted above, the opening timeline still carries real uncertainty, so it's worth factoring this in cautiously rather than as a firm date in business planning.
  </p>
</div>
