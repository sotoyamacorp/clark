# Clark Meets Global Free Zone Body WFZO Over International Exposure

## Introduction

"How is Clark actually viewed by the international investor community?" It's a question companies evaluating a Philippine expansion sometimes ask. Domestic information — tax incentives, registration procedures — is relatively easy to research. How Clark is perceived internationally, and where it sits within global free zone networks, is harder to gauge from the outside.

In August 2026, a development offered a partial answer. Agnes VST Devanadera, President and CEO of Clark Development Corporation (CDC), met with Dr. Mohammed Alzarooni, Chairman of the World Free Zones Organization (WFZO), at Shangri-La The Fort in Manila. WFZO reportedly offered to feature Clark on its international platforms and communication channels.

On its own, this is a thin story — a meeting took place, an offer was made. No concrete investment outcome has materialized yet. This article lays out, without overstating it, what actually happened, what WFZO is, and what practical significance (if any) this could hold for companies considering Clark.

## What Happened: The Manila Meeting

In August 2026, CDC President and CEO Agnes VST Devanadera met with WFZO Chairman Dr. Mohammed Alzarooni at Shangri-La The Fort in Manila. According to reporting, WFZO offered to feature Clark on its international platform and communication channels, including its newsletter network.

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">What was on the table</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    Per reporting, WFZO's offer included listing Clark on its "One Zone" platform, featuring it through newsletters and communication channels, and encouraging CDC's participation in knowledge-sharing, networking, investment facilitation, policy dialogue, and capacity-building activities. Devanadera reportedly said, in substance, that successful economic zones don't grow in isolation — that learning international best practices, building partnerships, and continuously improving services matter — and pointed to Clark's integrated approach as a source of competitiveness.
  </p>
</div>

Worth noting: this is not a signed formal agreement, nor does it represent any specific investment deal. It's an offer, at this stage. How it develops into concrete activity, if at all, had not been made public as of this writing (August 2026).

---

## What WFZO Actually Is

The World Free Zones Organization (WFZO) is a non-profit international body whose members include operators of economic zones, free zones, and industrial parks, along with lawyers, consultants, government agencies, and international organizations that work in that space. It officially launched in Dubai, UAE on May 19, 2014, and is headquartered there.

Reported membership figures put WFZO at roughly 1,600 economic zones across 163 countries, though this number isn't consistently displayed on WFZO's own website and varies somewhat across its own materials and partner descriptions. Anyone wanting a precise, current count should query WFZO directly.

WFZO's core activities are knowledge-sharing among member zones, its annual World Congress, policy advocacy, and disseminating best practices around investment promotion. It is not a body that provides direct funding or investment guarantees to zones — it operates on networking and information-sharing. That distinction matters when calibrating how much weight to put on this kind of announcement.

## The Philippines' International Standing: A Separate Story From WFZO

Everything above is about one specific zone — Clark — building its own relationship with an industry body, WFZO. A separate, broader question is how the Philippines as a country is actually viewed by international investors. That's a national-level story, distinct from this one, and it's worth laying out on its own terms.

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Credit ratings: investment grade intact, but outlooks bear watching</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    In April 2026, the three major rating agencies weighed in on the Philippines in quick succession. Fitch Ratings kept its rating at BBB but revised the outlook from stable to negative, citing rising energy import costs and a widening current account deficit. S&P Global Ratings kept its BBB+ rating but revised the outlook from positive to stable. Moody's Ratings, by contrast, held both its rating (Baa2) and outlook (stable) unchanged. All three agencies kept the Philippines at investment grade — this was an outlook revision, not a downgrade, and that distinction is worth getting right.
  </p>
</div>

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Business environment and competitiveness rankings: gradual improvement</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    In the World Bank's 2025 Business Ready (B-READY) report, the Philippines ranked 53rd out of 101 economies, with an overall score of 61.04, up from 59.81 in 2024. It scored particularly well on the regulatory framework pillar, ranking 26th. Separately, in IMD's 2026 World Competitiveness Ranking, the Philippines placed 47th out of 70 economies, up four spots from 51st out of 69 the year before. Both rankings still put the country toward the lower end of the Asia-Pacific field, but the trend line is upward, which is worth noting.
  </p>
</div>

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">FDI trends: momentum remains weak</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    According to Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) data, net foreign direct investment inflows into the Philippines came to $7.79 billion in 2025, down 17.1% from $9.4 billion in 2024 — the lowest level in a decade outside the pandemic years. Reporting has also noted that the Philippines has captured a disproportionately small share of the roughly $244 billion in foreign capital that flowed into Southeast Asia over the same period. Momentum stayed weak into 2026: net FDI inflows in January fell 39.2% year-on-year to just $0.4 billion.
  </p>
</div>

It would be a mistake to reduce this to a simple "the Philippines is viewed well" or "viewed poorly" binary. The more accurate picture is mixed: credit ratings remain investment grade but the outlook needs watching, business-environment and competitiveness rankings are improving gradually, and actual FDI inflows are lagging regional peers. Set against that backdrop, the CDC-WFZO development discussed above is a separate, zone-specific effort by Clark to build its own international recognition — not a proxy for how the country as a whole is being assessed.

## Clark's Track Record of International Ties

To understand this isn't a one-off, it helps to look at what international recognition and partnerships CDC and Clark have accumulated before this.

<div class="space-y-6 my-8">
  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Joining ICCA (2023)</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      In 2023, CDC joined the International Congress and Convention Association (ICCA), the global body for the meetings and conventions industry. The stated goals were to learn industry best practices and expand its professional network, to use ICCA's meetings database and communication network for business development and investment attraction, and to build Clark's position as a MICE (meetings, incentives, conferences, exhibitions) destination in the Philippines.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">GCG Leadership Award (December 2025)</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      In December 2025, Devanadera was named the inaugural recipient of the Governance Commission for Government-Owned or -Controlled Corporations (GCG) Leadership Award, and CDC received a related governance recognition. It's a separate axis from international-body recognition, but it points to a broader period of accumulating recognition for CDC, domestically as well.
    </p>

### The Clark-Specific Angle: A Separate Track From PEZA

One thing worth flagging: the Philippines' economic zone system includes zones administered by PEZA (the Philippine Economic Zone Authority) and, separately, zones like the Clark Freeport Zone administered by CDC under the Bases Conversion and Development Authority (BCDA) framework. These are distinct regulatory tracks — as covered in our earlier piece on how <a href="/en/articles/clark-rbelt-local-tax-2026/" class="text-accent-700 underline hover:text-accent-500">local tax rules differ</a> depending on which framework applies.

That same split shows up in how each track engages internationally. PEZA reportedly signed a cooperation agreement with WFZO back in February 2022, setting up a framework under which PEZA-related entities could become voting members of WFZO. In other words, at the national level, the Philippine economic zone sector already had a WFZO connection through PEZA as of 2022. CDC and Clark's current engagement is a separate, zone-specific move to build its own relationship with WFZO — not the extension of an existing national-level membership. It's worth remembering, when thinking about the Philippine economic zone landscape broadly, that whether a zone falls under PEZA or CDC changes both which international bodies and which domestic rules come into play.

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">How to identify which agency applies to you</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    As a rule of thumb: if your target site sits inside a specific freeport or economic zone, the zone's own authority is your counterpart — CDC for Clark, SBMA for Subic, AFAB for Bataan, APECO for Aurora. If you're locating in one of the many PEZA-accredited ecozones or IT parks scattered around the country, PEZA is your counterpart. If you're not tied to a specific zone but qualify under national investment priorities, BOI is your counterpart. One more thing worth knowing: the CREATE MORE Act (Republic Act No. 12066), in force since November 2024, largely harmonized the core incentive menu — including extending the maximum income tax holiday period to 17–27 years — across these investment promotion agencies (IPAs). In practice, that means the bigger question isn't which incentives you can get, but which agency you'll actually be registering and dealing with, which comes down to where your project is located.

## Who Is CDC President and CEO Agnes VST Devanadera?

Devanadera's background is useful context for assessing CDC's external credibility in this kind of engagement. She was appointed CDC President and CEO by President Bongbong Marcos in September 2022. Before that, she served as Government Corporate Counsel, Solicitor General, Secretary of Justice, Undersecretary of the Interior and Local Government, mayor of Sampaloc, Quezon (the town's first woman mayor), National President of the League of Municipalities of the Philippines, and Chairperson of the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC) from 2017 to 2022 under the Duterte administration. She is a graduate of Ateneo de Manila Law School.

Her long track record in legal and regulatory administration is relevant background for evaluating how CDC negotiates with international bodies and maintains institutional continuity.

## What This Means for Companies: Separating the Announcement From Future Impact

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">What can be said at this stage</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    This offer is a signal that Clark has some standing within the international economic-zone community and is drawing interest from industry bodies like WFZO. If Clark is featured on WFZO's platform and communication channels, that could raise its visibility among zone operators, investment-promotion officials, and international-organization contacts within that network. Combined with ICCA membership (2023) and the GCG Leadership Award (2025), this fits a pattern of CDC steadily building recognition, both domestically and internationally.
  </p>
</div>

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">What can't be said yet</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    At the same time, this is not a funded agreement or a specific investment deal — it's an offer, at this stage. WFZO itself does not provide direct funding or investment guarantees, so a fair assessment is that this partnership by itself is unlikely to directly change whether or on what terms a Japanese company can expand into Clark. What matters for companies evaluating Clark is to treat this kind of international-recognition news as a secondary confidence signal, while basing the actual expansion decision on concrete, individual factors — <a href="/en/articles/clark-rbelt-local-tax-2026/" class="text-accent-700 underline hover:text-accent-500">tax incentive eligibility</a>, talent availability, and the practical registration process with CDC.
  </p>
</div>

If there's one takeaway for companies evaluating expansion, it's this: news like this is a reasonable signal that Clark, as a zone, is not operating in a closed regional market but is actively working to build recognition within the international community of zone operators. That's worth factoring in — but it shouldn't be the deciding factor. Treat it as one input alongside the institutional and operational due diligence that actually determines whether an expansion works.
