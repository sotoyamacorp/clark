# PH-Japan Trade Hits a Record ₱1.27T ($22B), as JPEPA Revision Talks Advance

## Introduction

One thing that's easy to overlook when considering expansion into the Philippines is the Japan-Philippines Economic Partnership Agreement (JPEPA). About 18 years after it took effect in 2008, bilateral trade between the two countries reached a record ₱1.27 trillion (roughly $22 billion) in 2025. At the same time, talks to revise JPEPA itself — centered on auto tariffs — are now underway.

This article covers the latest in Philippines-Japan trade and investment relations, and what companies should know about JPEPA.

## PH-Japan Trade Hit a Record ₱1.27 Trillion ($22B) in 2025

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">"Investors don't ask for favors, only fairness, predictability, and speed"</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    Bilateral trade between the Philippines and Japan reached a record ₱1.27 trillion (roughly $22 billion) in 2025. At the 42nd Annual Joint Meeting of the Philippines-Japan Economic Cooperation Committee (PHILJEC) and the Japan-Philippines Economic Cooperation Committee (JPECC) on February 19, 2026 — attended by roughly 130 representatives from both countries — Philippine Executive Secretary Ralph Recto said, "The Philippines is ready to do more business with you... investors do not ask for favors, only fairness, predictability, and speed." He emphasized the government's push to make doing business in the Philippines faster and more reliable. Japanese investor confidence has reportedly been rising in sectors including renewable energy, transportation, and manufacturing, with Mitsubishi Motors' EV manufacturing plans and Tsuneishi Shipbuilding's involvement both drawing attention.
  </p>
</div>

---

## What Is JPEPA? In Force Since 2008, Eliminating Tariffs on 94% of Product Lines

<div class="border-l-4 border-navy-600 bg-navy-50 p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">The basic trade framework underpinning the PH-Japan relationship</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    JPEPA (the Japan-Philippines Economic Partnership Agreement) is a comprehensive bilateral free trade agreement that took effect in December 2008. The two countries agreed to eliminate or reduce tariffs on 94% of product lines, and the agreement covers not just goods but services trade, investment, and movement of people — a genuinely comprehensive framework. For Japanese companies operating in the Philippines, it's a foundational system worth understanding for the tariff benefits it provides.
  </p>
</div>

---

## Revision Talks Are Underway, Centered on Auto Tariffs

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">A displacement-based tariff structure that hasn't kept pace with the EV era</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    When JPEPA took effect, auto tariffs were structured around engine displacement: vehicles with 1.5-2.0 liter engines could enter tariff-free, while vehicles with 3.0-liter-plus engines — typically luxury models — were tariffed. That structure favored Japanese automakers at the time, but it's increasingly out of step with reality as electric vehicles gain ground. In February 2026, Japanese automakers reportedly asked the Philippine government for relief from a 20% import tariff, and Manila and Tokyo are now in talks to revise JPEPA.
  </p>
</div>

## Why This Matters for Companies

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Reading the government's "fairness, predictability, speed" message</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    Secretary Recto's remarks reflect what the Philippine government believes matters most to Japanese investment decisions: clear rules, coordinated government action, and decisions made fast enough not to miss opportunities. How the JPEPA revision talks play out will be a key variable shaping tariff-cost assumptions, particularly for companies in the automotive and manufacturing sectors. Companies planning to expand using existing JPEPA tariff preferences should keep an eye on how the revision negotiations progress.
  </p>
</div>
