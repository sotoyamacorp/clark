# The 2026 Philippine Energy Crisis: What Happened and Why It Matters

## Introduction

On this site, we've covered individual economic data points for the Philippines in 2026 — the peso's depreciation, slowing GDP growth. What these share is a common backdrop: an "energy crisis" that has been unfolding since February 2026. Triggered by escalating tensions in the Middle East, it grew serious enough that the Philippine government declared a national energy emergency in March.

This article covers how the crisis unfolded, its impact on the Philippine economy, the government's response, and what companies operating there should keep in mind.

## What Triggered It: The Strait of Hormuz Closure and the Philippines' Vulnerability

The 2026 Iran war led to the effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz — a critical oil shipping chokepoint — in late February 2026. Roughly 20% of the world's oil supply passes through the strait, and the disruption hit the Philippines directly.

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">A structural weakness: 98% of oil imports come from the Middle East</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    The Philippines imports an estimated 98% of its oil from the Middle East, making it one of the countries hit hardest by the disruption. As of March 20, 2026, national oil reserves had fallen from 55-57 days' worth to 45 days. By late May, inventory remained around 50 days, with supply concerns persisting.
  </p>
</div>

## The National Energy Emergency, Fuel Prices, and Blackouts

<div class="space-y-6 my-8">
  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">A national emergency declared March 24, 2026, via Executive Order 110</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      What officials initially characterized as "price disruption" deteriorated rapidly. On March 24, 2026, President Marcos declared a state of national energy emergency through Executive Order No. 110 — making the Philippines the first country to declare an energy emergency in response to the war.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Diesel was around ₱55 before the crisis, then topped ₱130 by March</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      Before the crisis began in late February 2026, the common retail price for diesel in Metro Manila was around ₱55 a liter. By March 24, 2026, it had exceeded ₱130 (about $2.64) per liter. Gasoline, which was in the ₱60s per liter before the crisis, surpassed ₱100 (about $2.03) per liter by the same date. LNG prices tripled and coal rose 30%. Of the Philippines' 14,485 gas stations nationwide, 425 had closed by March 27.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">What is a "rotating blackout," exactly?</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    A rotating blackout (or rotational brownout) is a way of managing insufficient power supply without leaving any single area without power indefinitely — outages are cycled through different areas on a schedule instead. In this case, grid operator NGCP declared a "red alert" for the Luzon grid and directed distributor Meralco to implement manual load dropping. Affected areas are rotated between 2pm and midnight, with each outage typically lasting 2-3 hours. It's an emergency measure meant to prevent a full grid collapse. On May 13-15, up to roughly 2 million people (across parts of Bulacan, Cavite, Laguna, Metro Manila, Quezon, and Rizal) were affected.
  </p>
</div>

<p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed mb-8">
The economic and social impact was broad. The Philippine Institute for Development Studies (PIDS) projected the crisis could push between 1.3 million and 3.1 million Filipinos into poverty. Tourist arrivals in Baguio dropped 40-50%, with hotel bookings down 30%. Roughly 40,000 overseas Filipino workers (OFWs) were stranded in Manila. Several regions — including Bangsamoro, Cagayan, Sorsogon, Baguio, and Zamboanga City — declared states of calamity.
</p>

## The Government's Response, and Where Things Stand as of July 2026

<div class="grid grid-cols-1 md:grid-cols-2 gap-6 my-12">
  <div class="bg-white border-2 border-gray-200 rounded-lg p-6 shadow-sm">
    <div class="text-3xl mb-3">💰</div>
    <h4 class="text-base font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">₱20 billion released from the Malampaya fund, excise taxes suspended</h4>
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-700 mb-4">₱20 billion (about $406.09 million) was released from the Malampaya natural gas fund for fuel procurement. On April 13, excise taxes on LPG and kerosene were removed, cutting prices by roughly ₱3.36-5.60 per liter. Republic Act 12316 extends this excise suspension through December 31, 2028.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="bg-white border-2 border-gray-200 rounded-lg p-6 shadow-sm">
    <div class="text-3xl mb-3">🛢️</div>
    <h4 class="text-base font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Diversifying supply toward Russia, Malaysia, and beyond</h4>
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-700 mb-4">Petron used a US sanctions waiver to order 700,000 barrels from Russia. PNOCEC arranged a 900,000-barrel shipment from Malaysia in April, as the country worked to diversify away from Middle East suppliers.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">DOE's specific measures: price adjustment caps, anti-profiteering monitoring, boosting coal</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    The national emergency declaration granted the Department of Energy (DOE) authority over fuel pricing. On April 20, 2026, Secretary Sharon Garin announced a uniform adjustment mechanism — rather than fixing prices outright, the DOE sets a minimum rollback and a maximum increase for each pricing cycle. This makes it easier to monitor individual oil companies for profiteering, with penalties for non-compliant firms ranging from three months to a year in prison and fines of ₱50,000 to ₱300,000. The DOE also moved to "temporarily" ramp up coal-fired power generation to help contain electricity price spikes amid disrupted gas supplies. These measures were bundled under a framework called UPLIFT (Unified Package for Livelihoods, Industry, Food, and Transport), which combines fuel subsidies, commuter assistance, and support for transport workers and small businesses.
  </p>
</div>

<p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed mb-8">
An Iran-US ceasefire was announced on April 8, 2026, briefly raising hopes the situation would stabilize. But in practice, fuel price volatility hadn't subsided even by July. Diesel rose as much as ₱3.57 per liter during the week of July 7-13, and by another ₱10.68 per liter — with kerosene up ₱11.77 — during the week of July 21-27, as prices spiked again each time tensions in the Middle East flared up. Trading on July 24 and 27 saw a modest pullback in oil prices amid progress in third-party negotiations involving China and Pakistan and a halt in Iran-US missile strikes, but the situation is far from resolved.
</p>

## How the Private Sector, Including Japanese Companies, Has Reacted

Alongside the government response, the private sector has been pursuing its own measures.

<div class="space-y-6 my-8">
  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Business groups backed the emergency declaration, but warn of damage to smaller firms</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      The Philippine Chamber of Commerce and Industry (PCCI) called the national emergency declaration "timely and necessary to mitigate the economic impact of the global energy supply disruption." At the same time, PCCI flagged that continued high energy costs have become a "primary bottleneck" to growth, particularly for the micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs) that make up 99.5% of Philippine businesses, warning that "continuous fuel price hikes lead to thinner margins, forcing businesses to either cut personnel or risk insolvency."
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Major business groups jointly pledged renewable energy investment and efficiency measures</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      Five major business organizations — the Management Association of the Philippines, the Makati Business Club, PCCI, the Federation of Philippine Industries, and the Federation of Filipino-Chinese Chambers of Commerce and Industry — jointly expressed willingness to invest in renewable energy, particularly solar power, and pledged "aggressive energy-saving measures" across corporate and industrial facilities. In practice, many manufacturers have shifted production to off-peak hours and adopted more flexible work arrangements to keep factories running.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Japanese investment appetite has held up: PEZA ran a Japan mission in late April</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      Despite the ongoing crisis, Japanese companies' investment appetite for the Philippines doesn't appear to have significantly retreated. The Philippine Economic Zone Authority (PEZA) ran an outreach mission to Japan from April 27 to May 1, 2026, aimed at expanding partnerships in advanced manufacturing, electronics, agri-technology, health technology, construction services, and professional advisory services. Shin-Etsu Chemical, which already operates the Philippines' largest magnet manufacturing facility, is reportedly considering further investment amid growing global demand for EV components. Japanese companies are also already embedded in the Philippines' energy supply itself — Tokyo Gas and Prime Infra operate an LNG receiving terminal in Batangas, while Sumitomo Corporation, J-Power, and Aboitiz Power jointly run the CBK Pumped Storage Power Plant in Laguna.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

## What It Means for Companies, and What to Prepare For

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Treat this as an operational risk, not just a cost issue</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    This crisis isn't purely a matter of higher fuel costs. It carries real operational risk: production line disruptions from rotational blackouts, logistics bottlenecks from closed gas stations, and higher travel/shipping costs from reduced flights and rising fuel surcharges. It's also worth understanding as backdrop for economic indicators we've covered elsewhere — <a href="/en/articles/philippines-peso-inflation-bsp-2026/" class="text-accent-700 underline hover:text-accent-500">the peso's record low and BSP's pivot to rate hikes in July 2026</a>, and <a href="/en/articles/philippines-gdp-growth-q1-2026/" class="text-accent-700 underline hover:text-accent-500">Q1 2026 GDP growth slowing to 2.8%</a> — both of which this crisis has factored into. Whether you're already operating in the Philippines or considering expansion, this is a good moment to revisit backup power capacity, fuel reserves, and business continuity planning.
  </p>
</div>
