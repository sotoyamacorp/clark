# Clark's Industrial Land Market: An Established Developer Expands, What It Signals

## Introduction

If you're evaluating factory or warehouse space in Clark, one thing you probably want to know is: how strong is demand for industrial land, really? On June 23, 2026, Berthaphil — known as the first investor in the Clark Freeport Zone — signed a supplemental lease agreement with CDC (Clark Development Corporation) for an additional 4.1 hectares.

This article breaks down what this expansion involves, what it signals about demand for industrial land in Clark, and what it means for companies considering a move there.

## Who Berthaphil is, and what this expansion involves

Founded in 1995, Berthaphil is an industrial real estate developer known as the first investor in the Clark Freeport Zone. Operating across Clark's economic, freeport, and airport zones, it runs eight business parks, leases space to more than 300 tenant companies, and supports over 20,000 jobs.

### What country is Berthaphil from?

Berthaphil was founded in 1995 by Andre Hercz and family. The company's origin traces back to then-President Fidel Ramos (in office 1992-1998) personally inviting the Hercz family to become the Clark Freeport Zone's first investor. Berthaphil's official website doesn't disclose its ownership structure or shareholders' nationality.

That said, this isn't a major practical concern for companies exploring Clark. Enterprises registering in the Clark Freeport Zone can be up to 100% foreign-owned under CDC (Clark Development Corporation) rules. In other words, whether Berthaphil is foreign- or locally-owned, it operates within the same regulatory framework available to any registered investor — including Japanese companies, which can likewise enter at a high equity stake.

### Who else is playing in this space

Berthaphil isn't the only developer active around Clark's industrial and office real estate. Major Philippine developers like SM Prime, Filinvest Land, and Megaworld also operate office towers and other facilities within the Clark Freeport Zone.

Filinvest, in particular, operates at a much larger scale in the area. It holds a 288-hectare landbank in the adjacent New Clark City (in Tarlac province, under BCDA — a separate zone from the Clark Freeport Zone; see our [Pax Silica article](/en/articles/clark-pax-silica-ai-hub/) for that distinction), of which 120 hectares are being developed as the Filinvest Innovation Park industrial hub. It offers lots ranging from standard 1-hectare parcels up to 11-hectare "mega lots," plus ready-built factory units in three sizes (2,500, 4,000, and 5,000 square meters) — a somewhat different footprint and scale than Berthaphil, whose Clark Freeport Zone holdings now total around 45 hectares.

For companies exploring Clark, the takeaway is that it's not a one-developer market. Your options span both the zone you choose (Clark Freeport Zone vs. New Clark City) and which developer you work with within it.

## What this means for Japanese companies

**For companies exploring factory, warehouse, or office space in Clark, the fact that a major established developer keeps expanding is a useful signal on both the demand and supply side.** On the demand side, it points to continued strong appetite for space from manufacturing, BPO, and logistics companies in the area. On the supply side, it's a reminder that the best-located parcels may fill up first — if you're seriously evaluating Clark, it's worth starting your research and site visits early.

It's also worth noting that registered developers like Berthaphil, operating within the Clark Freeport Zone, are well versed in CDC's leasing process and zone infrastructure. Beyond negotiating directly with CDC, moving into an existing business park run by one of these developers (via sublease) can be a faster path to getting operations up and running.
