# Philippine Green Lane investments reach PHP351 billion

## Introduction

Permits across national agencies and local governments can become a critical path for large Philippine investments. Created under Executive Order No.18, Green Lanes use the Board of Investments' One-Stop Action Center for Strategic Investments (OSAC-SI) to coordinate and expedite strategic projects.

## H1 2026 results

BOI reported 17 certifications from January to June 2026, with PHP351.02 billion of investment and 39,368 expected jobs. Renewable energy accounted for 14 projects and PHP349.38 billion, or 99.53% of the value. Two food-security projects represented PHP980 million.

Since the initiative began in February 2023, 239 projects worth PHP6.32 trillion and approximately 421,000 expected jobs have been certified. Renewable energy accounts for 183 projects, 76.57% of the total, and 85.68% of certified project cost.

| Indicator | H1 2026 | Since launch |
|---|---:|---:|
| Certified projects | 17 | 239 |
| Project cost | PHP351.02bn | PHP6.32tn |
| Expected jobs | 39,368 | About 421,000 |

The H1 list included a PHP670-million industrial-zone project by Una Grande Tierra Development Corporation in Pampanga, making the program relevant to the industrial ecosystem around Clark and Central Luzon.

## Where Green Lane sits in the longer investment trend

Beyond the headline H1 2026 numbers, it helps to see how Green Lane has grown since launch and where it fits within the Philippines' broader investment trend.

### Cumulative Green Lane certifications over time

| Point in time | Cumulative certified projects | Cumulative investment |
|---|---:|---:|
| February 2023 | — | Program launch |
| Late January 2024 | — | About PHP1.2tn |
| December 26, 2024 | 176 | PHP4.54tn |
| December 31, 2025 | — | PHP6.11tn |
| End of H1 2026 (June 2026) | 239 | PHP6.32tn |

Cumulative certified investment has passed PHP6 trillion roughly three years after launch. The mix remains heavily concentrated in renewable energy (85.68% of cumulative value), so how far the program broadens into other sectors is worth watching.

### BOI-approved investments over time (nationwide, past 7 years)

Green Lane only fast-tracks permits for a subset of strategic projects; it is a small slice of the total investment BOI approves each year. Placed against the broader trend, the picture looks like this:

| Year | BOI-approved investments (nationwide) | Note |
|---|---:|---|
| 2019 | About PHP1.14tn | Pre-pandemic |
| 2020 | PHP1.02tn | Down 11% y/y, then the second-highest on record |
| 2021 | PHP655.4bn | Pandemic-driven trough |
| 2022 | PHP729.0bn | Up 11% y/y, recovery underway |
| 2023 | PHP1.26tn | Then an all-time high |
| 2024 | PHP1.62tn | BOI's highest level in its 57-year history |
| 2025 | PHP1.56tn | Second consecutive year above PHP1.5tn, second-highest on record |

Read together, Green Lane (launched February 2023) emerged during the 2021-2022 trough and has since helped drive the recovery by fast-tracking permits for large renewable-energy-led projects.

## What Green Lane changes

OSAC-SI serves as the single point of entry and assigns coordination for permits across agencies and LGUs. EO18 calls for agency Green Lanes, simultaneous processing and sector investor guidebooks.

It does not remove substantive environmental, land-use, construction or grid requirements. Certification should not be read as final approval of every permit or a guarantee that the announced investment will be completed.

## What the SIPP is

"SIPP" comes up repeatedly in Green Lane eligibility criteria. It stands for the Strategic Investment Priority Plan, a list of activities eligible for tax incentives, drafted by DTI-BOI and approved by the President. It is revised roughly every few years, and a 2026 version has already been approved.

The SIPP sorts eligible activities into three tiers.

- **Tier I**: modern agriculture, advanced construction technology, mobile healthcare, ecological zones, carbon capture, waste-to-value and circular-economy projects, and forest management for carbon credits.
- **Tier II**: sectors the government treats as strategic industries, including defense-related services, desalination, EV infrastructure, sustainable aviation fuel, and critical-minerals processing.
- **Tier III**: activities involving R&D that meaningfully raises value-added, productivity or efficiency; patent commercialization; highly technical manufacturing; and fields seen as critical to structural transformation, such as cybersecurity, AI and data centers. The 2026 version newly named AI, quantum technology, cybersecurity and biotechnology as frontier priorities.

How the SIPP relates to Green Lane:

- Green Lane eligibility requires a project to fall under priorities set by the Philippine Development Plan or the SIPP, and to be judged "of national significance" or "highly desirable."
- The SIPP itself is a separate framework from Green Lane's permit-facilitation role — it defines which activities are eligible for tax incentives such as income-tax reductions under the CREATE MORE law.
- In short, the SIPP determines which activities can qualify for tax incentives, while Green Lane is a separate layer that determines how quickly permits move for strategic projects, including SIPP-eligible ones.

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8"><h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">SIPP eligibility is not the same as receiving tax incentives</h4><p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed">Falling under a SIPP priority sector does not automatically grant tax incentives — actually receiving them requires a separate registration and review with BOI or PEZA. Treat SIPP eligibility, Green Lane certification and incentive registration as three separate decisions to manage.</p></div>

## Eligibility and documents

Strategic investments align with the Philippine Development Plan, SIPP (see previous section) or related national priorities. Cumulative certifications cover renewable energy, digital infrastructure, PPPs, infrastructure and water, food security, manufacturing and pharmaceuticals.

The 2026 BOI guidance identifies these core documents:

1. Green Lane application form
2. CEO-signed letter of intent to the DTI Secretary
3. Company profile
4. Project brief covering technology, jobs, impacts, suppliers, timetable and funding
5. SEC registration and latest GIS
6. List of permits being applied for
7. Secretary's Certificate for the authorized representative
8. Sector documents such as a DOE service contract for renewable energy

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8"><h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Build a permit map first</h4><p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed">Track each permit, responsible agency, prerequisites, filing date, target date and owner. This is not only an application requirement; it lets management and the assigned Green Lane officer identify bottlenecks early.</p></div>

## Implications for Japanese investors

Green Lane is most relevant to projects such as large manufacturing, data centers, renewable energy and food value chains that depend on multiple agencies. A small sales subsidiary or ordinary office does not automatically qualify as strategic.

Green Lane certification and investment-incentive registration have different purposes. Manage corporate and foreign-ownership rules, incentive registration, Green Lane, sector licenses and LGU permits as separate decision lines.
