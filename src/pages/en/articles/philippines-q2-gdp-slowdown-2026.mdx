# Q2 GDP Slows to 2.3%, Weakest Since 2009: H2 Target Rises to 4.4%

## Introduction

Just three days after we covered [the gap between the government's H2 confidence and the World Bank's caution](/en/articles/philippines-h2-growth-target-2026/), fresh data landed that underscores exactly that gap. On August 7, 2026, the Philippine Statistics Authority reported second-quarter real GDP growth of just 2.3% — the weakest reading since the pandemic era.

This article covers what's behind the Q2 GDP print and how it's reshaped the outlook for the second half.

## Q2 Growth Was 2.3%, the Weakest Since 2009 Excluding the Pandemic

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Further deceleration from 5.5% a year earlier and 2.8% in Q1</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    Data released by the Philippine Statistics Authority on August 7, 2026 showed real GDP grew just 2.3% year-on-year in the second quarter (April-June). That's a sharp deceleration from 5.5% growth in the same period last year, and even a further slowdown from [Q1's already five-year-low 2.8%](/en/articles/philippines-gdp-growth-q1-2026/) — the weakest reading since 2009 outside the pandemic. The impact of oil prices tied to Middle East tensions appears to be compounding with each successive quarter.
  </p>
</div>

## What DEPDev Is: Formerly NEDA, the Agency Behind National Development Planning

<div class="border-l-4 border-navy-600 bg-navy-50 p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Renamed in 2026; also the source of the quarterly GDP figures</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    DEPDev (the Department of Economy, Planning and Development) is the successor to the agency long known as NEDA (the National Economic and Development Authority), renamed and elevated to full department status in 2026. It's responsible for drafting national development plans and medium-term growth strategy, and it's also the source of the quarterly GDP data covered in this article. Secretary Arsenio Balisacan, an economist by training, leads the agency and speaks regularly on the administration's broader economic policy.
  </p>
</div>

---

## Industry Contracted 2.4%, Turning Negative

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Services and agriculture held up</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    By sector, services grew 4.5% and agriculture, forestry, and fishing grew 2.7% — both holding relatively firm. Industry, however, contracted 2.4% year-on-year, turning negative. [The pullback in public construction tied to the flood-control corruption scandal](/en/articles/philippines-worldbank-growth-outlook-2026/) appears to be weighing on the broader industry sector, including construction.
  </p>
</div>

---

## Balisacan: Weak Domestic Demand, But a Bright Spot in Semiconductor Exports

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Contracting investment and slowing household spending, alongside resilient manufacturing and exports</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    [DEPDev (formerly NEDA)](/en/articles/philippines-h2-growth-target-2026/) Secretary Arsenio Balisacan said domestic demand remained subdued. Investment kept contracting amid weak public construction, while household spending also slowed on higher inflation, job losses, and [slower remittance growth tied to Middle East tensions](/en/articles/philippines-ofw-remittances-2026/). On the brighter side, he pointed to a recovery in agricultural output, stronger manufacturing activity, and improving exports — particularly semiconductor shipments supported by global AI-related demand.
  </p>
</div>

---

## Semiconductor Exports Run Through Foreign Players Like Texas Instruments and Amkor

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Electronics make up about 59% of total exports, and semiconductors nearly 80% of that</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    The companies driving the Philippines' semiconductor and electronics exports are mainly foreign-owned manufacturers with plants inside PEZA-registered ecozones (Cebu, Calabarzon, Clark, and others) — names like Texas Instruments Philippines, Amkor Technology Philippines, Integrated Micro-Electronics (IMI, part of the Philippine-based Ayala Group), ROHM Electronics Philippines, and ON Semiconductor Philippines. What the Philippines actually exports is less about finished semiconductor chips and more about back-end packaging and testing of semiconductor components and devices — the front-end wafer fabrication for AI chips like GPUs largely happens in Taiwan and South Korea, with the Philippines occupying the back-end assembly and test segment of that supply chain. In terms of composition, electronics exports totaled $49.64 billion in 2025, or 58.81% of the Philippines' total exports, and semiconductors (integrated circuits and related components/devices) made up 76.6% of that electronics figure (roughly $21 billion).
  </p>
</div>

## The Growth Needed in H2 Rose From 3.7% to 4.4%

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">The bar to hit the target just got higher</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    Per Balisacan, hitting the low end of the government's full-year target range (3.5-4.5%) now requires average growth of 4.4% across the second half (Q3-Q4). [As of August 5, Balisacan had put that figure at 3.7%](/en/articles/philippines-h2-growth-target-2026/) — meaning the bar rose by 0.7 percentage points in just three days, as Q2's actual result came in below expectations. The government's "much improved second half" scenario now rests on a tougher starting point.
  </p>
</div>

## Why This Matters for Companies

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Time to further discount the government's optimistic scenario</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    Whether the government's H2 recovery scenario plays out still hinges on how quickly catch-up spending materializes, but the fact that the required growth rate jumped 0.7 points in three days raises real questions about the scenario's feasibility. At the same time, the bright spot in semiconductor and AI-related export demand means the impact varies significantly by sector. It's worth mentally sorting your exposure: manufacturing and export-oriented businesses face a tailwind, while public infrastructure and construction-related businesses face a headwind.
  </p>
</div>
