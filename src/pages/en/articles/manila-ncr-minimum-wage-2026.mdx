# Metro Manila's Minimum Wage Rises ₱85 in Two Stages: What It Means for Labor Cost Planning

## Introduction

If your hiring plan is built on the assumption that "labor in the Philippines is cheap," it's worth a second look. On June 23, 2026, the Regional Tripartite Wages and Productivity Board of the National Capital Region (RTWPB-NCR) signed Wage Order No. NCR-27, raising the minimum wage in Metro Manila by ₱85 per day, in two stages.

This article breaks down exactly what changed and what it means for Japanese companies planning around labor costs.

## What Wage Order NCR-27 says

Under Wage Order No. NCR-27, signed by RTWPB-NCR on June 23, 2026, private-sector minimum wage earners in Metro Manila get a total increase of ₱85 per day, rolled out in two tranches.

### Timeline and exact figures

<div class="space-y-6 my-8">
  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Tranche 1: effective July 25, 2026</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      A ₱60/day increase. Non-agriculture sector wages went from ₱695 to ₱755; agriculture, retail/service businesses with 15 or fewer employees, and manufacturers with fewer than 10 employees went from ₱658 to ₱718.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Tranche 2: effective January 20, 2027</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      An additional ₱25/day. Non-agriculture wages rise from ₱755 to ₱780; the smaller-business tier rises from ₱718 to ₱743.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Who's affected</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      The Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) estimates this directly affects around 1.1 million private-sector workers in Metro Manila.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

## What this means for Japanese companies

**If your expansion plan rests on the general assumption that "labor is cheap in the Philippines," you need to build in variation by region and timing.** If your cost estimates assume hiring in Metro Manila specifically, we'd recommend re-running the numbers against the wage floor that took effect on July 25, 2026.

Regions outside Metro Manila, Clark included, haven't seen as sharp an increase so far. That said, since DOLE has signaled it's reviewing regional wage levels more broadly, it's worth continuing to track wage trends outside the capital too. Labor cost is only one input — weighing it against productivity and retention when choosing a location still matters just as much.
