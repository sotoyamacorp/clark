# Marcos Signs ₱60B "EVIS" Incentive Program for Local EV Manufacturing; Mitsubishi Responds

## Introduction

On July 29, 2026, President Marcos signed Executive Order (EO) No. 121, formally establishing the Electric Vehicle Incentive Strategy (EVIS) program to support local electric vehicle (EV) manufacturing. The program carries up to ₱60 billion in fiscal support — and Mitsubishi Motors has already responded with a concrete investment plan. This article covers what the program involves, and what it means for Japanese automotive-related companies.

## Up to ₱60 Billion Total, Split Among Up to 4 Companies at ₱15 Billion Each

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">The Board of Investments (BOI) oversees the program</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    President Marcos signed Executive Order (EO) No. 121 on July 29, 2026 (published in the Official Gazette on July 30), formally establishing the Electric Vehicle Incentive Strategy (EVIS) program. Its stated goals are to attract investment in EV manufacturing, strengthen the domestic production base, create jobs, reduce dependence on imported fuel, and position the Philippines as a major player in the global EV supply chain. Total fiscal support is capped at ₱60 billion, available to up to four companies (models), each eligible for up to ₱15 billion. The Board of Investments (BOI) oversees implementation and is required to report annual results to the Office of the President.
  </p>
</div>

## Two Incentive Types: Fixed Investment Support (FIS) and Production Volume Incentive (PVI)

<div class="border-l-4 border-navy-600 bg-navy-50 p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">BEVs get up to 40% support, HEVs and others up to 30%</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    The program's incentives come in two forms. The first, Fixed Investment Support (FIS), subsidizes a portion of eligible capital expenditure: battery electric vehicle (BEV) manufacturing projects and their parts qualify for up to 40% of eligible capex, while hybrid (HEV), plug-in hybrid (PHEV), and fuel-cell vehicle manufacturing qualify for up to 30%. The second, Production Volume Incentive (PVI), pays out up to 12% of the ex-factory unit price (capped at ₱200,000 per unit), contingent on meeting a minimum planned production volume of 10,000 units.
  </p>
</div>

## A 10-Year Window From Registration, With a 10,000-Unit Minimum Production Requirement

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Complete vehicle production and mandatory parts manufacturing are also required</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    Incentives apply for 10 years from the registration date of the enrolled EV model (or its parts). To qualify for the Production Volume Incentive specifically, manufacturers must produce complete EV units, manufacture mandatory parts and components, and commit to a minimum production capacity of 10,000 units annually. The program is deliberately designed to require genuine local production and parts manufacturing — not simple knockdown assembly.
  </p>
</div>

## Mitsubishi Motors Pledged a ₱7 Billion HEV Local Production Investment

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Pilot production targeted for Q3 2027 at its Laguna plant</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    Mitsubishi Motors Philippines Corporation (MMPC) was quick to respond to EO 121's signing. The company announced a ₱7 billion investment to produce a new HEV model at its existing plant in Santa Rosa City, Laguna, aligning the plan with the EVIS program. The investment covers local production of the model as well as assembly of batteries and other components. MMPC is targeting pilot production in Q3 2027, with full commercial production expected by mid-2028, pending approval of its EVIS application. It's a notable example of a Japanese automaker announcing a concrete investment plan within days of the program's signing.
  </p>
</div>

## Four-Wheel Market: Toyota Above 50%, Mitsubishi Around 16%

<div class="border-l-4 border-navy-600 bg-navy-50 p-6 shadow-sm my-8"><h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">The context is a roughly 490,000-unit new-vehicle market, not EVs alone</h4><p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">Industry tallies put 2025 Philippine new-vehicle sales at roughly 490,000 units. Among CAMPI-TMA members in May 2026, Toyota held more than 50% and Mitsubishi around 16%. Mitsubishi's Xpander, Mirage/G4, L300 and Montero Sport fit family, commercial and multi-passenger demand, backed by a long local manufacturing and dealer presence. Its ₱7 billion HEV plan is therefore the electrification move of an established second-place-scale brand, not a greenfield experiment.</p></div>

## Two Wheels: A Market More Than Three Times Larger Than Cars

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8"><h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">The five major brands sold 1.72 million units in FY2024; Honda alone exceeds one million</h4><p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">MDPPA members Honda, Yamaha, Kawasaki, Suzuki and TVS sold about 1.723 million motorcycles from April 2024 to March 2025. Honda recorded 1.041 million retail sales in calendar 2025. Motorcycles are commuting, delivery and household mobility infrastructure, so zero tariffs for e-motorcycles and e-bikes affect logistics and family budgets as much as decarbonization. Vehicle class still matters: light e-bikes and road-going electric motorcycles face different registration, licensing and road-access rules.</p></div>

## EV Prices Are Expected to Fall 6-12%, Up to ₱200,000 per Unit

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">China's BYD currently holds over 70% of the Philippine EV market</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    Department of Energy (DOE) estimates suggest the cost-structure changes under EVIS could reduce EV prices by 6-12%, translating to savings of up to ₱200,000 per vehicle depending on the model. The Philippine EV market reached $3.4 billion in 2025, and in Q1 2026, xEV (electrified vehicle) sales grew 36.2% year-on-year, with BEVs up 122.9% and PHEVs surging 924.6%. That said, China's BYD currently dominates the market with over 70% share (26,122 units sold in 2025, up 446% year-on-year) — and EVIS appears partly designed to shift that import-heavy structure by building out domestic and foreign-invested local production capacity.
  </p>
</div>

## Why This Matters for Companies

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Opportunities may extend beyond automakers to parts and materials suppliers</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    The Philippines' automotive manufacturing capacity is still modest compared to other major ASEAN producers like Thailand and Indonesia (roughly 150,000 units a year), but EVIS is a clear policy signal aimed at changing that. With the Department of Finance, DTI, and Department of Energy all pushing the program together, and Mitsubishi's quick response as a proof point, it's becoming a genuinely realistic option for Japanese automakers. Beyond the automakers themselves, battery, parts, and materials suppliers may see new opportunities tied to enrolled manufacturers' investment decisions — worth watching as more companies apply and register under the program.
  </p>
</div>
