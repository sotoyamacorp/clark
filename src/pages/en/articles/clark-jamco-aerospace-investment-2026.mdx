# Japan's Jamco Invests ₱600M in Clark, Shifting Final Assembly From Japan

## Introduction

Adding to [Clark's growing aerospace cluster — the AeroDistrict plan and Lufthansa Technik's MRO expansion](/en/articles/clark-aerodistrict-aerospace-hub-2026/) — comes a fresh sign of Japanese companies' presence there. Jamco Philippines, the local subsidiary of Tokyo-headquartered aircraft interiors major JAMCO Corporation, is investing ₱600 million in Clark Aviation Capital — and shifting final assembly work previously done in Japan over to Clark.

This article covers the investment and what it means for Japanese companies.

## ₱600 Million Invested Over Three Years, Targeting Full Operations by 2028

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">A formal lease agreement signed with CIAC</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    Jamco Philippines Inc. (JPI), a wholly owned subsidiary of Tokyo-based aircraft parts major JAMCO Corporation, announced it will invest at least ₱600 million (roughly $9.74 million) over the next three years in its expansion project at Clark Aviation Capital. On June 23, 2026, JPI and the Clark International Airport Corporation (CIAC) formally signed a lease agreement, with CIAC President and CEO Joseph Alcazar and JPI President Giovanni Atzei putting pen to paper. The expanded facility is targeting full operations by 2028.
  </p>
</div>

## Who Jamco Is: Founded 1955, a Major Interiors Supplier to Airbus and Boeing

<div class="border-l-4 border-navy-600 bg-navy-50 p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Sole supplier for parts of the Boeing 787</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    JAMCO Corporation is a publicly listed company (Tokyo Stock Exchange Prime, ticker 7408) headquartered in Tachikawa, Tokyo. It was founded in 1955 as C. Itoh Aircraft Maintenance and Engineering Co., Ltd., renamed New Japan Aircraft Maintenance Co., Ltd. in 1970, and took its current name, JAMCO Corporation, in 1988. Its four core business lines are aircraft interiors (galleys, lavatories, seats), aircraft seats, aircraft components, and aircraft maintenance. It's a leading supplier of premium galleys, lavatories, and seats for first- and business-class cabins to Airbus and Boeing, and is the sole supplier of the galley, lavatory, bar unit, and flight deck bulkhead door for the Boeing 787 specifically.
  </p>
</div>

## What CIAC Is: A Government Land Developer, Not an Investment Promotion Agency

<div class="border-l-4 border-navy-600 bg-navy-50 p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Not PEZA, CDC, or BOI — CIAC is the landlord for the airport area</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    CIAC (Clark International Airport Corporation) is a non-chartered government-owned and controlled corporation (GOCC) under the Bases Conversion and Development Authority (BCDA), operating under the policy supervision of the Department of Transportation (DOTr). Beyond running the airport itself, CIAC also develops and leases out the 2,367-hectare government-owned Clark Aviation Capital area surrounding it. The key distinction worth understanding: CIAC is not an investment promotion agency (IPA) like PEZA, CDC, or the Board of Investments (BOI) that grants tax incentives — it functions as the landlord for the airport-area land. Jamco's existing production site (at Philexcel Business Park, established 2011) sits inside a PEZA-registered ecozone, and its tax incentives likely stem from that PEZA registration, but this new ₱600 million expansion is a direct lease with CIAC rather than a fresh IPA registration (neither CIAC's nor Jamco's official announcements state this explicitly — this is an inference based on Philexcel Business Park being a PEZA-registered ecozone). For companies weighing Clark, it's worth understanding that "Clark" isn't just the CDC-administered Clark Freeport Zone — CIAC's Clark Aviation Capital is a separate land parcel with a separate point of contact.
  </p>
</div>

## Shifting From Partial Assembly in Japan to Full Final Assembly in Clark

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">A move that amounts to relocating production capability</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    What stands out about this expansion is that it involves transitioning partial assembly work previously done in Japan into full final assembly in Clark. This isn't simply scaling up — it's a genuine shift of the production process itself to Clark, and a concrete sign that Japanese companies are starting to position Clark as a more upstream production base within their supply chains.
  </p>
</div>

---

## Workforce to Grow From 450 to as Many as 1,400, Shipping Direct to Airbus and Boeing

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">15 years in Clark, supporting global supply chains</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    JPI has operated in Clark for 15 years, supporting the global supply chains of the world's two largest aircraft manufacturers, Airbus and Boeing. Finished interior products ship directly to Airbus's facility in Toulouse, France, and to Boeing's facilities in South Carolina and Seattle, Washington. With this expansion, the workforce is projected to grow from roughly 450 employees today to as many as 1,400 once complete. Combined with [Lufthansa Technik's MRO expansion covered earlier](/en/articles/clark-aerodistrict-aerospace-hub-2026/), it's clear Clark's aerospace cluster keeps getting deeper.
  </p>
</div>

## Why This Matters for Companies

<div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm my-8">
  <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">Clark's aerospace cluster keeps getting deeper</h4>
  <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
    Jamco's investment is a good example of a Japanese company with 15 years of history in Clark going deeper still. For Japanese companies in aerospace or precision manufacturing weighing an expansion, "why is an existing Japanese company expanding its Clark footprint" is a useful lens for evaluating the opportunity. Taken together with Clark International Airport, the [AeroDistrict plan](/en/articles/clark-aerodistrict-aerospace-hub-2026/), and [FedEx's logistics investment](/en/articles/clark-fedex-southeast-asia-hub-2026/), Clark is emerging not just as a manufacturing base but as a genuinely multi-layered aviation cluster spanning operations, maintenance, cargo, and parts manufacturing.
