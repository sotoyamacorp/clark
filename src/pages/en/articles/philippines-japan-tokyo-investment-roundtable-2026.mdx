# President Marcos Secures $3.4B in Investment Pledges from Japanese Firms in Tokyo

## Introduction

Wondering how seriously the Philippine government is courting Japanese investment right now? On May 27, 2026, at a high-level business roundtable in Tokyo, President Marcos secured $3.4 billion (roughly ₱210 billion) in investment pledges from major Japanese companies.

This article covers what happened at the roundtable and what it means for Japanese companies weighing a move into the Philippines.

## What the Tokyo Business Roundtable involved

President Marcos made a four-day visit to Japan marking the 70th anniversary of the normalization of Japan-Philippines diplomatic relations. The high-level business roundtable at Tokyo's Imperial Hotel was part of that trip.

## A unified government approach centered on trade and tourism

At the roundtable, President Marcos emphasized that "trade and tourism will be the most critical growth engines" for the Philippine economy, and outlined a unified government approach led jointly by the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) and the Department of Tourism (DOT). He framed the 70th anniversary of diplomatic normalization as "a new chapter of deeper economic integration and shared growth," pledging continued progress on institutional efficiency and investment stability.

**Notably, the announcement didn't specify which economic zones or cities these investments will actually land in.** As we've covered before, Clark has an established track record as a hub for manufacturing, IT-BPO, and logistics investment from Japanese companies (see our pieces on [Clark's history and value](/en/articles/clark-history-and-value/) and [demand for industrial land in Clark](/en/articles/clark-industrial-real-estate-2026/)), so momentum around smart manufacturing and tourism infrastructure investment could well spill over into Clark and other major economic zones. That said, whether any Clark-specific projects are part of this particular pledge is unconfirmed at this stage — worth watching for follow-up reporting.

## What this means for Japanese companies

**Even companies that weren't at the roundtable can take something useful from this.** First, it's a clear signal that the Philippine government is investing sustained, head-of-state-level effort into courting Japanese investment. Individual investment decisions still come down to each company's own circumstances, but stronger government-to-government ties are a useful data point when weighing the durability of institutional stability and incentive programs for companies already operating there.

The range of industries represented — manufacturing, trading, energy, retail, aviation — also suggests the Philippine government isn't targeting a narrow set of sectors, but casting a wide net for Japanese investment. If your company's industry overlaps with these priority areas, this is a reasonable moment to step up information-gathering through channels like JETRO, the Philippine Embassy, or DTI.
