# President Marcos Secures $3.4B in Investment Pledges from Japanese Firms in Tokyo

## Introduction

Wondering how seriously the Philippine government is courting Japanese investment right now? From May 26-29, 2026, President Marcos traveled to Japan as a State Guest. As part of that visit, a high-level business roundtable in Tokyo secured $3.4 billion (roughly ₱210 billion) in investment pledges from major Japanese companies.

This article covers what the State Visit designation actually means, what happened at the roundtable, and what it means for Japanese companies weighing a move into the Philippines.

## An unusually high honor: traveling to Japan as a "State Guest"

The first thing worth understanding is that this wasn't just a business trip — it was an official visit made at the invitation of the Japanese government, with Marcos received as a state guest.

<div class="space-y-6 my-8">
  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">1. The Philippines' first state guest visit to Japan since 2015</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      From May 26-29, 2026, President Marcos and the First Lady visited Japan as state guests at the invitation of the Japanese government — the first such visit by a Philippine president since 2015. It included a welcome ceremony and state banquet with Emperor Naruhito and Empress Masako. Marcos was conferred Japan's highest honor, the Grand Cordon of the Supreme Order of the Chrysanthemum, and the First Lady received the Grand Cordon of the Order of the Precious Crown.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">2. A summit meeting with Prime Minister Takaichi Sanae</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      On May 28, Marcos held a summit with PM Takaichi Sanae, issuing a joint statement deepening the two countries' "Strengthened Strategic Partnership." 2026 also marks the 70th anniversary of the normalization of Japan-Philippines diplomatic relations.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="border-l-4 border-accent-500 bg-white p-6 shadow-sm">
    <h4 class="font-bold text-gray-900 mb-3">3. The business roundtable was part of the State Visit itself</h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed">
      The Tokyo Business Roundtable was jointly organized by the Philippine Department of Tourism (DOT) and Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) as part of the four-day State Visit. Head-of-state diplomacy and economic outreach were built into the same itinerary — that's a notable feature of this trip.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

## Marcos's 4th trip to Japan since taking office

Philippine presidents serve a single, non-renewable 6-year term. Since taking office in June 2022, Marcos has visited Japan at least four times.

## A unified government approach centered on trade and tourism

At the roundtable, President Marcos emphasized that "trade and tourism will be the most critical growth engines" for the Philippine economy, and outlined a unified government approach led jointly by the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) and the Department of Tourism (DOT). He framed the 70th anniversary of diplomatic normalization as "a new chapter of deeper economic integration and shared growth," pledging continued progress on institutional efficiency and investment stability.

**Notably, the announcement didn't specify which economic zones or cities these investments will actually land in.** As we've covered before, Clark has an established track record as a hub for manufacturing, IT-BPO, and logistics investment from Japanese companies (see our pieces on [Clark's history and value](/en/articles/clark-history-and-value/) and [demand for industrial land in Clark](/en/articles/clark-industrial-real-estate-2026/)), so momentum around smart manufacturing and tourism infrastructure investment could well spill over into Clark and other major economic zones. That said, whether any Clark-specific projects are part of this particular pledge is unconfirmed at this stage — worth watching for follow-up reporting.

## What this means for Japanese companies

**The real takeaway from this whole sequence of events is the pattern of government support paired with Japanese business collaboration.** Top-tier diplomacy — a state guest reception, an audience with the Emperor and Empress, a summit with the Prime Minister — was built into the same four-day itinerary as a roundtable of chairman- and president-level executives. That says the Philippine government treats courting Japanese investment as both economic policy and a pillar of diplomacy.

Even companies that weren't at the roundtable can take something useful from this. It's a clear signal that the Philippine government is investing sustained, head-of-state-level effort into courting Japanese investment. Individual investment decisions still come down to each company's own circumstances, but stronger government-to-government ties are a useful data point when weighing the durability of institutional stability and incentive programs for companies already operating there.

The range of industries represented — manufacturing, trading, energy, retail, aviation — also suggests the Philippine government isn't targeting a narrow set of sectors, but casting a wide net for Japanese investment. If your company's industry overlaps with these priority areas, this is a reasonable moment to step up information-gathering through channels like JETRO, the Philippine Embassy, or DTI.
