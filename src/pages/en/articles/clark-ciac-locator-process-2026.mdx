# Six steps to become a locator in Clark Aviation Capital

## Introduction

Setting up a factory, MRO facility, logistics base or office around Clark International Airport involves more than leasing a site. Companies must distinguish the land and lease process of Clark International Airport Corporation (CIAC) from registration and permits administered by Clark Development Corporation (CDC) and other agencies. This article first walks through the general, nationwide process for starting a company in the Philippines—which applies regardless of location—before covering the CIAC/CDC steps that are specific to Clark Aviation Capital.

## CIAC and CDC have different roles

CIAC is the government corporation responsible for developing and managing the Clark Civil Aviation Complex. CDC administers the Clark Freeport and Special Economic Zone, including locator registration, business regulation and application forms. A company should therefore plan for both interfaces rather than treating “Clark approval” as one document.

| Body | Main role | What investors assess |
|---|---|---|
| CIAC | Aviation-complex land, proposal evaluation and lease | Site, use, lease terms and development plan |
| CDC | Economic-zone registration and regulation | Registration, construction and operating permits |
| Other agencies | Environmental, fire and sanitary matters | Project-specific clearances and certificates |

## The general process for setting up a company in the Philippines

Before getting into the CIAC and CDC steps, it helps to understand the standard, nationwide process that any foreign-owned company follows to start operating in the Philippines. This framework applies the same way in Manila, Cebu or Clark—it is not specific to any one location.

| Stage | What it involves | Main agency |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Register the business entity | Choose a structure—domestic corporation, branch or representative office—and register it with the SEC (Securities and Exchange Commission). Sole proprietorships instead register a business name with DTI. | SEC (via eSPARC) / DTI |
| 2. Fund paid-up capital and check foreign-ownership limits | If foreign equity exceeds 40% and the company is not an export enterprise (at least 70% of output exported), the Foreign Investments Act generally requires a minimum paid-up capital of US$200,000 (reduced to US$100,000 for enterprises using advanced technology or directly employing at least 50 workers). Also check the Foreign Investment Negative List for any sector-specific equity caps. | Bank (ITF account) / SEC |
| 3. Register with the BIR | Within 30 days of receiving the SEC (or DTI) certificate, register for a Tax Identification Number (TIN), obtain the Certificate of Registration, and secure authority to issue receipts and register books of account. Missing the deadline can trigger penalties of roughly ₱5,000–₱20,000 depending on the type of business. | BIR (via ORUS or NewBizReg) |
| 4. Secure the LGU business permit | After obtaining barangay clearance, apply for the Mayor's Permit at the city or municipality where the business is located. The Philippine Business Hub offers a one-stop channel that combines SEC, BIR and LGU steps. | LGU / Philippine Business Hub |
| 5. Register as an employer for statutory benefits | If hiring staff, register as an employer with SSS (social security), PhilHealth (health insurance) and Pag-IBIG/HDMF (housing fund). | SSS / PhilHealth / HDMF |
| 6. (Optional) Register with BOI, PEZA or another investment promotion agency | To qualify for tax incentives, register separately with BOI or PEZA depending on the business activity, export ratio and location. This is not a requirement to legally operate—it determines eligibility for incentives. | BOI / PEZA and others |

Since the CREATE MORE Act, the incentive menus offered by BOI and PEZA are largely aligned, so the choice mainly comes down to export ratio and location (inside an economic zone or not).</p></div>

**How this relates to the CIAC/CDC process:** Stages 1–6 above are the standard, nationwide steps required for any business activity in the Philippines. A company setting up inside Clark Aviation Capital needs these steps in addition to—not instead of—the CIAC land and lease process and CDC registration and permits (environmental, fire, building, sanitary, etc.) described below. In other words, the CIAC/CDC process does not replace general company registration; it is an additional layer specific to the aviation complex.

## The six-stage process

### 1. Submit a letter of intent

The letter should identify the company, proposed activity, site requirement and investment. A useful submission quantifies floor area, infrastructure needs and logistics flows instead of merely stating an interest in Clark.

### 2. Provide business and corporate documents

CIAC evaluates the proponent's legal standing, business proposal, financial capacity and technical capability. A Japanese parent should prepare a consistent English explanation of ownership, funding, project experience, decision makers and the timetable for establishing the Philippine entity.

### 3. Negotiate the term sheet

The parties discuss the site, rent, term and development obligations. Compare total occupancy cost, including site preparation, utility capacity, construction-period charges, road access and reinstatement—not headline rent alone.

### 4. Execute the lease

After terms are agreed, the parties proceed to the lease agreement. Before signing, reconcile the contract with internal approvals and address what happens if a permit required for the intended operation cannot be obtained.

### 5. Complete CDC registration and permits

CIAC's guide states that it endorses the locator to CDC after the lease. The following stage may include an environmental compliance certificate, fire, occupancy and sanitary permits, and authority to operate. Requirements vary by activity and building, so obtain a project-specific matrix.

### 6. Build and commence operations

The company then moves through construction, equipment installation, recruitment, commissioning and operations. CIAC's page shows sequence, not a guaranteed standard duration. Design changes, utilities and permits should be managed as separate critical-path items.

## Investor checklist

1. Check ownership structure and foreign-investment restrictions.
2. Quantify land, power, water, telecommunications and access needs.
3. Prepare a five-year investment, employment and operating plan.
4. Put the CIAC lease, CDC registration and construction permits on separate tracks.
5. Confirm incentive eligibility in writing based on the proposed activity.
6. Budget for permit delays and maintain a fallback schedule.

For aviation, logistics and manufacturing projects, airport proximity is only one variable. Bonded operations, hazardous materials, night access, power quality and availability of skilled labor can determine operating cost. A site visit should follow the actual inbound and outbound route and inspect utility connection points.
